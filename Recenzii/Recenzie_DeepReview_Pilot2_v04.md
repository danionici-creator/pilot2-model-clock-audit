# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This draft technical protocol (v0.4) specifies a preregistered empirical evaluation contract to test whether an artificial agent's accumulated internal change ($c_e(t) = \sum_{\tau < t} D(S(\tau), S(\tau+1))$) provides incremental predictive validity per unit of storage for forecasting its own catastrophic forgetting and scheduling memory replay, beyond a saturated baseline of cheap online optimizer telemetry (`B_strong`). The study structures its inquiry across four core questions: offline incremental predictive validity over clocks, counts, and logged gradient/momentum/canary summaries (Q1/H1); storage efficiency relative to full $O(|\theta|)$ parameter-snapshot net displacement (Q2/H2); exploratory sensitivity to experience ordering (Q3/H3); and closed-loop causal utility in budget-matched rehearsal scheduling verified against online block-permuted placebos (Q4/H4a–b), followed by a planned transfer protocol on pretrained language models (Stage C). Following initial Stage 0 technical runs on a single laptop CPU, v0.4 withdraws non-generalizing algorithmic rule tasks to focus on in-sample associative fact retention, lowers the base learning rate to $\eta_0 = 3 \times 10^{-4}$, caps primary evaluation intervals at $\Delta N < 1,280$ steps to remove late-horizon untrained-input logit drift, redefines the childhood reference battery $R$ as a battery of forgotten inputs, and reformulates computational feasibility criteria in wall-clock hours.

## Strengths
* **Rigorous Identification Audit of Model-Centric Clocks:** Rather than comparing a cumulative parameter-update clock solely against raw step counts, the protocol isolates whether accumulated state change carries non-redundant cardinal rate information ($\bar{\rho} = \Delta c_e / \max(\Delta N, 1)$) beyond elementary online optimizer statistics such as learning-rate-weighted gradient and momentum norms, Synaptic Intelligence path-integral work, and decoupled weight-decay path lengths.
* **Carefully Engineered Decoupling and Falsification Stream Families:** The multi-family experimental design systematically separates committed step counts from parameter updates via variable re-exposure and batch sizes (F2) and cosine learning-rate warm restarts (F3), while introducing an adversarial high-change, low-interference distractor stream on a disjoint vocabulary (F6) to test whether global change indices are misled by benign parameter drift.
* **Scale-Invariant and Preconditioner-Aware Path Formulations:** The candidate distance specifications explicitly account for Pre-LN Transformer scale invariance and AdamW decoupled weight decay via layer-norm-normalized pre-decay updates ($D_{1,\text{rel}}$), and identify the algebraic cancellation of Adam's preconditioner in same-step Fisher/second-moment weighting to motivate a lagged-curvature metric ($D_2$) paired with explicit same-step momentum baselines (`C5d`).
* **Principled Equivalence Testing and Bilateral Validity Gates:** The inferential pipeline combines headroom-scaled Two One-Sided Tests (TOST), joint training-and-test BCa bootstrap resampling, a doubled-budget baseline-saturation gate (`G3b`), and a calibrated synthetic residual positive control (`G4b`) so that both superiority and statistical equivalence represent falsifiable, audit-certified outcomes.
* **Causal Closed-Loop Reliance Intervention and Transparent Revision Tracking:** Stage B moves beyond offline decodability by testing whether a budget-capped rehearsal scheduler causally relies on the temporal alignment of $c_e$ relative to a seed-matched twin fed block-permuted increments online (`G8`), and the protocol maintains exceptional transparency by documenting every Stage 0 failure and design change in detailed revision logs.

## Weaknesses
* **Stage B Deployment Model (`S-pred`) Filter Conflict, Trigger Non-Monotonicity, and Placebo Mismatch:** Please consider resolving the conflict between placing the new $\Delta N < 1,280$ cap inside the T1 inclusion filter (§6) and fitting the Stage B deployment model "without the inclusion filter" (§8), which either trains `S-pred` on post-saturation logit drift ($\Delta N \ge 1,280$) or forces flat tree extrapolation when active tasks exceed $1,280$ steps in Stage B, while also addressing negative jumps in `S-pred`'s total-forgetting trigger upon external re-exposure and the mismatch between task-specific interval conditioning and per-step 256-step block permutation in the `G8` residual placebo (§9).
* **Asymmetric Step-Count Truncation Bias and Residual Within-Cap Logit Drift ($\Delta N < 1,280$):** Because F2, F3, and F6 decouple step counts from forgetting speed and untrained fact losses already cross uniform chance ($\ln 64 \approx 4.16\text{ nats}$) within $400\text{--}800$ steps (§5.1), truncating strictly on the competitor clock $\Delta N < 1,280$ prematurely censors slow-forgetting low-rate intervals (and long distractor stretches in F6) while retaining post-chance logit inflation in $\Delta N \in [512, 1,280)$; the authors might want to add bracketing step caps ($\Delta N < 640$ and $\Delta N < 2,048$) and require directional concordance on bounded accuracy (`T1-acc`) or online chance-clipped loss ($F_k^{\text{clip}}$) before claiming functional support in §15.
* **Exact Collinearity of `C0` ($\Delta c_a$) and `C1` ($\Delta N$) on F1–F3 Training Runs and Unscaled Ridge Basis:** Because F1–F3 contain no idle intervals, elapsed ticks `C0` ($\Delta c_a$) and step counts `C1` ($\Delta N$) are identical across 100% of training and validation triplets, causing GBDT tie-breaking and Ridge $L_2$ regularization to assign active step-count weights to `C0` and corrupt out-of-sample predictions on idle-interval streams (F4 and F5); please also consider scaling positive accumulators by their mean per-step increment before applying `log1p` in Ridge regression (§8) so small-magnitude accumulators ($Z \ll 1$) are not forced to be linear relative to $\Delta c_e$.
* **Uncontrolled Interval Duration ($\Delta N$) in Gate `G5` and Structural Contradictions in the Frozen Interpretation Table (§15):** In Gate `G5` (`X_cov + c_e` vs. `X_cov + C6`), adding $\{\Delta c_e, \bar{\rho}\}$ supplies exact interval duration $\Delta N = \Delta c_e / \bar{\rho}$ whereas `X_cov` and `C6` ($W_{\text{eff}} = 16$) are duration-blind, allowing $c_e$ to pass `G5` purely as a step counter unless both models include `C1`; furthermore, please consider revising §15 to enforce mutual exclusivity, apply the §8 hyperparameter-divergence and `G2`/`G4` gates to `H1 equivalent`, and add fallback rows if F6 or F5 is dropped under the 200-run cap.
* **Forgotten Reference Battery ($R$) Canary Degradation and Deterministic Decay of Unused Embeddings:** Once $R$ is forgotten after $400\text{--}800$ steps, `C10` and `C10b` read tail log-loss on a single dead childhood label (suffering cross-item sign cancellation in `C10b` when value-token biases permute), and keeping `maximum context 8` alongside 32 unused digit tokens and 92 childhood-only $V_{\text{ref}}$ tokens under AdamW decoupled weight decay injects deterministic $\sum \eta_\tau$ shrinkage into parameter norms; please consider masking unused/reference token embeddings from weight decay, adding label-free output entropy on $R$ to `C10`/`C10b` (plus $L_R(t_0)$ to `X_cov`), and instituting a Q2–Q4 relative-rate concordance gate for `D3`.
* **Fact-Only Scope Restriction Relative to Generalization Benchmarks:** With the withdrawal of rule tasks leaving only in-sample associative fact memorization in a $0.93\text{M}$-parameter full-parameter Transformer, the authors might want to qualify the binding claims in §0.3 and §15 so that Stage A support or equivalence is explicitly scoped to short-horizon in-sample associative retention rather than out-of-sample generalization under bilinear LoRA updates as studied in FOREVER (Reference Paper: FOREVER: Forgetting Curve-Inspired Memory Replay for Language Model Continual Learning, Yujie Feng, Hao Wang, Jian Li, Xu Chu, Zhaolu Kang, Yiran Liu, Yasha Wang, Philip S. Yu, and Xiao-Ming Wu, 2026).
* **Stage 0 Calibration Unblinding Risk and Positive-Control (`G4b`) Breakdown:** Because §12a computes out-of-fold $\Delta R^2_{\text{cal}}(c_e^D)$ on the 24 calibration runs before the author decides whether to continue, stop, or "simplify the design again," please consider splitting Stage 0 into a $c_e$-blinded pipeline audit (Step 0a) and an automated, gate-filtered $D^*$ lock (Step 0b) with pre-hashed disjoint seeds, while repairing the `G4b` noise variance formula $\sigma^2_{\xi,r}$ so it cannot become negative when $1 - R^2_r(B_{\text{strong}}) \le 2\delta$ or attenuate across runs with heterogeneous slopes.
* **Omitted Training-Replicate Variance Floor ($\sigma^2_{\text{train}}$), H2 Margin Mismatch, and Unmeasured Analysis/Stage B Compute:** Pairing each test-set bootstrap draw with a single training-run replicate induces an irreducible variance floor $\sigma^2_{\text{train}}$ omitted from the §12 sample-size equations (and an uncentered 19.1-vs-30 unique-run shift in BCa $\hat{z}_0$), flooring $\delta_{\text{H2}}$ at $0.01$ in §12 contradicts the unfloored $0.80$ contrast in §10.2, and the §12a/§14 cost projections do not yet measure the multi-hour CPU cost of thousands of GBDT/604-feature Ridge bootstrap refits or the full Stage B run count ($719\text{--}989$ runs when including $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ calibration, `S-FOREVER` validation, `G6`, and `G7`).

1. **[FATAL] Logical Contradictions, Non-Exclusivity, Missing Gate Preconditions, Conflation of Stream Non-Identifiability with Telemetry Reconstructibility, and Unmapped Outcomes in the Frozen Interpretation Table (§15, pp. 21–22; §0.3, p. 3; §2, p. 5)**

   - **Problem:** Section 15 is frozen with the protocol as the binding evaluation contract and states in its preamble (p. 21): `"Gates passed" means G2, G3b, G4 and G5 passed. Rows on H1 are mutually exclusive.` Comparing the rows of §15 against the decision and gate rules in §0.3, §2, §8, §10, §11, and §12 reveals five structural contradictions that represent a critical potential concern:
     1. *Asymmetric hyperparameter-divergence rule allowing false Level 2 closure (Row 5 vs. Row 6):* In §8 (p. 13), the divergence rule states: *"if the locked and the re-tuned $\Delta R^2$ lead to different outcomes and differ by more than 0.01, H1 is reported as inconclusive (sensitive to hyperparameters), never as closing Level 2."* In §16 (p. 23, Row 15), this rule is cited as the primary mitigation for *"Hyperparameter locking hides an interaction"*. Yet in §15 (p. 22), Row 5 applies the divergence rule **only** when the locked statistic is positive (`H1 lower bound > δ, but locked and re-tuned outcomes diverge (§8)`), whereas Row 6 (`H1 equivalent, and G3b and G4b passed`) declares `"Counts and logged summaries suffice; Level 2 is closed for this regime"` without checking whether the re-tuned $\Delta R^2$ diverged. If adding $\{\Delta c_e^{D^*}, \bar{\rho}^{D^*}\}$ requires a different tree depth, leaf size, or L2 penalty than `B_strong` to exploit an interaction with $t_0$ covariates—so that the locked $\Delta R^2$ lies inside $(-\delta, \delta)$ (`equivalent`) while the re-tuned $\Delta R^2$ is `supported` or `inconclusive` and differs by $> 0.01$—Row 6 closes Level 2 in direct violation of §8 and §16.
     2. *Missing Stage A `G2` and `G4` guards on Row 6 (`H1 equivalent`) vs. Rows 10–11:* Unlike Rows 1–3, Row 6 requires only `G3b and G4b passed`, omitting `G2 passed` and `G4 passed`. Because `G2` (identifiability of the interval rate $\bar{\rho}$ from `B_strong`) is evaluated on Stage A test triplets (§11, p. 17: *"Consequence of failure: H1 is reported as not identifiable, never as equivalence"*), if $\bar{\rho}^{D^*}$ is collinear with `B_strong` on test runs ($R^2 \ge 0.90$, so `G2` fails), adding $\{\Delta c_e^{D^*}, \bar{\rho}^{D^*}\}$ to `B_strong` mechanically yields $\Delta R^2 \approx 0 \in (-\delta, \delta)$ (`H1 equivalent`). Under §15 as written, this satisfies **both** Row 6 (*"Level 2 is closed"*) and Row 10 (`G2 fails`: *"Not identifiable in this design"*).
     3. *Conflating stream non-identifiability (`G2` failure) with telemetry reconstructibility (§0.3, p. 3; §2, Consequence 1, p. 5; §15, Rows 9–10, p. 22):* Section 0.3 (p. 3) states that if $c_e$ is equivalent to `B_strong`, *"the benefit attributed to a model-centric clock is reproducible from elementary optimizer statistics"*, and §15 Row 9 (p. 22) reports `"c_e beats B_standard but H1 is equivalent"` as *"The information in c_e is reconstructible from cheap telemetry"*. However, `B_strong` is specifically engineered to reconstruct $c_e$ from specialized online optimizer telemetry (e.g., `C5d` $\sum \eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$ is the same-step Adam identity for $D2$, `C5f` is the weight-decay path, and `C10b` is the reference-battery path). If the stream generators decouple $\bar{\rho}$ from standard clocks and step/token/event counts ($R^2(\bar{\rho} \mid X_{\text{cov}} \cup B_{\text{standard}}) < 0.90$), **and** the specialized accumulators in `B_strong` reconstruct $\bar{\rho}$ with $R^2(\bar{\rho} \mid B_{\text{strong}}) \ge 0.90$, that is **not** a failure of the stream design—it is direct empirical evidence that the model-centric clock rate $\bar{\rho}$ is reconstructible from specialized online telemetry (`B_strong`). Conversely, if $R^2(\bar{\rho} \mid B_{\text{strong}}) = 0.91 \ge 0.90$ at Stage A while the remaining $9\%$ unexplained variance still delivers a statistically significant gain $\Delta R^2 > \delta$ (`H1 supported`), bundling `G2 passed` into Rows 1–3 would reject a genuine positive effect as unidentifiable.
     4. *Direct violations of mutual exclusivity across H1 rows:*
        - If `H1 lower bound > δ`, `"Gates passed"`, and locked vs. re-tuned outcomes diverge (§8), both Row 1 (or 2/3) and Row 5 apply simultaneously.
        - Row 6 (`H1 equivalent, and G3b and G4b passed`) and Row 9 (`c_e beats B_standard but H1 is equivalent`) overlap whenever $c_e$ beats `B_standard` and matches `B_strong`; moreover, because `B_standard` is designated as descriptive-only (§0.7, p. 4), §15 leaves undefined what statistical threshold constitutes `"c_e beats B_standard"` (e.g., whether the one-sided 97.5% BCa lower bound of $\Delta R^2$ over $X_{\text{cov}} \cup B_{\text{standard}}$ must exceed $\delta$ or $0$).
        - Because `"Gates passed"` bundles `G5` and omits `G6` and `G7`, an outcome where `H1 lower bound > δ` and `G2, G3b, G4` pass but `G5` fails matches none of Rows 1–5, while any outcome where `G6 fails` (Row 13) overlaps with Rows 1–8.
     5. *Unmapped outcomes when `F6` or `F5` is dropped under the §12 budget cap, asymmetric `F6` criteria, and missing Stage B / Stage C branches:*
        - Under the sample-size cap $N_{\text{test}} \le 200$ (§12, p. 18), if the cap is exceeded at Stage 0, `F6` is dropped first and `F5` second. Yet Rows 1–3 of §15 all condition on both `F5` and `F6`, rendering Row 1 unreachable if `F6` or `F5` is dropped.
        - In Row 1, `F6 upper bound ≥ 0` passes even when the point estimate $\Delta\bar{R}^2_{F6}$ is substantially negative with a wide confidence interval (e.g., $-0.03$, 95% CI $[-0.07, +0.005]$).         - In Stage B (Rows 18–21, p. 22), the study is explicitly powered for TOST equivalence at $\delta_B = 2$ percentage points (§10.1, §12), yet §15 has **no rows** for `H4a equivalent`, `H4b equivalent`, or `H4a/H4b inconclusive` (which determine whether admissibility criterion (d) in §0.1 and §1 is met); Row 19 (`H4a supported, task-selection check failed`) omits `G8 passed`; and Row 21 (`H4a or H4b supported, but G8 failed`) pools `G8` across both schedulers even though `G8` is evaluated separately for `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` and `S-c_e` (§11, p. 18).

   - **Concrete fix:** In **§15 (pp. 21–22)** and **§2 (Consequence 1, p. 5)**:

2. **[MAJOR] Confounded Task-Selection Check Between the Identification Note (§2) and the Interpretation Table (§15) (§2, Consequence 2, p. 5; §15, Rows 18–19, p. 22; §16, Row 18, p. 23)**
   - **Section:** §2 (Consequence 2, p. 5), §15 (Rows 18–19, p. 22), and §16 (Row 18, p. 23).
   - **Problem:** Section 2 (Consequence 2, p. 5) notes that ranking tasks on $\Delta c_e$ alone induces the same weak ordering as $\Delta N_k$ (least-recently-rehearsed, LRR), and argues that inside a learned predictor `S-pred`, *"the conditional ranking can differ from least-recently-rehearsed, because the spacing $c_e(t_{0,k}) - c_e(t_{0,j})$ is traded off against covariates at $t_0$; whether it does is checked on validation runs before H4a is interpreted (§9)."* In **§15 (Rows 18–19, p. 22)** and **§16 (Row 18, p. 23)**, `H4a` (`S-pred(B_strong + c_e) − S-pred(B_strong)`) is interpreted as evidence about task selection if this check passes—where §9 (p. 14) defines the check as both `S-pred(B_strong)` and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` selecting a task *other than LRR* on $\ge 10\%$ of rehearsals.
     However, in `H4a`, the baseline `S-pred(B_strong)` **already** combines all $t_0$ covariates `X_cov` (competence $\bar{L}_{B,k}(t_0), \text{Acc}_{B,k}(t_0)$ without the $\ge 0.90$ inclusion filter, prior mastery, input overlap, task tenure, prior exposures) with ~15 cardinal interval accumulators (`B_strong`). Consequently, `S-pred(B_strong)` will already deviate from naive LRR on $\ge 10\%$ of rehearsals due to `X_cov` and `B_strong` alone. Even if adding $\{\Delta c_e^{D^*}, \bar{\rho}^{D^*}\}$ changes **zero** task selections relative to `S-pred(B_strong)` at matched decision states—affecting `H4a` solely by altering *when* the summed predicted forgetting crosses $\theta_{\text{eff}}$—both models will pass the non-LRR check.
   - **Why it matters:** Passing a check against covariate-free LRR does not show that $c_e$ altered task selection relative to `B_strong`, so §15 Row 18 will misattribute a pure rehearsal-timing effect in H4a to $c_e$-driven task selection.
   - **Concrete fix:** In **§2 (Consequence 2, p. 5)**, **§15 (Rows 18–19, p. 22)**, and **§16 (Row 18, p. 23)** (and §9, p. 14), require the task-selection check on validation runs to verify that when `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` and `S-pred(B_strong)` score the active task pool on the **same decision-grid states**, they select **different top-ranked eligible tasks ($\Delta N \ge 64$) from each other on at least 10% of rehearsals** (using the matched-time task-agreement metric already logged in §9, p. 15).

3. **[MAJOR] Scope Restriction to In-Sample Associative Fact Memorization vs. Unqualified Audit and Closure Claims on FOREVER and Level 2 (§0.3, pp. 2–3; §1, pp. 4–5; §15, pp. 21–22; §16, Row 8, p. 23; §21, Row 1, p. 30 — Tasks 1 & 4)**
   - **Section:** §0.3 (pp. 2–3), §1 (pp. 4–5), §15 (pp. 21–22), §16 (Row 8, p. 23), and §21 (Row 1, p. 30).
   - **Problem:** Following the Stage 0 withdrawal of algorithmic rule tasks (held-out accuracy $0.02\text{–}0.30$ with overfitting; §4.1, §21), Stage A and Stage B test *only* short-horizon ($\Delta N < 1,280$), task-incremental, in-sample associative retention of 256 memorized 5-token key–value pairs (`[TASK_k] [K1] [K2] → [VALUE]`) in a $0.93\text{M}$-parameter full-parameter Transformer. Evaluating Task 4 and Task 1 across these sections shows:
     1. *Does the identification audit of §0.3 remain informative?* **Yes, as a controlled mechanistic test of trajectory identification in associative memory overwrite:** because memorized factual associations in Transformers are stored in weight matrices and overwritten by subsequent parameter updates, Stage A remains a valid test of (i) whether the interval rate $\bar{\rho} = \Delta c_e / \max(\Delta N, 1)$ under full-parameter AdamW training is reconstructible from cheap online telemetry (`G2`), and (ii) whether cumulative change $c_e$ predicts associative memory overwrite (`H1`) beyond `B_strong` under variable novelty (F2), cosine warm restarts (F3), and high-change/low-interference drift (F6).
     2. *Where §0.3 and §15 overstate transfer to FOREVER and Level 2 (and omission of §15 in §21 Row 1):* While §21 (Row 1, p. 30) added scope disclaimers to §1 (p. 5), §4.1 (p. 6), and §16 (Row 8, p. 23) (*"declared scope: in-sample retention, no generalisation"*), it **omitted §15 (*Interpretation table (frozen with the protocol)*, pp. 21–22)**, and **§0.3 (p. 3)** still asserts unconditionally regarding FOREVER (Feng et al., 2026): *"If the answer is equivalence, the benefit attributed to a model-centric clock is reproducible from elementary optimizer statistics; if it is superiority, the clock carries information of its own. Both outcomes bear directly on that line of work."* Likewise, **§15 (pp. 21–22)** was not updated in v0.4 to qualify its H1 support (Row 1: *"first empirical support for the experiential index (Level 2)"*) or Level 2 closure (Row 6: *"Counts and logged summaries suffice; Level 2 is closed for this regime"*) interpretations by task regime, and **§16 (Row 8, p. 23)** cites *"Stage C on text domains"* as the mitigation even though Stage C is explicitly excluded from this preregistration (§13, p. 20). Stage A differs from FOREVER in three structural ways that can change the H1 outcome:
        - **Full-parameter vs. bilinear LoRA updates:** In Stage A's full-parameter AdamW regime, the update $\Delta\theta_\tau^{\text{pre}} = -\eta_\tau \hat{m}_\tau / (\sqrt{\hat{v}_\tau} + \varepsilon)$ lies in the exact same coordinate space as logged optimizer moments (`C5d`, `C5e`). By contrast, FOREVER (and Stage C, §13 item 3) adapts **LoRA** weights $\Delta W_{\text{eff}} = (\alpha/r)(B_{\tau+1}A_{\tau+1} - B_\tau A_\tau) = (\alpha/r)(\Delta B \cdot A_\tau + B_\tau \cdot \Delta A + \Delta B \cdot \Delta A)$, a bilinear product whose Frobenius norm couples each step's update in one low-rank factor with the accumulated parameter state of the other factor while AdamW moments exist only in the separate adapter factors $(A, B)$.
        - **Candidate distance selection ($D^*$ vs. $D1$):** H1 tests only $D^*$ selected on calibration runs from $\{D1, D1_{\text{rel}}, D2, D3\}$ (§5.1); if $D^* = D3$ (output $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ on $R$), H1 does not test FOREVER's parameter-norm clock $c_e^{D1}$ on test runs unless $D1$ and $D1_{\text{rel}}$ are also reported alongside $D^*$.
        - **In-sample lookup overwriting with task tokens vs. held-out generalisation:** Overwriting 256 memorized key–value pairs marked by dedicated `[TASK_k]` tokens differs mechanically from losing out-of-sample domain generalisation without task tokens, where forgetting is often driven by representation-subspace rotation or output-layer misalignment ("spurious forgetting"; Zheng et al., 2025).
     3. *What a supported, equivalent, and inconclusive H1 can now be claimed to show:*
        - **Supported H1:** $\bar{\rho}^{D^*}$ carries non-reconstructible predictive information beyond `B_strong` for *short-horizon ($\Delta N < 1,280$) in-sample associative retention in full-parameter task-incremental learning* (transfer to held-out generalisation and LoRA requires Stage C).
        - **Equivalent H1:** `B_strong` suffices within $\pm\delta$ for *short-horizon in-sample associative retention in full-parameter task-incremental learning*, closing Level 2 only for that regime (it cannot refute model-centric clocks on LoRA/LLM generalisation benchmarks unless Stage C also yields equivalence, or unless Gate `G2` shows $\bar{\rho}$ itself is reconstructible with $R^2 \ge 0.90$).
        - **Inconclusive H1:** Level 2 is unresolved even for in-sample associative retention at the prespecified sample size ($N_{\text{test}} \le 200$) and SESOI $\delta$.
   - **Why it matters:** Without qualifying §0.3 and §15, an equivalence finding in a $0.93\text{M}$-parameter rote-memorization world would be over-interpreted as refuting model-centric clocks in pretrained LoRA continual generalisation, or a positive finding would be over-interpreted as demonstrating generalisation-forgetting prediction before Stage C is run.
   - **Concrete fix:**

  Furthermore, the test-run seed lists are committed and hashed only *after* Stage 0 concludes (§12a, p. 20; §17, p. 24), and Row 5 of the §12a table selects $D^*$ unconditionally by maximum out-of-fold $\Delta \bar{R}^2_{\text{cal}}$ over `B_strong` while G5 and G2b are merely *"recorded for $D^*$"* and G2 is checked only after $D^*$ is selected.

##### 2. [FATAL] Mathematical and Pipeline Breakdown of Positive-Control Gate G4b ($Z = e + \xi$) Under High Within-Run $R^2_r(\text{B\_strong})$, Pooled GBDT Fitting, and $n = 16$ Sample Size (§6, p. 10; §8, p. 13; §11, p. 17; §12a, p. 19)

* **Problem:** Gate G4b (§11, p. 17) adds a synthetic predictor $Z = e + \xi$ to `B_strong` on the calibration runs (out of fold), where $e$ is the out-of-fold residual of T1 given `B_strong` and $\xi \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2_{\xi,r})$ has per-run variance:
  $$\sigma^2_{\xi,r} = s^2_{e,r}\left[\frac{1 - R^2_r(\text{B\_strong})}{2\delta} - 1\right],$$
  with $s^2_{e,r}$ the episode-weighted residual variance and `monotonic_cst = +1` on $Z$. G4b requires the full locked-hyperparameter GBDT pipeline to yield an estimated $\Delta R^2 \in [1.25\delta, 2.5\delta]$ and outcome *supported* (lower BCa bound $> \delta$). Because passing G4b is a mandatory Stage 0 continuation criterion (§12a, p. 19) and a precondition for interpreting an equivalent H1 outcome as closing Level 2 (§11, p. 17), a false failure of G4b either halts the study at Stage 0 or renders equivalence uninterpretable. While an idealized per-run univariate linear regression of a zero-mean residual $e$ on $Z$ yields $(1 - R^2_r)\frac{s^2_{e,r}}{s^2_{e,r} + \sigma^2_{\xi,r}} = 2\delta$, it is a potential concern that four mathematical breakdowns arise when embedding this formula in the pooled Stage 0 GBDT pipeline:
  1. **Negative noise variance ($\sigma^2_{\xi,r} \le 0$) when $1 - R^2_r(\text{B\_strong}) \le 2\delta$:** Gate G4b states *"On calibration runs only, out of fold"* without restricting to F2–F3. Under the v0.4 cap ($\Delta N < 1,280$, §6, p. 10), removing the 32–64% post-saturation logit-drift variance leaves smooth associative forgetting curves where $R^2_r(\text{B\_strong})$ on F1 runs (or on low-noise F2/F3 runs) can exceed $1 - 2\delta$ (for example, if $\delta = 0.05$ and $R^2_r(\text{B\_strong}) = 0.92$, then $\frac{1 - 0.92}{2(0.05)} - 1 = -0.20 < 0$). Even on F2–F3, Gate G3 only checks the *mean* headroom $\bar{r}_{AB} - \bar{R}^2(\text{B\_strong}) \ge 2.5\delta$ across runs; individual runs can still have $1 - R^2_r \le 2\delta$, yielding $\sigma^2_{\xi,r} \le 0$ and causing Gaussian noise generation to fail at runtime. *(Relatedly, in §10.1, $\delta = \max(0.02, \min(0.05, 0.15 \times \text{headroom}))$, so G3's condition $\text{headroom} \ge 2.5\delta$ holds automatically whenever $\text{headroom} \ge 0.02 / 0.15 = 0.1333$, but at $\text{headroom} = 0.05$ with $\delta = 0.02$ it allows G4b's $2\delta = 0.04$ signal to consume $80\%$ of total headroom.)*
  2. **Cross-run slope heterogeneity and evaluation-run leakage in a pooled fit (explicit calculation):** Within run $r$, $\text{Cov}_r(e, Z) = s^2_{e,r}$ and $\text{Var}_r(Z) = s^2_{e,r}\frac{1 - R^2_r(\text{B\_strong})}{2\delta}$, so the optimal linear regression slope of $e$ on $Z = e + \xi$ in run $r$ is:
     $$\beta_r = \frac{\text{Cov}_r(e, Z)}{\text{Var}_r(Z)} = \frac{s^2_{e,r}}{s^2_{e,r} + \sigma^2_{\xi,r}} = \frac{2\delta}{1 - R^2_r(\text{B\_strong})}.$$
     Because $R^2_r(\text{B\_strong})$ varies across runs, $\beta_r$ differs from run to run. However, a single pooled model fitted across the 18 fitting runs of a fold without run identity can only learn a single global compromise slope $\bar{\beta} \ne \beta_r$, attenuating expected within-run incremental skill to $\mathbb{E}[\Delta R^2_r(\bar{\beta})] = 2\delta\left[1 - \left(\frac{\bar{\beta} - \beta_r}{\beta_r}\right)^2\right] < 2\delta$.
     *Explicit numerical calculation:* Suppose half the runs have $1 - R^2_1 = 0.10$ ($\beta_1 = 0.40$ at $2\delta = 0.04$) and half have $1 - R^2_2 = 0.30$ ($\beta_2 = 0.1333$) with unit $\text{SST}_r = 1$. Then $\text{Var}_1(Z) = 0.25$ and $\text{Var}_2(Z) = 2.25$. Even an exact pooled linear oracle learns $\bar{\beta} = \frac{0.5(0.10 + 0.30)}{0.5(0.25 + 2.25)} = \frac{0.20}{1.25} = 0.16$, yielding on Run 1 an achieved $\Delta R^2_1 = 2(0.16)(0.10) - (0.16)^2(0.25) = 0.032 - 0.0064 = \mathbf{0.0256}$—a **36% attenuation** below $2\delta = 0.04$. Moreover, using a held-out evaluation run's realized $R^2_r(\text{B\_strong})$ and $s^2_{e,r}$ to scale $Z$ leaks evaluation-run target statistics into the feature scaling.
  3. **Non-zero out-of-fold mean residuals ($\bar{e}_r \ne 0$), cross-fold residual leakage, and locked-GBDT split competition:** By §8 (p. 13), $1 - R^2_r(\text{B\_strong}) = \text{SSE}_r / \text{SST}_r = (s^2_{e,r} + \bar{e}_r^2) / s^2_{y,r}$, where $\bar{e}_r \ne 0$ is the out-of-fold mean error on run $r$, reducing achievable $\Delta R^2_r$ by the factor $\frac{s^2_{e,r}}{s^2_{e,r} + \bar{e}_r^2} < 1$. Furthermore, in 4-fold cross-fitting (§12a), if $e$ on the 18 fitting runs of outer fold $k$ is taken from the outer 4-fold predictions of `B_strong`, then $e$ on those fitting runs was computed using models trained on the 6 evaluation runs of fold $k$ (cross-fold leakage). Conversely, when $Z$ is constructed without leakage, it is an orthogonal low-SNR residual ($\text{SNR} = \frac{2\delta}{1 - R^2_r - 2\delta} \in [\frac{1}{9}, \frac{1}{4}]$) competing against $\sim 35$ features of `B_strong` inside a `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` refitted from scratch under **locked** `max_iter`, `max_leaf_nodes`, `min_samples_leaf`, and `l2_regularization`.
  4. **Sample-size starvation of the `outcome supported` check on $n = 16$ F2–F3 calibration runs:** By explicit calculation, for a true effect of $2\delta$ (a shift of $\delta$ above the MET boundary $\delta$) to achieve $0.80$ power at one-sided $\alpha = 0.025$ on $n = 16$ F2–F3 calibration runs, the per-run SD $\sigma$ of $\Delta R^2$ must satisfy:
     $$16 \ge (z_{0.975} + z_{0.80})^2 \left(\frac{\sigma}{\delta}\right)^2 = 7.85 \left(\frac{\sigma}{\delta}\right)^2 \implies \frac{\sigma}{\delta} \le \sqrt{\frac{16}{7.85}} = 1.428.$$
     Whenever $\sigma / \delta > 1.43$ (for example, $\delta = 0.02$ and $\sigma = 0.04$, where §12 properly scales Stage A to $n_{\text{MET}} = 32$ and $n_{\text{TOST}} = 43$ test runs), requiring `outcome supported` on only $n = 16$ calibration runs has $\approx 51\%$ power ($z_{0.975} \cdot 0.04 / \sqrt{16} = 0.0196 \approx \delta$), causing frequent false Stage 0 failures even when the pipeline is working properly.

* **Concrete fix:** In **§11 (p. 17, rows G3 & G4b)** and **§12a (p. 19)**:
  1. Require `headroom >= max(0.10, 3.0 \delta)` in G3 (unless $\bar{r}_{AB} \ge 0.85$ and `B_strong` is already within $0.05$ of the ceiling while G2 passes, in which case Stage 0 proceeds with $\delta = 0.02$), restrict G4b explicitly to the **16 F2–F3 calibration runs** (the H1 confirmatory domain), and compute $e$ on the fitting runs of each outer fold via inner 3-fold cross-fitting strictly within that fitting fold so no evaluation-run information leaks into training.
  2. Scale $Z$ using the run-centered residual $\tilde{e}_{r,i} = e_{r,i} - \bar{e}_r$ so that its population regression slope is **identically $\beta_r \equiv 1$ across every run $r$** and noise variance is strictly positive:
     $$Z_{r,i} = \alpha_r^2 \tilde{e}_{r,i} + \xi_{r,i}, \quad \alpha_r^2 = \min\!\left(0.95,\; \frac{2\delta}{s^2_{e,r} / s^2_{y,r}}\right), \quad \xi_{r,i} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\Bigl(0,\; \alpha_r^2(1 - \alpha_r^2)s^2_{e,r}\Bigr)$$
     (where on held-out evaluation runs, $\alpha_r^2$ uses the fitting-fold average so no evaluation-run target statistics leak). Here $\text{Cov}_r(\tilde{e}_r, Z_r) = \text{Var}_r(Z_r) = \alpha_r^2 s^2_{e,r}$, so $\beta_r = 1$ for all $r$ and subtracting $1 \cdot Z_{r,i}$ reduces $\text{SSE}_r$ by $\alpha_r^2 s^2_{e,r} = 2\delta \cdot \text{SST}_r$ without cross-run slope attenuation or negative variances.
  3. Widen the G4b point-estimate band to $[1.05\delta, 2.50\delta]$ to accommodate finite-sample GBDT leaf shrinkage under locked hyperparameters, and evaluate the `outcome supported` (lower 97.5% BCa bound $> \delta$) requirement at the powered target sample size $n_{\text{H1}}$ in the §12 calibration bootstrap power simulation rather than on $n = 16$ runs alone.

##### 3. [FATAL] Stage B Closed-Loop Defects: Non-Monotone and $K_{\text{active}}(N)$-Scaled `S-pred` Trigger, Incompatible Aggregation Levels in Gate G8, and Missing Stage B Operational Specifications (§9, pp. 14–15; §11, p. 18; §12, p. 19; §12a, p. 20; §17, p. 24; §20, Row 6, p. 28)

* **Problem:** Three interacting specifications across §9, §11, §12, §12a, §17, and §20 threaten to make the Stage B closed-loop `S-pred` schedulers (`S-pred(B_strong)` and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)`), H4a/H4b, and Gate G8 either confounded or mathematically undefined:
  1. **Negative jumps upon external re-exposures and $40\times$ scale drift with $K_{\text{active}}(N)$ in `S-pred`'s trigger statistic (§9, p. 14):** Section 9 defines `S-pred` to trigger a rehearsal when *"the increase in total predicted forgetting over active tasks since the last rehearsal reaches $\theta_{\text{eff}}$"*. Let $\Phi(N) = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{A}(N)} \hat{F}_k(N)$ denote total predicted forgetting over active tasks $\mathcal{A}(N)$:
     - First, in streams with external re-exposure segments (e.g., F2), whenever the environment re-exposes an already-active task $j \in \mathcal{A}(N)$ between agent-triggered rehearsals, task $j$'s anchor $t_{0,j}$ resets to the end of that segment and $\hat{F}_j(N)$ drops discontinuously from $3\text{--}5\text{ nats}$ back to $\approx 0$. This abrupt **negative jump** in $\Phi(N) - \Phi(N_{\text{last}})$ cancels the forgetting accumulated by the other $K_{\text{active}} - 1$ tasks, stalling rehearsals and dumping the remaining budget into the terminal flush (violating Gate G7's $\le 5\%$ flush limit).
     - Second, as $K_{\text{active}}(N) = |\mathcal{A}(N)|$ grows from $1$ task early in life to $40$ tasks late in life, the unnormalized sum $\Phi(N)$ accumulates up to $40\times$ faster per step at the end of life than at the start, whereas the pacing formula $\theta_{\text{eff}}(N) = \theta_{\text{cal}} \cdot \frac{(N_{\text{life}} - N)/N_{\text{life}}}{\max(\varepsilon, (K_{\text{reh}} - k_{\text{spent}}(N))/K_{\text{reh}})}$ uses a single time-invariant constant $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ calibrated against uniform spending $N/N_{\text{life}}$.
     - Third, §9 does not explicitly state that all trigger accumulators pause during rehearsal segments and reset at the **end** of the 64-step rehearsal segment ($N_{\text{end}} = N_{\text{trig}} + L_{\text{reh}}$) rather than at the trigger step $N_{\text{trig}}$.
  2. **Incompatible aggregation levels in the Stage B causal placebo (Gate G8) for `S-pred` (§9, pp. 14–15; §11, p. 18; §20, Row 6, p. 28):** Section 9 states: *"Its realised per-step $c_e$ increments, cut into 256-step blocks with idle zeros kept in place, are permuted with a fixed seed and fed online, step by step, to a placebo twin with the same seed. For `S-pred`, the permuted quantity is the out-of-fold residual of $\Delta c_e$ given `B_strong`, so that the feature pairs stay within the range seen in training."* However, in `S-pred`, $\Delta c_e(t_{0,k}, t_1)$ and `B_strong`$(k, t_{0,k}, t_1)$ are **task-specific multi-step interval quantities** evaluated simultaneously across up to $K \le 40$ active tasks with different last-exposure anchors $t_{0,k}$ (and include task covariates $X_{\text{cov},k}$ at $t_{0,k}$ and non-additive clamped window SDs C6), whereas 256-step blocks are a 1D per-step stream. Furthermore, once the placebo twin triggers a rehearsal at a different step than the live run, its task exposure anchors $t_{0,k}^{\text{placebo}}$ diverge from the live run's, Stage B test runs have no cross-fitting folds (making *"out-of-fold"* undefined on Stage B runs), and adding a negative residual from a high-update block to a low-update block can produce $\Delta c_e^{\text{placebo}} < 0$, violating the non-negativity of $c_e$ and $\bar{\rho}$.
  3. **Missing Stage B operational constants ($Q / K_{\text{reh}}$, stream families, `S-oracle` trigger timing, T3 evaluation grid, and Stage 0 source for $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up},B}$ and $n_B$; §9, p. 14; §12, p. 19; §12a, pp. 19–20; §17, p. 24):**
     - Section 9 defines $K_{\text{reh}} = Q / L_{\text{reh}}$ with $L_{\text{reh}} = 64$ and Gate G7 (§11, p. 18) asserts *"realised rehearsal steps $= Q$ on every Stage B run"*, but the numerical value of $Q$ (or $K_{\text{reh}}$) is never stated anywhere in the protocol. Neither §9 nor §10.2 states which stream families (e.g., F2 and F3 split equally) generate the Stage B validation and test runs, or *when* `S-oracle` triggers its $K_{\text{reh}}$ rehearsals.
     - Section 12 (p. 19) states that $n_B = \max(40, \lceil 13.5(\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}/\delta_B)^2 \rceil)$ uses *"the upper bound of the SD of paired Stage B differences, ... frozen after the power simulation"*, §12a (p. 19, *Cost* row) requires verifying at Stage 0 that *"projected wall-clock time $\le 72\text{ h}$ for Stage A ... and $\le 120\text{ h}$ for Stage B"*, and §17 (p. 24) requires freezing and hashing all sample sizes in the Zenodo preregistration *after Stage 0 and before any Stage A or B training, validation, or test run*. However, the 24 Stage 0 calibration runs are open-loop Stage A runs; no closed-loop Stage B runs exist at Stage 0 (`S-pred`, $\theta_{\text{cal}}$, and `S-C*` do not exist until Stage A training and Stage B validation runs are completed), so $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}(\Delta T3)$, $n_B$, and the projected Stage B wall-clock time cannot be computed at Stage 0 as written.
     - If T3 averages probe-B accuracy over segment-end evaluations as well as the 256-step grid, schedulers that trigger more rehearsals late in life (when $K_{\text{active}} \approx 40$ and accuracy is lower) generate more rehearsal segment-end evaluations late in life, biasing the unweighted average.

* **Concrete fix:**
  1. In **§9 (p. 14, *Pacing* and *Schedulers*)**, specify that all threshold schedulers reset their trigger accumulator to $0$ at the **end** of each rehearsal segment $N_{\text{end}} = N_{\text{trig}} + L_{\text{reh}}$ (and at $N = 0$, with no decisions during rehearsal segments and an explicit guard `if k_spent(N) < K_reh:`), and define `S-pred`'s trigger statistic on the 64-step decision grid $\tau \in \{N_{\text{end}} + 64, \dots, N\}$ as the **cumulative mean positive 64-step predicted forgetting increment** across active tasks not trained or rehearsed during $(\tau - 64, \tau]$:
     $$\Delta \hat{\Phi}_{\text{cum}}(N_{\text{end}}, N) = \sum_{\substack{\tau \in (N_{\text{end}},\, N] \\ \tau \equiv 0 \pmod{64}}} \frac{1}{\max(1, |\mathcal{U}(\tau)|)} \sum_{k \in \mathcal{U}(\tau)} \max\!\Bigl(0,\; \hat{F}_k(t_{0,k}, \tau) - \hat{F}_k(t_{0,k}, \tau - 64)\Bigr),$$
     where $\mathcal{U}(\tau) = \{k \in \mathcal{A}(\tau - 64) : \Delta N_k(\tau) \ge 64 \text{ and } k \text{ not trained in } (\tau - 64, \tau]\}$.
  2.
