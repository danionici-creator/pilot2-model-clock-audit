# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY

## Summary
This submission comprises two linked manuscripts examining agent-held temporal evidence and internal change indices in persistent artificial agents and continual learning models. The first document (*"Does a model-centred clock carry information beyond optimizer telemetry? Closure report of Pilot 2"*, pages 1–12) empirically tests whether candidate "experiential indices" of accumulated internal change—including parameter-space update distances, functional output divergence on probe batteries ($D_3$), and the model-centric update-norm clock $\tau = \sum \Vert\Delta\theta\Vert_2$ introduced in the FOREVER replay framework—carry information beyond online optimizer telemetry or same-cost forward-pass competitors. Across a synthetic continual-learning world, ported language-model setups (Qwen3-0.6B with LoRA on synthetic facts and SuperNI), and a reproduction using the FOREVER authors' codebase, every candidate fails the pre-fixed out-of-sample reconstruction gate ($R^2 \ge 0.90$, Gate G2), halting the primary forgetting-prediction hypothesis ($H_1$) as unidentifiable, while replay-scheduling comparisons demonstrate practical equivalence within prespecified margins when replacing $\tau$ with the free Adam moment-norm clock $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$. The second document (*"A Time of Their Own: Agent-Held Temporal Evidence for Persistent AI Agents"*, Version 1.4, pages 13–32) is a conceptual Perspective that classifies temporal evidence across explicit threat models (A, B, and C), separates a hardware-rooted continuity anchor $(c_a, N)$ from a dual clock of accumulated adaptation ($c_e$), proposes a six-level falsification programme against conventional competitors, introduces a governance principle separating epistemic access to rollback evidence from control over restoration, and integrates the negative and unidentifiability results of Pilot 2 into Level 2.

## Strengths
* **Rigorous Pre-Confirmatory Identifiability Gate and Baseline Hygiene:** Formalizing an out-of-sample reconstruction gate ($R^2 < 0.90$ against free online optimizer telemetry and same-cost forward-pass statistics) prior to testing downstream predictive validity prevents statistical confounding and exposes when proposed "model-centric" trajectory clocks merely read back the deterministic bookkeeping of adaptive optimizers.
* **Practical Audit of Model-Centric Replay Clocks in LLM Continual Learning:** Demonstrating that FOREVER's parameter-update clock $\tau = \sum \Vert\Delta\theta\Vert_2$ is closely reconstructed by and functionally interchangeable with the zero-cost Adam moment-norm statistic $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ provides a valuable methodological corrective for continual learning, showing that replay trigger scheduling does not require duplicating trainable weights in memory.
* **Clear Architectural Taxonomy and Threat-Model Separation:** The Perspective establishes a well-structured conceptual synthesis that separates platform-level continuity guard checks, monitor-adjudicated continuity claims, and raw agent-bound temporal evidence routed into a learning agent's predictive self-model across benign (A), privileged-software (B), and physical (C) threat models.
* **Transparent Disclosure of Protocol Evolution and Reproducibility Artifacts:** The authors provide an exceptionally candid timeline documenting how the experimental plan adapted after Gate G2 failed, alongside comprehensive archive indexing, local SHA-256 hash verification scripts, and explicit scope limitations.

## Weaknesses
* **Incomplete Decoupling of Parameter Update Norms in the FOREVER Clock-Swap Variant:** In the clock-swap ablation of the FOREVER codebase, only the replay trigger counter was replaced with $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ while the replay regularization strength $\beta$ remained computed from the exact parameter update norm $\Vert\Delta\theta\Vert$, meaning the modified runs still require storing a copy of trainable parameters and could allow $\beta$ to buffer timing discrepancies from delayed triggers (Reference Paper: FOREVER: Forgetting Curve-Inspired Memory Replay for Language Model Continual Learning, Feng et al., 2026).
* **Overstated High-Level Framing of Same-Cost Probe Competitors as Free Optimizer Telemetry:** While Document 1 accurately tests the functional divergence clock $D_3$ against same-cost competitors (`C10c`, per-item loss changes computed from the same reference-battery forward passes), the high-level summaries in Document 2 (Abstract, Changes table, §6 caution, and Conclusion) compress this into the unqualified claim that every candidate was reconstructed from "free optimizer telemetry" or "statistics the optimizer already keeps," overlooking that an optimizer does not run extra probe forward passes for free.
* **Terminological Tension Between "Not Identifiable" and "Closed" Across the Two Documents:** Document 2 (§5) amends its closure rule to stipulate that candidates failing the reconstruction gate exit as "not identifiable... never as equivalence" without explicitly stating that this exit operationally eliminates the candidate, creating a rhetorical tension with Document 1's title and text declaring the line "closed in this framework as a documented negative result" and with Document 2's Conclusion stating that optimizer statistics "proved sufficient."
* **Confounded Implementation Deviations and Unisolated Evaluation Noise in the FOREVER Comparisons:** The ported setups bundle four simultaneous changes relative to the original FOREVER code (`bf16` without `GradScaler`, continuous single-stream training across phases instead of per-phase `Trainer`/AdamW resets, batch accumulation changes, and $3/10$ epoch truncation on SuperNI), while the single-run-per-cell $2 \times 2$ design in the authors' code does not isolate unseeded stochastic evaluation decoding (`temperature=0.02`) from `fp16` training trajectory variance.
* **Compression of Coordinate-Wise Preconditioning Mechanics in AdamW:** The theoretical background writes Adam's step size as a global scalar ratio $\Vert\Delta\theta\Vert \approx \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert / \sqrt{\bar{v}}$, which elides the distinction between the steady-state constant update-RMS regime ($\Vert\Delta\theta\Vert \propto \eta$) and the transient LoRA regime where $\Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ fluctuates faster than coordinate-wise $\sqrt{\hat{v}}$ adapts, as well as prior findings on how non-isotropic preconditioning distorts cumulative parameter paths (Reference Paper: Rotational Equilibrium: How Weight Decay Balances Learning Across Neural Networks, Kosson et al., 2024; Muon is Scalable for LLM Training, Liu et al., 2025; Cumulative Learning Rate Adaptation: Revisiting Path-Based Schedules for SGD and Adam, Atamna et al., 2025).

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Document 1 (Pilot 2 Report): Question, Background on FOREVER & Adam, Protocol Timeline, and Experimental Set-ups (§1–§4)] (Pages: 1-5)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[MAJOR] Question of mathematical formulation and prior work — Coordinate-wise preconditioning, constant update RMS vs. first-moment norms, and decoupled weight decay in AdamW (§2, page 2):**
   - **Location:** Page 2, Section 2 ("Known mechanism" and "Other uses of accumulated change").
   - **Potential Concern:** Section 2 writes the Adam step-size relation as the scalar ratio $\Vert\Delta\theta\Vert \approx \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert / \sqrt{\bar{v}}$ (citing Kosson, Messmer & Jaggi 2024; Liu et al. 2025; Wang & Aitchison 2025) and immediately concludes: *"A close relation between $\tau$ and the free quantity $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ is therefore expected"*. This formulation compresses three distinct mathematical mechanisms that affect both the theoretical premise and the interpretation of prior work (A2/A4):
     1. *Coordinate-wise Preconditioning vs. Global Ratio of Norms:* In AdamW, the update is computed element-wise as $\Delta\theta_{t,i} = -\eta_t \big(\hat{m}_{t,i} / (\sqrt{\hat{v}_{t,i}} + \epsilon) + \lambda \theta_{t-1,i}\big)$. Because division by $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ occurs coordinate-wise before taking the Euclidean norm, $\Vert \hat{m}_t \oslash (\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon) \Vert_2$ only reduces to a scalar multiple of the unpreconditioned norm $\Vert\hat{m}_t\Vert_2 / \sqrt{\bar{v}_t}$ if $\hat{v}_t$ is nearly isotropic across coordinates or uncorrelated with $\hat{m}_t^2$, and if $\sqrt{\bar{v}_t}$ varies slowly over time. Omitting $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ entirely in the surrogate clock $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert_2$ leaves the clock unnormalized with respect to shifts in overall gradient variance across a task—which directly explains why the ratio $\sum\Vert\Delta\theta\Vert / \sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ drifts over a task as $\hat{v}_t$ adapts after step 24.
     2. *Constant Update-RMS Regime vs. $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ Proxy:* The cited result from Liu et al. (2025, `arXiv:2502.16982`) and Kosson et al. (2024, `arXiv:2305.17212`)—that AdamW's update RMS is tightly concentrated around $0.2\text{--}0.4$—applies to the *already-normalized* ratio $\hat{m}_{t,i} / \sqrt{\hat{v}_{t,i}}$ under steady-state training where $\hat{v}_t \approx \mathbb{E}[g_t^2]$. In that regime, $\Vert\hat{m}_t\Vert_2$ and $\sqrt{\text{Tr}(\hat{v}_t)}$ scale together with gradient magnitude and cancel out, so $\Vert\Delta\theta_t\Vert_2 \approx c \cdot \eta_t \sqrt{P}$ is governed by the learning rate $\eta_t$ alone rather than $\Vert\hat{m}_t\Vert_2$. Conversely, the report later finds (page 6) that $\sum \eta$ alone achieves only $R^2 = 0.44\text{--}0.69$ on the LM and SuperNI arms while $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ achieves $R^2 = 0.94\text{--}0.96$. This occurs because in short LoRA fine-tuning phases (especially with frequent optimizer resets), $\Vert\hat{m}_t\Vert_2$ fluctuates faster across 8-step windows than $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ adapts.
     3. *Prior Work Nuances and Decoupled Weight Decay (A4):* Section 2 cites Atamna et al. (2025, `arXiv:2508.05408`) as comparing the cumulative parameter path with a random walk for Adam. However, a central point of Atamna et al. (2025) is that naive cumulative path-length measures fail under Adam precisely because non-isotropic preconditioning by $\hat{v}_t$ distorts the update geometry, requiring explicit rescaling by the preconditioner. Similarly, while Benzing (2022, `arXiv:2006.06357`) proves that trajectory-integrated per-parameter importance in Synaptic Intelligence (Zenke et al. 2017, `arXiv:1703.04200`) approximates the square root of the diagonal Fisher information (the second moment tracked by Adam), neither paper shows that global L2 update-norm replay clocks ($\tau = \sum \Vert\Delta\theta\Vert_2$) reduce empirically to the *unpreconditioned* first-moment norm $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert_2$. Finally, decoupled weight decay ($-\eta_t \lambda \theta_{t-1}$) is omitted from the equation in Section 2; while negligible for zero-initialized LoRA factors, it is non-zero in full-parameter training (Synthetic world, weight decay $0.01$, where "weight-decay path" is included in `B_strong` in §4).
   - **Suggested Fix:** In Section 2, define the scalar symbol $\bar{v}$ explicitly, write the exact coordinate-wise AdamW update equation (including decoupled weight decay), distinguish the steady-state constant-RMS regime ($\Vert\Delta\theta\Vert \propto \eta$) from the transient/slowly-varying-$\hat{v}$ regime ($\Vert\Delta\theta\Vert \propto \eta\Vert\hat{m}\Vert$), and note the contrast with Atamna et al. (2025) regarding non-isotropic preconditioning by $\hat{v}_t$.

2. **[MAJOR] Question of experimental validity — Confounding structural deviations between the ported set-ups and the authors' FOREVER code (§4, page 4; related to Abstract & §1, pages 1–2):**
   - **Location:** Page 4, Section 4 ("Language-model arm, port" and "SuperNI (port)" vs. "Authors' code").
   - **Potential Concern:** In Section 1 (Outcome 3, pages 1–2), the report emphasizes a major behavioral difference between the author's ports and the original FOREVER code: in the ports, $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ and $\tau$ trigger replay at practically identical steps, whereas in the authors' code $\sum \eta \Vert\hat{m}\Vert$ triggers replay systematically later than $\tau$ (in 16 of 17 comparable triggers, by up to 36 of 313 steps). However, Section 4 reveals that the LM and SuperNI ports bundle four simultaneous structural deviations from the original FOREVER code:
     1. Switching from `fp16` with `GradScaler` to `bf16` without `GradScaler` (eliminating `GradScaler` step skips where $\Delta\theta = 0$ while moments are still present);

   - **Suggested Fix:** Explicitly state in Section 4 ("Language-model arm, port" and "SuperNI (port)") whether the ports maintain a single continuous AdamW optimizer state and LR schedule across the 24-step calibration boundary and replay phases. ### [Document 1 (Pilot 2 Report): Empirical Results (Gate G2 & Replay Scheduling), Scope/Closure, Limitations, and FOREVER Audit (§5–§10)] (Pages: 5-12)

1. **[MAJOR] Confounding of Replay Regularization Strength ($\beta$) with the Replay Trigger Clock in Scheduling Ablations and Cost Claims (Abstract, §1, & §2, Pages 1–2; §4, Page 5, Top Paragraph; §5.2, Pages 6–8; §5.3, Page 8; §6, Pages 8–9; §8 Note 9, Page 11)**
   - **Location:** Page 2, Section 2 (*"FOREVER ... defines model-centric time $\tau = \sum_t \Vert\theta_t - \theta_{t-1}\Vert_2$ ... and the replay strength $\beta$ is set from the same update norms"*); Page 5, Section 4, top paragraph (*"The clock-swap variant changes only the counter that triggers replay; $\beta$ is still computed from $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$"*); §5.2 ("Scheduling: does the $\tau$ clock schedule replay better than a free clock?", pages 6–8); §5.3 ("What each clock costs", page 8); §6 ("A reading of the scheduling results", pages 8–9); and §8 Note 9 (page 11).
   - **Potential Concern:** In the FOREVER framework, parameter-update norms $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert_2$ serve two distinct functional roles: (1) accumulating into the model-centric clock $\tau = \sum \lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$ to trigger memory replay at thresholds $d \times \text{day}$, and (2) dynamically calibrating the intensity-aware replay regularization strength $\beta$. In the clock-swap ablation of the authors' code (`R2` and the Stage 1 repeated runs `eta_m_A1`), only the trigger counter was replaced with $\sum \eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$, while $\beta$ continued to be computed from the exact parameter-space update norms $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$. Furthermore, §4 and §5.2 do not specify whether the re-implemented port experiments (`T-A` on synthetic facts and `S2` on SuperNI) likewise retained $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$ inside $\beta$ across the alternative clock arms ($\sum \eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$, $\sum \eta$, and step count) or replaced it with each arm's respective clock. This raises two substantive issues:
     1. *Methodological confound and alternative explanation for scheduling equivalence (A2):* Retaining $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$ in $\beta$ means the free-clock runs are not completely decoupled from parameter update norms; they isolate only the *trigger-timing* function of $\tau$, not the complete removal of $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$ from the FOREVER algorithm. Dynamically scaling replay intensity $\beta$ via actual parameter displacement $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$ during replay phases could actively buffer or compensate for shifted trigger steps (such as the 36 vs. 27 replays in `T-A` or the systematic 0–36 step delays and missed fourth replay trigger on `agnews` in `eta_m_A1`), providing an alternative explanation for why aggregate retention metrics remained within the equivalence margins.
     2. *Inconsistency with operational and memory cost claims in §5.3 (A3):* The study frames $\sum \eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$ as a "free clock" that avoids reading the model's parameters or keeping an extra copy of the trainable weights in memory, and the table in §5.3 (page 8) states that the $\sum \eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$, $\sum \eta$, and `Steps` clocks require `"none"` or `"none beyond the optimizer's own state"` in extra memory and answers `"no"` to `"Reads the model's parameters?"`. While that is true of the trigger counter in isolation, executing FOREVER with $\beta$ computed from $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$ still requires storing a reference copy of all trainable parameters from the previous step (4.6 MB in LoRA, or the full weight set under full fine-tuning) and reading the model's weights at each step unless $\beta$ is also replaced by $\eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$ (or computed only at sparse intervals / held fixed).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly clarify in §4, §5.2, §5.3, and §6 how $\beta$ was parameterized in the LM and SuperNI port runs (`T-A` and `S2`), restrict the scheduling claim for the authors' code in §§1, 2, 4, 5.2, and 6 to *replay trigger timing* while holding the $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$-derived $\beta$ active, discuss $\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert$-driven $\beta$ as a potential buffering factor for delayed triggers, and note in §5.3 (and §7) that eliminating the extra parameter copy and parameter reads in FOREVER requires substituting $\eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$ inside the $\beta$ calculation as well (or using a fixed $\beta$).

   - **Location:** §5.1 ("Authors' code", page 6), §5.2 ("Replay triggers", pages 7–8), §6 ("Closed, in this framework", page 8), and §8 (Note 1 on page 10 and Note 7 on page 11).
   - **Potential Concern:** In §5.1 (page 6) and §5.2 (pages 7–8), the report observes that in the authors' original `fp16` code $\sum \eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$ triggers replay systematically later than $\tau$ (in 16 of 17 comparable triggers, by up to 36 of 313 steps, causing `agnews` in arm `eta_m_A1` to miss its fourth replay trigger altogether) because the ratio $\sum\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert / \sum \eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$ drifts downward after the 24-step calibration window ($0.967, 0.861, 0.705$ in `R2`; $0.966, 0.903, 0.810$ in `eta_m_A1`). Also, §5.1 (page 6) does not specify whether the window-level $R^2$ values for the authors' code ($0.922, 0.749, 0.969$) are in-sample OLS fits per task/run or out-of-sample predictions.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Connect §8 Note 1 to §5.1 ("Analysis (d)") and §8 Note 7 by explaining how per-phase `Trainer` resets of $\hat{v}_t$, the extra fourth replay reset on `agnews` in `R2`, and `fp16` `GradScaler` step skips drive the intra-task drift of $\sum\lVert\Delta\theta\rVert / \sum \eta\lVert\hat{m}\rVert$. Clarify in §5.1 whether the secondary reconstruction $R^2$ on the authors' code is an in-sample OLS fit or held-out, whether $\eta_t\lVert\hat{m}_t\rVert$ increments were masked on steps where $\Delta\theta_t = 0$, and amend the summary sentence in §6 (page 8) to acknowledge the single-run within-regime `agnews` exception in `R2`.

3. **[MINOR — Question of Technical Completeness] Crash-Consistency Staging Boundary, State-Digest Verification, Active-Timer Assumption, and Session Scope in the Continuity Anchor Protocol Sketch (§4, pp. 20–21)**
   - **Location:** Document 2, §4 (*The continuity anchor* and *Protocol sketch*, pp. 20–21, lines 1–28).
   - **The Problem:** While the 4-step protocol sketch on p. 21 is explicitly offered *"only to make the assumptions explicit, not as a new protocol,"* and asserts that *"Under threat model B the sketch is sufficient if the host cannot read the sealing key, cannot reorder steps (1) and (2), and cannot interpose between the monitor and the self-model; races between checking and using a reading, and threat model C, lie outside it,"* four technical details under Threat Model B (where a compromised host orchestrator controls storage, checkpoints, and process scheduling, while $N$ and the sealing key reside in trusted hardware) represent potential concerns for clarity:
     1. *Crash-consistency boundary between Step (1) and Step (2):* In Step (1), *"the monitor increments $N$ to $N_{k+1}$, reads $c_a$, and seals the tuple of the state digest $H(S_{k+1})$, $N_{k+1}$, $c_a$, and a lineage identifier"*. In Step (2), *"The state and its seal are committed together and crash-consistently, so that a crash leaves either the old pair or the new one."* Because the monotonic counter $N$ resides inside trusted hardware and is mutated in Step (1) while the state-and-seal pair $(S_{k+1}, \text{seal}_{k+1})$ is written to untrusted host disk in Step (2), a crash or host-induced power loss occurring *after* $N$ is incremented to $N_{k+1}$ in Step (1) but *before* the disk commit completes in Step (2) leaves only the old pair $(S_k, \text{seal}_k)$ on disk while the live hardware counter reads $N_{k+1} > N_k$. When $S_k$ is reloaded in Step (3), comparing sealed $N_k$ against live $N_{k+1}$ misclassifies an interrupted commit as an adversarial *restoration*. State-continuity protocols cited just above (Memoir [47] and Ariadne [61]) prevent this specifically by staging the pending state digest inside trusted non-volatile memory or using a two-phase counter increment so the monitor can distinguish an interrupted commit from a rollback.
     2. *Explicit state-digest recomputation in Step (3):* Step (3) states: *"When a state is loaded, the monitor verifies the seal, reads the live values of $N$ and $c_a$ through the trusted path, and classifies the event"*. Under Threat Model B, verifying that the loaded state $S$ matches the seal requires recomputing $H(S)$ over the loaded checkpoint and verifying that it matches the digest inside the sealed tuple (not merely verifying the MAC/signature on the seal), and keeping the live counter $N$ inside non-restorable monitor storage so $N > N(t_0)$ can be evaluated rather than keeping only a single digest.
     3. *Powered suspension in Step (3):* Step (3) states that *"an equal counter with the time reference advanced beyond calibration error is a suspension."* As noted later under *Engineering limits* (p. 23), remanence-decay clocks measure only *unpowered* intervals; if $c_a$ is an unpowered decay clock and a Threat Model B host suspends the agent process while keeping the hardware powered, $\Delta c_a \approx 0$ and Step (3) will not detect the suspension unless $c_a$ is active during the pause.
     4. *Load-time verification vs. step-level execution session:* Excluding *"races between checking and using a reading"* at the end of the sketch sets aside the Time-of-Check to Time-of-Use (TOCTOU) vector under Threat Model B, where a compromised host pauses or clones the agent *after* Step (3) completes at load time but *before* Step (1) commits the next transition. In [47, 61], sufficiency requires that Step (3) (load/check), Step (4) (self-model inference), and Step (1) (next state proposal) execute within a single isolated TEE session or be bound by an atomic monitor transaction.
   - **Why It Matters:** Clarifying these requirements aligns the 4-step sketch directly with the state-continuity guarantees of Memoir [47] and Ariadne [61] cited on p. 21 and with the engineering limits on p. 23.
   - **Suggested Fix:** In Step (2) (p. 21), specify that crash-consistency must span the hardware counter increment in (1) and the disk commit in (2) (e.g., by staging the sealed new state or its digest before incrementing $N$ [47, 61]). In Step (3), state that the monitor recomputes $H(S)$ on the loaded state to verify the seal against the live counter $N$ held in trusted storage, and qualify suspension detection with *"(for a time reference $c_a$ that advances during the pause; see Engineering limits)."* In Step (4), note that under Threat Model B the check in (3), the self-model's use of the reading in (4), and the next commit in (1) must occur within an uninterrupted trusted execution session.

#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[MINOR — Question of Scope / Mechanism Design] Leaky abstraction between "bounded epistemic access" and "policy access" regarding behavioral restorability and evaluation gaming (Page 27, §7 Risks and a Design Principle, lines 11–28)**
   - **The Problem:** In §7 (p. 27), the design principle separates *bounded epistemic access* (the proposed default, where discontinuity evidence informs *"uncertainty, estimates of staleness, invalidation of cached knowledge, and requests for verification"*) from *policy access* (where evidence influences external actions directly). The text then notes that because a restored agent reading non-restorable evidence receives different inputs, *"Restoring behaviour therefore requires that discontinuity evidence enter only epistemic functions — estimates of staleness, invalidation of cached knowledge, calibrated uncertainty — rather than unconstrained action selection — readability by the self-model and readability by the whole policy are not the same thing — or that the platform virtualise the continuity evidence during governance recovery and evaluation."* A potential concern with this formulation is that in any closed-loop agent (especially those evaluated under Level 5, *"Closed-loop use: Does agent-held evidence improve decisions, such as re-verifying stale knowledge..."*, Table 2, p. 24), epistemic states directly govern downstream actions: invalidating cached knowledge or issuing a request for verification *is* an external or behavioral divergence. Moreover, if an LLM agent's context or self-model has epistemic access to a rollback/branching indicator (even restricted to uncertainty or verification requests), the model can still detect that it is inside a branched evaluation harness, triggering the situational-awareness risks discussed on p. 26.
   - **Why It Matters:** Presenting "restricting discontinuity evidence to epistemic functions" and "platform virtualisation" as alternative ("or") solutions to preserve behavioral restorability and evaluation integrity is misleading. If epistemic functions have any causal utility for decision-making (as required by Level 5), they will alter post-restore behavior relative to $t_0$. Thus, architectural restriction to epistemic variables alone cannot guarantee behavioral restorability or hide evaluation branching; monitor-enforced virtualisation of the continuity evidence is strictly required whenever exact behavioral reproduction or blind evaluation is needed.
   - **Suggested Fix:** Clarify in §7 (p. 27) that whenever epistemic states causally influence downstream verification queries or tool decisions, behavioral divergence after restoration is unavoidable. State explicitly that restricting evidence to epistemic modules only bounds the *scope* of policy reactions (preventing arbitrary refusal), whereas preserving exact behavioral restorability and blinding the agent to evaluation branching strictly requires authorised monitor-enforced virtualisation.
