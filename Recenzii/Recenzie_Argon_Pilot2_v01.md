# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This draft technical protocol ("Pilot 2 — Technical Protocol") specifies a preregistered empirical study testing whether an agent-held $O(1)$-storage scalar experiential index of accumulated state change, $c_e(t) = \sum_{\tau < t} D(S(\tau), S(\tau+1))$, provides incremental predictive validity and replay-scheduling utility for catastrophic forgetting beyond a union of cheap logged optimizer and event summaries ($B_{\text{cheap}}$). The protocol evaluates four primary research questions—predictive validity (Q1/H1), storage efficiency relative to full parameter/functional displacement (Q2/H2), path dependence across matched-multiset order blocks (Q3/H3), and closed-loop rehearsal scheduling (Q4/H4)—beginning on CPU-scale synthetic Transformer streams (Stages A and B) and transferring to pre-trained Pythia language models under full fine-tuning and LoRA (Stage C).

## Strengths
* The protocol addresses a practically important and under-explored question in continual learning: whether a single $O(1)$-storage running scalar of internal parameter or output-distribution path length can meaningfully improve online forgetting prediction and replay scheduling beyond zero-storage platform telemetry, or whether the hypothesis can be decisively closed via statistical equivalence.
* The experimental methodology incorporates strong safeguards against common continual learning evaluation pitfalls, including uniting all cheap baselines into a single competence-audited ensemble ($B_{\text{cheap}}$), enforcing a Three-Way Decision Rule (Minimal-Effect Testing paired with Two One-Sided Tests for equivalence), and requiring a causal yoked-stream reliance check in Stage B.
* The identification note in Section 2 astutely formalizes the mathematical boundary of a global monotone scalar index—proving that ranking tasks solely by $\Delta c_e$ at a fixed evaluation time reduces to least-recently-rehearsed ordering—and appropriately structures Stage B around temporal budget allocation and covariate-interacted predictors.

## Weaknesses
* Including the initial probe-A loss $\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ and probe-A accuracy in $X_{\text{cov}}$ while defining both the $\ge 0.90$ inclusion filter and the target $F_k(t_0, t_1) = \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_1) - \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ on the exact same finite probe half A introduces shared measurement noise and regression-to-the-mean truncation bias; the authors might consider evaluating the $t_0$ covariates and inclusion filter on disjoint probe half B.
* The baseline $B_{\text{cheap}}$ omits elementary $O(1)$-storage within-step multiplicative summaries—specifically the step-weighted gradient norm $\sum_\tau \eta_\tau \|\tilde{g}_\tau\|_2$ and the Synaptic Intelligence trajectory work integral $\sum_\tau |\langle g_\tau, \Delta\theta_\tau \rangle|$—as well as initial state norms ($\|\theta(t_0)\|_2$, $\text{Tr}(v(t_0))$) and absolute step $N(t_0)$, while per-step $D_2$ under AdamW algebraically cancels the second-moment preconditioner $v_\tau$ and competitor C6 ($W=200$) collapses into $\Delta c_e^{D_1}$ on intervals $\le 400$ steps (Reference Paper: Continual Learning Through Synaptic Intelligence, Friedemann Zenke, Ben Poole, and Surya Ganguli, 2017).
* In Hypothesis H2, applying BCa bootstrapping directly to the ratio of incremental $R^2$ values suffers from the Fieller–Creasy problem (unbounded variance and erratic coverage when the displacement denominator approaches zero across resamples); please consider reformulating H2 into a linearized Fieller difference test $Z_r(0.80) = \Delta R^2_r(c_e) - 0.80\,\Delta R^2_r(\text{Disp}^{D^*}) \ge 0$ gated on $\Delta R^2(\text{Disp}^{D^*}) > \delta$ (Reference Paper: Some Problems in Interval Estimation, Edgar C. Fieller, 1954; Reference Paper: Ratios: A Short Guide to Confidence Limits and Proper Use, Volker H. Franz, 2007).
* In Hypothesis H3, a global task-agnostic scalar $\Delta c_e(t_0, t_1)$ cannot capture task-specific retroactive interference in the $XY$ vs. $YX$ key-overlap design because total path length over a fixed multiset is blind to which prior task $k$ overlaps with $X$ versus $Y$ and can correlate negatively with recency-driven overwriting; the authors might consider redesigning the order blocks around global plasticity/curriculum non-commutativity or midpoint trajectory integrals (Reference Paper: Continual Learning in the Teacher-Student Setup: Impact of Task Similarity, Sebastian Lee, Sebastian Goldt, and Andrew Saxe, 2021).
* Unweighted pooling of all evaluation triplets $(k, t_0, t_1)$ within a run heavily over-weights early-learned tasks that accumulate dozens of overlapping 200-step evaluation intervals compared to late-learned tasks; please consider weighting triplets by the inverse number of evaluation points per task exposure so each exposure episode contributes equally to model fitting and within-run $R^2_r$ (Reference Paper: Bootstrapping Clustered Data, Christopher A. Field and Alan H. Welsh, 2007).
* The statistical decision rules in Sections 10–12 do not reconcile the fixed 95% (MET, one-sided $\alpha=0.025$) and 90% (TOST, one-sided $\alpha=0.05$) BCa confidence intervals with the Holm step-down multiplicity adjustment across H1–H4, nor do they account for Holm-adjusted $\alpha_k$, central TOST power splitting ($\beta/2$), or standalone F5 subgroup power in the sample-size formula (Reference Paper: Equivalence Testing for Psychological Research: A Tutorial, Daniël Lakens, Anne M. Scheel, and Peder M. Isager, 2018; Reference Paper: Conditional Equivalence Testing: An Alternative Remedy for Publication Bias, Harlan Campbell and Paul Gustafson, 2018).
* Several Stage A specifications will not transfer unchanged to Stage C (pre-trained Pythia models and LoRA)—most notably computing $D_1/D_2$ directly on concatenated LoRA adapter matrices $(A, B)$ rather than the effective bilinear weight update $\Delta W = \frac{\alpha}{r}\Delta(BA)$ (which suffers from $B(0)=0$ initialization asymmetry and gauge non-identifiability), requiring next-token accuracy $\ge 0.90$ on open-ended text, and storing full-vocabulary D3 probability tensors (Reference Paper: LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less, Dan Biderman et al., 2024; Reference Paper: Spurious Forgetting in Continual Learning of Language Models, Junhao Zheng et al., 2025; Reference Paper: Effect of Scale on Catastrophic Forgetting in Neural Networks, Vinay V. Ramasesh, Aitor Lewkowycz, and Ethan Dyer, 2022).
* While triggering replay via accumulated parameter updates is closely related to recent model-centric replay schedulers, the Stage B open-loop validation thresholds $\theta_e$ do not guarantee equal rehearsal expenditure $Q$ on stochastic test runs without an online proportional budget-pacing controller, and the Stage A/B CPU compute estimates undercount dense probe evaluation forward passes (Reference Paper: FOREVER: Forgetting Curve-Inspired Memory Replay for Language Model Continual Learning, Yujie Feng et al., 2026; Reference Paper: Learn the Time to Learn: Replay Scheduling in Continual Learning, Marcus Klasson, Hedvig Kjellström, and Cheng Zhang, 2023).

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Research Questions, Identification Logic, and Agent Specification (§0–§3)] (Pages: 1-4)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

##### 1. Substantive Issues in §§1–3

* **1. [FATAL — Potential Concern] Confounding of Parameter-Space Distances ($D_1, D_2$) by Algebraic Cancellation of the Fisher Proxy $v$, Decoupled Weight Decay, and Pre-LN Scale Invariance (§3 Agent, §1 Q1, §5 Distances, §7 C8b)**
  * **Section:** §3 (Agent table: Architecture, Optimizer, and Persistent state $S$, p. 4), §1 (Q1, p. 3), §5 (Distances D1, D2, and $\text{Disp}^D$, p. 6), and §7 (Reference predictor C8b, p. 7).
  * **The Problem:** Section 3 specifies a Pre-LN Decoder-only Transformer trained with AdamW ($\beta = (0.9, 0.999)$, $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$, weight decay $\lambda = 0.01$, gradient clipping at norm $1.0$), where $S = (\theta, m, v)$, $D$ is computed on $\theta$, and $v$ is used as a diagonal Fisher proxy. Under AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019, `arXiv:1711.05101`), the parameter update at step $\tau$ is:
    $$\Delta \theta(\tau) = \theta(\tau+1) - \theta(\tau) = -\eta_\tau \left(\frac{\hat{m}_\tau}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_\tau} + \epsilon} + \lambda \theta(\tau)\right).$$
    This creates five concrete mathematical confounds for Q1/H1 and C8b:
    1. *Algebraic cancellation of $v_i$ in per-step $D_2$:* When $v_i(\tau)$ is used as a diagonal Fisher proxy in the per-step distance $D_2(\tau) = \left(\sum_i v_i(\tau) (\Delta \theta_i(\tau))^2\right)^{1/2}$ (§5), multiplying the squared Adam step $(\Delta \theta_i(\tau))^2 \approx \eta_\tau^2 \hat{m}_i(\tau)^2 / \hat{v}_i(\tau)$ by the *current* $v_i(\tau)$ algebraically **cancels out the second moment $v_i(\tau)$** in the denominator (up to $\epsilon$, bias correction $1 - \beta_2^\tau \approx 1$ after the 3,000-step childhood phase, and weight decay):
       $$D_2(\tau) \approx \left(\sum_i v_i(\tau) \cdot \eta_\tau^2 \frac{\hat{m}_i(\tau)^2}{\hat{v}_i(\tau)}\right)^{1/2} = \eta_\tau \sqrt{1 - \beta_2^\tau} \, \|\hat{m}(\tau)\|_2 \approx \eta_\tau \|\hat{m}(\tau)\|_2.$$
       Rather than weighting parameter change by prior task Fisher curvature sensitivity (as EWC does by anchoring at $v_i(t_0)$; Kirkpatrick et al., 2017, `doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114`), per-step $D_2(\tau)$ under Adam simply strips out Adam's adaptive preconditioning and collapses back to the learning-rate-scaled momentum norm $\eta_\tau \|\hat{m}(\tau)\|_2$. Because $B_{\text{cheap}}$ (§7) logs cumulative learning rate ($\sum \eta_t$) and cumulative gradient norm ($\sum \|g_t\|_2$) only as separate sums without their within-step product $\sum \eta_t \|g_t\|_2$, $D_2$ (and $D_1$) can beat $B_{\text{cheap}}$ under variable learning rates (F3, F5) simply by recovering the unlogged step-wise product $\eta_t \|g_t\|_2$.
    2. *Ambiguous moment evaluation time in $\text{Disp}^{\text{D2}}(t_0, t_1)$ (C8b):* In §5 and §7 (C8b), $\text{Disp}^{\text{D2}}(t_0, t_1) = \text{D2}(S(t_0), S(t_1))$ does not specify whether the Fisher weight $v_i$ is evaluated at $t_0$ (when task $k$ was mastered, as required by EWC) or at $t_1$ (where $v(t_1)$ reflects the curvature of recent intervening tasks rather than task $k$), nor whether $v_i$ is raw $v_{\tau,i}$ or bias-corrected $\hat{v}_{\tau,i} = v_{\tau,i}/(1-\beta_2^\tau)$.
    3. *Decoupled weight-decay shrinkage drift:* Even when a segment is already mastered and task gradients vanish ($\hat{m}_\tau \approx 0$, as in F2 re-exposure segments), AdamW applies a deterministic weight-decay update $-\eta_\tau \lambda \theta(\tau)$ of Euclidean norm $\approx \eta_\tau \lambda \|\theta(\tau)\|_2 > 0$. Over a 23,000-step continual run, $\|\theta(\tau)\|_2$ drifts systematically across training phases. Because $B_{\text{cheap}}$ does not include $\|\theta(t_0)\|_2$, $\Delta c_e$ can gain predictive power in Q1 simply by acting as a proxy for background weight-norm drift and global run age $t_0$ rather than data-driven plastic adaptation.
    4. *Pre-LN scale invariance:* In a Pre-LN Transformer, linear projections immediately preceding LayerNorm are scale-invariant in the forward pass: scaling weights $\theta$ by $\alpha > 1$ leaves layer outputs unchanged while scaling the effective functional step size as $\|\Delta \theta\|_2 / \|\theta\|_2$ rather than raw Euclidean distance $\|\Delta \theta\|_2$ (Zhang et al., 2019, `arXiv:1810.02338`).
    5. *Undefined $v$ and imprecise collinearity claim in the SGD robustness arm:* In the SGD with momentum $0.9$ arm (§3), the optimizer does not maintain a second moment $v$, leaving $D_2$ undefined unless a passive EMA is specified. Moreover, the note in §3 that *"(under SGD, $\|\Delta \theta\|$ and $\|g\|$ are near-collinear; under Adam they are not)"* does not hold under variable learning rates $\eta_t$ (F3), gradient clipping at $1.0$, weight decay, and momentum $0.9$ directional cancellation unless $\eta_t$ and clipping are accounted for.
  * **Why It Matters:** If H1 is supported under $D_1$ or $D_2$, it would be impossible to determine whether $c_e$ succeeded because path-length adaptation predicts forgetting, or because $D_1$ tracks global parameter norm $\|\theta(\tau)\|_2$ via weight decay while $D_2$ algebraically reconstructs the missing step-wise product $\sum \eta_t \|g_t\|_2$ / $\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$. Moreover, C8b is invalid as an EWC reference bound if $v(t_1)$ is used instead of $\hat{v}(t_0)$.
  * **Concrete Fix:**
    1. In §3, §5, and §7 (C8b), document the algebraic relation $\sum_i \hat{v}_i(\tau) (\Delta \theta_{\text{adam}, i}(\tau))^2 \approx \eta_\tau^2 \|\hat{m}(\tau)\|_2^2$, explicitly anchor net displacement $\text{Disp}^{\text{D2}}(t_0, t_1)$ at the bias-corrected second moment saved at $t_0$:
       $$\text{Disp}^{\text{D2}}(t_0, t_1) = \left(\sum_i \hat{v}_i(t_0) \big(\theta_i(t_1) - \theta_i(t_0)\big)^2\right)^{1/2}, \qquad \hat{v}_i(\tau) = \frac{v_i(\tau)}{1 - \beta_2^\tau},$$
       weight per-step $D_2$ by a running consolidated curvature envelope $\bar{v}_i(\tau) = \max_{\tau' \le \tau} \hat{v}_i(\tau')$ (or lagged segment-boundary curvature) so it does not algebraically cancel the instantaneous Adam denominator, and specify that the SGD robustness arm tracks a passive EMA $v_\tau = 0.999 v_{\tau-1} + 0.001 g_\tau^2$ alongside explicit SGD learning rate $\eta_{\text{SGD}}$ and weight-decay settings.
    2. To eliminate the weight-decay and scale-invariance confounds, include a layer-wise scale-normalized parameter distance $\text{D1}_{\text{rel}}(\tau) = \left(\sum_\ell \frac{\|\Delta\theta^{(\ell)}(\tau)\|_2^2}{\|\theta^{(\ell)}(\tau)\|_2^2 + \varepsilon}\right)^{1/2}$ (computed prior to the decoupled weight-decay step) on calibration runs alongside raw D1, and include $\|\theta(t_0)\|_2$, $\sqrt{\text{Tr}(v(t_0))}$, $\sum_\tau \eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$, and the step-weighted gradient norm $\sum_{\tau=t_0}^{t_1} \eta_\tau \|g_\tau\|_2$ in the baseline predictor set.

* **2. [MAJOR — Potential Concern] Ambiguity Between Pre-Clipping and Post-Clipping Logged Gradient Norms (§1 Q1, §3 Agent)**
  * **Section:** §1 (Q1: *"logged gradient summaries"*, p. 3) and §3 (Optimizer (primary): *"gradient clipping at norm 1.0"*, p. 4).
  * **The Problem:** Section 3 specifies global gradient clipping at norm $1.0$, but neither §1 nor §3 specifies whether the logged gradient summaries in Q1 (competitor C5, $\sum \|g_t\|_2$) and the optimizer moments $(m_t, v_t)$ are computed on the **unclipped** gradient $g_t^{\text{raw}}$ or the **clipped** gradient $g_t^{\text{clip}} = g_t^{\text{raw}} \cdot \min(1, 1/\|g_t^{\text{raw}}\|_2)$. In standard PyTorch (`clip_grad_norm_`), clipping mutates `p.grad` in-place prior to `optimizer.step()`, so $m_t, v_t$, and $\Delta \theta_t$ see $g_t^{\text{clip}}$. If C5 logs only unclipped $\|g_t^{\text{raw}}\|_2$, $c_e$ gains a mechanical advantage whenever gradients spike above $1.0$; conversely, if C5 logs only clipped $\|g_t^{\text{clip}}\|_2$, C5 saturates at $1.0$ per step during high-loss novel segments, compressing its dynamic range.
  * **Why It Matters:** An asymmetry between the gradient seen by the optimizer and the gradient logged in the baseline can artificially inflate or deflate the incremental validity of $c_e$ in Q1.
  * **Concrete Fix:** Specify in §3 that gradient clipping at norm $1.0$ is applied immediately before `optimizer.step()`, and include **both** the unclipped sum $\sum \|g_t^{\text{raw}}\|_2$ and the clipped, LR-weighted sum $\sum \eta_t \|g_t^{\text{clip}}\|_2$ in Q1's logged gradient summaries.

* **3. [MAJOR — Potential Concern] Storage Cost Accounting ("One Stored Number") vs. Per-Task Interval Snapshots and the Scope Restriction on Criterion (b) (§1)**
  * **Section:** §1 (Research question, pp. 3–4).
  * **The Problem:**
    1. *Running scalar vs. per-task snapshot storage:* Section 1 states that $c_e(t)$ *"is a non-decreasing scalar held by the agent. It costs one stored number."* However, Q1–Q3 evaluate the task-specific interval increment $\Delta c_e(t_{0,k}, t_1) = c_e(t_1) - c_e(t_{0,k})$ for each of the $K \approx 40$ learned tasks since its last exposure $t_{0,k}$. While maintaining the running accumulator $c_e(t)$ in persistent state $S$ costs $1$ scalar, computing $\Delta c_e(t_{0,k}, t_1)$ requires storing $1$ snapshot scalar $c_e(t_{0,k})$ **per tracked task exposure $t_{0,k}$** ($K$ scalars total, matching §5's table header *"Storage per $t_0$: 1 scalar"*).
    2. *Incomplete rationale for dropping the reset criterion (b) and run-age drift:* Section 1 states: *"The reset criterion (b) is not applicable here. $\Delta c_e$ is used as an interval index, not as a summary of the whole life history, so only order sensitivity is tested."* Even when $\Delta c_e(t_0, t_1)$ is computed over an interval $(t_0, t_1]$, its per-step rate $\rho(\tau) = D(S(\tau), S(\tau+1))$ depends directly on the persistent state $S(t_0) = (\theta(t_0), m(t_0), v(t_0))$ accumulated over the entire life history prior to $t_0$ (including progressive plasticity changes and moment accumulation over 23,000 steps; cf. Dohare et al., 2024, *Nature*, `doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7`). The actual methodological reason a full state reset at $t_0$ cannot be applied to T1 is that resetting $S(t_0)$ to random initialization would immediately erase the learned target task $k$ whose forgetting is being measured. Furthermore, if "task age at $t_0$" in the baseline covariates refers only to time since task $k$'s first introduction rather than absolute run step $N(t_0)$, $\Delta c_e$ can gain predictive power by acting as a proxy for global run age $N(t_0)$.
  * **Why It Matters:** Precision in storage accounting is essential because the primary metric of Level 2 (§0.1, Q2) is predictive validity *per unit of storage*, and controlling for global run age $N(t_0)$ prevents state-age drift from masquerading as interval adaptation.
  * **Concrete Fix:** Revise §1 to state that $c_e(t)$ costs **1 running scalar in $S$ plus 1 stored snapshot scalar $c_e(t_{0,k})$ per tracked task exposure $t_{0,k}$** (vs. $\approx 0.8\text{ M}$ numbers per $t_{0,k}$ for full state displacement). Update the scope-restriction note in §1 to explain that full-state reset is inapplicable because resetting $S(t_0)$ would erase target task $k$, ensure both **absolute run step $N(t_0)$** and **time since task $k$'s first exposure** are included as baseline covariates, and add a calibration check testing whether resetting optimizer moments $(m, v)$ at segment boundaries alters $\rho(t)$.

* **4. [MINOR — Potential Concern] Architectural Completeness, Multi-Threaded CPU Float32 Determinism, and Unissued Software Version Strings (§3 Agent)**
  * **Section:** §3 (Agent table: Architecture, Precision/device, and Software, p. 4).
  * **The Problem:**
    1. *Weight tying and linear biases:* For the 4-layer Decoder-only Transformer ($d_{\text{model}} = 128$, 4 heads, $d_{\text{ff}} = 512$, context 64, vocabulary $\approx 512$), the 4 Transformer layers contain $4 \times (4 \times 128^2 + 2 \times 128 \times 512) = 786,432$ weight parameters, while the token embedding matrix ($512 \times 128 = 65,536$) and positional embedding matrix ($64 \times 128 = 8,192$) bring the total to $\approx 0.86\text{ M}$ parameters if input and output token embeddings are **weight-tied**, or $\approx 0.93\text{ M}$ if **untied**. Whether embeddings are weight-tied (and whether linear layers and LayerNorms include bias terms) is not stated, which alters both the exact parameter count and whether token embedding updates are counted once or twice in $D_1$ and $D_2$.
    2. *CPU multi-threading non-determinism:* Section 3 specifies *"float32 on CPU; deterministic algorithms enabled; fixed seeds for data, initialisation, and dropout"*, and Gate G7 requires $100\%$ bit-identical reruns. On an 8-core CPU, `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)` alone does not guarantee bit-identical float32 reductions across runs if OpenMP/MKL/oneDNN thread counts or dynamic thread scheduling vary between invocations.
    3. *Hardcoded non-existent software versions:* Section 3 lists *"Python 3.14, PyTorch 2.13, NumPy 2.5, scikit-learn (version pinned at freeze)"*. While Python 3.14 exists, **PyTorch 2.13 and NumPy 2.5 do not correspond to existing stable releases** (PyTorch 2.x releases follow the 2.5–2.11 series and NumPy follows the 2.0–2.4 series). Hardcoding placeholder version numbers in a protocol meant to be frozen with SHA-256 hashes creates immediate reproducibility friction.
  * **Concrete Fix:** Specify in §3 whether input token embeddings and the output projection head are weight-tied and whether linear layers use biases; pin intra-op and inter-op thread settings (`torch.set_num_threads(1)`, `torch.set_num_interop_threads(1)`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS=1` per single-threaded worker across the 8 cores) in the configuration file; and replace the placeholder version strings with the exact verified package versions generated in `environment.txt` at Stage 0 freeze.

### [World Streams, Distance Metrics, Targets, and Predictor Baselines (§4–§7)] (Pages: 4-7)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

##### Categorized Audit of Problems in §§4–7 (FATAL, MAJOR, MINOR)

1. **[FATAL] §4.3 & §7 — Competitor C6 ($W = 200$ Steps) Algebraically Collapses to $c_e^{\text{D1}}$ on Short Intervals and Leaks Out-of-Interval Steps**
   * **Section:** §4.3 (Run structure: Evaluation grid) and §7 (Cheap competitors: Code C6).
   * **The Problem (Definitive Design Flaw):** Section 7 defines C6 as the *"mean and SD of $\|\Delta\theta\|$ over the last $W = 200$ steps before $t_1$ and the first $W$ steps after $t_0$"*. Simultaneously, §4.3 specifies that probes are evaluated *"at the end of every exposure segment and on a grid every 200 steps"*.
     1. *Temporal leakage when $\Delta N = N(t_1) - N(t_0) < 200$:* Whenever an exposure segment ends at $t_0$ and the next evaluation $t_1$ is the next grid point or the end of a short segment (e.g., a 50-step rehearsal segment or $<200$ steps to the next 200-step grid point), $\Delta N < 200$. If C6 windows are not clamped to $(t_0, t_1]$, taking *"the first $W = 200$ steps after $t_0$"* extends **into the future beyond $t_1$** (direct causal leakage of future updates into the predictor for $t_1$), and taking *"the last $W = 200$ steps before $t_1$"* extends **prior to $t_0$ into task $k$'s own training segment**.
     2. *Algebraic identity with $c_e^{\text{D1}}$ when $\Delta N \le 400$:* Even if C6 windows are clamped to $(t_0, t_1]$, for every evaluation interval with $\Delta N \le 400$ steps (which includes the first two 200-step grid evaluations after *every* exposure segment), the two 200-step boundary windows $(N(t_0), N(t_0)+200]$ and $(N(t_1)-200, N(t_1)]$ cover **100% of the interval $(t_0, t_1]$**. Because $B_{\text{cheap}}$ also contains $\Delta N$ (C1), the product of $\Delta N$ and C6's mean $\|\Delta\theta\|_2$ is **mathematically identical** to the full path integral $\Delta c_e^{\text{D1}}(t_0, t_1) = \sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \|\Delta\theta(\tau)\|_2$. Even at $\Delta N = 600$, C6 covers $67\%$ of the full path integral.
   * **Why It Matters:** C6 is included to test whether *local boundary* update statistics can impersonate accumulated history (Criterion (a), §0.2). Setting $W = 200$ on a 200-step evaluation grid hands the full path integral $c_e^{\text{D1}}$ to $B_{\text{cheap}}$ on short-to-medium horizons, either causing identifiability Gate G2 ($R^2 < 0.90$ between $\Delta c_e^{D^*}$ and $B_{\text{cheap}}$) to fail if $D^* = \text{D1}$ or producing an artificial equivalence result in H1.
   * **Concrete Fix:** Restrict C6's boundary window size in §7 to **$W_{\text{eff}} = \min(20, \lfloor \Delta N(t_0, t_1)/4 \rfloor)$ steps** strictly clamped inside $(N(t_0), N(t_1)]$ (so the initial window $(N(t_0), N(t_0) + W_{\text{eff}}]$ and final window $(N(t_1) - W_{\text{eff}}, N(t_1)]$ never cross $t_0$ or $t_1$ and never cover more than $50\%$ of any interval, with $20$ steps representing $10\%$ of the 200-step grid spacing), enforce a minimum interval length $\Delta N(t_0, t_1) \ge 50$ steps (or $\Delta c_a \ge 50$ ticks in F4) for inclusion in $T_1$, stratify H1 reporting by $\Delta N \le 400$ vs. $> 400$, and note that online computation uses a 20-step ring buffer (or an EMA with half-life 20 steps, costing 2 scalars).

2. **[FATAL] §4.1, §4.2, §6, §7, & §16 — Probe-A Selection Truncation, Mathematical Coupling with $X_{\text{cov}}$, Split-Half Asymmetry, and In-Sample Fact-Probe Contamination**
   * **Section:** §4.1 (Task types), §4.2 (Probes), §6 (Targets, T1), §7 (Covariates $X_{\text{cov}}$ & Ceiling), and §16 (Threats to validity, Row 2).
   * **The Problem (Definitive Statistical Flaw):**
     1. *Mathematical coupling and truncation bias (regression to the mean):* In §6, a triplet $(k, t_0, t_1)$ is included only if Probe-A accuracy at $t_0$ is $\ge 0.90$, and the target is $F_k(t_0, t_1) = \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_1) - \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$. Simultaneously, §7 gives both $\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ and Probe-A accuracy at $t_0$ to every model inside $X_{\text{cov}}$ (and §16 Row 2 lists only *"R disjoint from probes; placebo G4; T2 not confirmatory"* under mitigations for shared noise). Because Fact tasks have only 128 facts total (§4.1), Probe half A has only $64$ items (with binomial SE $\approx 3.75\%$ at $0.90$ accuracy). Conditioning inclusion on $\text{Acc}_{A,k}(t_0) \ge 0.90$ on a 64-item sample selects for downward statistical fluctuations in $\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ (winner's curse / regression to the mean at $t_1$), while putting $-\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ in the target $F_k(t_0, t_1)$ and $+\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ in $X_{\text{cov}}$ creates direct mathematical coupling ($\text{Cov}(\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_1) - \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0), \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)) = \text{Cov}(\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_1), \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)) - \text{Var}(\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)) < 0$, Oldham's / Blomqvist's bias) that interacts non-linearly with interval predictors in tree models.
     2. *Violation of split-half exchangeability for the Ceiling (§7 Ceiling):* Section 7 defines the Ceiling via Spearman–Brown split-half reliability of $T_1$ between Probe half A and Probe half B. However, because pairs are filtered asymmetrically on **Probe-A accuracy $\ge 0.90$ at $t_0$** without filtering on Probe-B accuracy at $t_0$, Probe A's baseline variance at $t_0$ is truncated at $0.90$ while Probe B's is untruncated. Halves A and B are therefore no longer parallel/exchangeable splits, biasing the Spearman–Brown ceiling estimate.
     3. *Memorization vs. generalization asymmetry between Fact and Rule probes:* In §§4.1–4.2, Rule task probes (256 items, 128 per half) are held out from training (testing out-of-sample algorithmic generalization), whereas Fact task probes (128 items, 64 per half) *are* the training set (testing in-sample rote memorization, since arbitrary key$\to$value pairs cannot be predicted without training exposure). Consequently, on Fact tasks, cumulative training loss C4 directly tracks probe items during re-exposure segments, and facts sampled in the final mini-batch right before step $s_0$ have artificially depressed $\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$.
   * **Why It Matters:** Shared measurement noise at $t_0$ distorts baseline $R^2(B_{\text{cheap}})$, non-linear splits on $\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ alter incremental $\Delta R^2$, and asymmetric truncation invalidates the reliability ceiling.
   * **Concrete Fix:**
     1. *Decouple the $t_0$ inclusion filter and $X_{\text{cov}}$ baseline from the target split (§6, §7, §16):* Use **Probe half B at $t_0$** to supply the baseline skill covariates in $X_{\text{cov}}$ ($\bar{L}_{B,k}(t_0)$ and $\text{Acc}_{B,k}(t_0)$), while keeping the target $F_k(t_0, t_1) = \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_1) - \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ purely on **Probe half A** (because Probe half B is only read at $t_0$ in Stage A and Stage B uses separate runs, this cannot leak future Stage B retention outcomes). Filter inclusion symmetrically at $t_0$ on full-probe ($A \cup B$) accuracy $\text{Acc}_{A \cup B, k}(t_0) \ge 0.90$ (or on Probe half B) so halves A and B remain exchangeable for the ceiling.
     2. *Equalize probe size and training exposure:* Increase Fact task size in §4.1 from 128 to **256 key$\to$value pairs** (128 items per probe half A and B, matching Rule tasks), mandate that training and rehearsal segments iterate over balanced full-epoch permutations of all 256 facts so every item in A and B receives identical exposure counts and recency at $s_0$, and explicitly state in §4.2 and §6 that Fact probes measure *in-sample associative retention* while Rule probes measure *held-out algorithmic generalization* (reporting T1 both pooled and stratified by task type).

4. **[MAJOR] §4.1, §4.2, §4.3, & §5 — Implicit Key-Pool Overlap and Non-Stationary Forgetting Drift in Childhood Reference Battery $R$**
   * **Section:** §4.1 (Reference tasks), §4.2 (Probes), §4.3 (Run structure), and §5 (Distances D3–D4).
   * **The Problem (Potential Concern):** Section 4.1 states that Reference tasks are *"a fixed set of fact and rule tasks, trained only during an initial childhood phase [3,000 steps]. They are never targets. They supply the reference battery $R$ (§5),"* while §4.2 states that *"No probe item belongs to $R$ or to the behavioural battery $B_{\text{beh}}$"*. In associative tasks formatted as `[TASK_k] [KEY] -> [VALUE]`, two sequences with different task prefixes (`[TASK_ref] [KEY_1] ->` vs. `[TASK_target] [KEY_1] ->`) are disjoint as full sequences even if they share the exact same `[KEY_1]` token from the shared key pool $P$. If $R$ shares keys with target tasks, then D3 ($\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ on $R$) and D4 (CKA on $R$) directly sense representation overwriting on target keys—a privileged overlap signal that does not exist when $R$ is a generic-text battery in Stage C.    * **Why It Matters:** Key-pool overlap and non-stationary forgetting of $R$ compromise the stationarity of D3/D4 and the clean transfer of §§4–5 from Stage A to Stage C.
   * **Concrete Fix:** Explicitly specify in §§4.1–4.2 that Reference tasks ($R$) and Behavioural battery ($B_{\text{beh}}$) draw their keys and rule moduli from a strictly disjoint pool $P_{\text{ref}}$ ($P_{\text{ref}} \cap P_{\text{target}} = \emptyset$) so $R$ acts as a task-agnostic functional sensor in Stage A just as generic text does in Stage C, and verify on calibration runs that the mean per-step rate $\rho_{\text{D3}}(t)$ on F1 does not drift by more than $\pm 20\%$ between the first and fourth quartiles of the Life phase (or include a tiny fixed maintenance rehearsal of $R$, e.g., 1 batch every 500 steps across all families).

6. **[MAJOR] §6 — Sensitivity of Unbounded Cross-Entropy Forgetting Target T1 to Output Logit-Temperature Drift**
   * **Section:** §6 (Targets, T1).

### [Prediction Models, Stage B Schedulers, Statistical Hypotheses, Gates, and Power (§8–§12)] (Pages: 7-9)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[MAJOR] Monotone Constraints on Multi-Component Predictors, Ridge Standardization, and Random-Search Tuning Variance (§8; Appendix Q4, Q13)**
   - **Location:** Section 8 (Bullets 1–4, p. 7).
   - **Potential Concern (Algorithmic Fairness and Optimization Noise in $\Delta R^2_r$):** Three specifications in §8 can bias or inject nuisance variance into $\Delta R^2_r = R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}} + c_e) - R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}})$:
     1. *Monotone constraints on C7 and rate ratios (§8, Bullet 1):* Section 8 specifies `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` *"with monotone-increasing constraints on all interval predictors (C0–C5, C7, $\Delta c_e$, Disp)."* However, C7 (§7) is a 3-component vector: (i) number of new tasks, (ii) number of re-exposure segments to already-mastered data, and (iii) number of rehearsals in $(t_0, t_1]$. Conditioning on elapsed steps $\Delta N$ (C1), having *more* re-exposure segments to already-mastered data in F2 (or rehearsals) means *fewer* steps spent on novel interfering tasks, which **decreases** forgetting. Enforcing a $+1$ monotone constraint on re-exposure/rehearsal counts prevents the baseline tree from learning that mastered re-exposure is benign, artificially handicapping $B_{\text{cheap}}$ in F2 and F5. Moreover, constraining both $\Delta c_e$ and $\Delta N$ (C1) to be strictly monotone-increasing prevents the tree from directly representing the rate ratio $\bar{\rho} = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$ (where holding $\Delta c_e$ fixed while increasing $\Delta N$ implies a *lower* rate of change), and §8 does not explicitly state that $X_{\text{cov}}$ (6 features) and C6 (4 features) have constraint `0`.
     2. *Hyperparameter random-search variance and feature subsampling dilution (§8, Bullet 3):* Tuning $B_{\text{cheap}}$ (18 features) and $B_{\text{cheap}} + c_e$ (19 features) via independent 50-configuration random searches allows finite validation noise to select different tree depths, learning rates, or L2 penalties, creating a constant model-selection offset across all test runs. Furthermore, if column subsampling (`max_features < 1.0`) is included in the search space, adding a 19th feature alters split-selection probabilities across collinear cumulative predictors.
     3. *Secondary Ridge model transforms and scaling (§8, Bullet 2):* Applying `log1p` to every interval predictor in the Ridge arm double-logs C1 (already defined in §7 as $\log(\Delta N + b)$, distorting the Kalajdzievski, 2024, `arXiv:2401.05605` power law), is undefined (`NaN`) on negative values of signed reference predictor C9 ($\langle \nabla_\theta L_k(S(t_0)), \theta(t_1) - \theta(t_0) \rangle$), and applies a uniform $\ell_2$ penalty $\lambda$ across unstandardized features of vastly different scales.
   - **Concrete Replacement:**
     1. Explicitly specify the `monotonic_cst` vector in §8: set `+1` strictly for `C0`, `C1` (fixing $b = 1$), `C2`, `C3`, `C4`, `C5`, `C7_new_tasks`, $\Delta c_e$, `Disp`, `C8`, and `C8b`; set `0` (unconstrained) for `C7_reexposures`, `C7_rehearsals`, all 4 local-window features of `C6`, and all covariates in $X_{\text{cov}}$ (and include unconstrained average-rate ratios $Z / \max(\Delta N, 1)$ for $Z \in \{\text{C2, C3, C4, C5}, \Delta c_e\}$).
     2. Fix `max_features = 1.0` and `max_bins = 255` in `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`. In addition to the 50-configuration random search, include the winning hyperparameter configuration of $B_{\text{cheap}}$ as a mandatory candidate for $B_{\text{cheap}} + c_e$ and report the **locked-hyperparameter paired $\Delta R^2_r$** alongside the re-tuned $\Delta R^2_r$.
     3. In the secondary Ridge arm, apply `log1p` to raw $\Delta N$ (not on top of $\log(\Delta N + b)$), use the signed transform $\text{sign}(x)\log(1 + |x|)$ when evaluating C9, include interactions with all task covariates in $X_{\text{cov}}$, and fit a `StandardScaler` on the training runs before cross-validating $\lambda$.

2. **[MAJOR] Internal Inconsistencies Across Gates G1, G2, G3, G5, G6, and G8, Plus Missing Explicit Split Sizes (§11, §12, §15)**
   - **Location:** Section 11 (Table, pp. 8–9), Section 12 (p. 9), and Section 15 (p. 10).
   - **Definitive Consistency and Specification Gaps:**
     1. *Gate G1 (Spearman–Brown formula vs. single-half target T1):* Gate G1 defines the ceiling as the *"Spearman–Brown split-half reliability of T1 on calibration runs ($\ge 0.70$)"* and states *"report all skills also as a fraction of the ceiling."* However, in §6, T1 is evaluated solely on **probe half A** ($F_{A,k}(t_0, t_1) = \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_1) - \bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$), while probe half B is held out for reliability and Stage B. #### 1. Explicit Answers to the 13 Reviewer Questions (Appendix, pp. 11–12)

1. **SESOI (Appendix Q1, p. 11):**
   - **Evaluation:** Fixing an absolute threshold of $\delta = 0.05$ on within-run $\Delta R^2$ introduces a potential concern regarding ceiling compression and baseline variation across stream families and stages. Because $B_{\text{cheap}}$ combines $X_{\text{cov}}$ (including baseline loss and accuracy) with eight interval predictors ($C0$–$C7$), if the Gate G1 split-half reliability ceiling $R^2_{\text{ceil}}$ is near $0.70\text{–}0.75$ and $B_{\text{cheap}}$ already achieves $R^2 \approx 0.66\text{–}0.71$, less than $0.05$ of explainable variance remains. Under those conditions, a minimal-effect test (MET) threshold of $\delta = 0.05$ requires a single scalar index to capture $\approx 100\%$ of all remaining explainable signal (or becomes mathematically impossible to pass: $0.66 + 0.05 > 0.70$), while making TOST equivalence within $(-0.05, 0.05)$ artificially easy to satisfy via ceiling compression. Conversely, if $R^2(B_{\text{cheap}}) = 0.10$ and $R^2_{\text{ceil}} = 0.90$, a $0.05$ absolute gain captures only $6.25\%$ of the residual explainable variance.
   - **Concrete Replacement:** Replace the raw absolute threshold with a **ceiling-normalized partial $R^2$ (fraction of remaining explainable variance)** paired with an absolute floor and cap: for each run $r$, define the residual explainable variance as $V_{\text{rem},r} = \max(R^2_{\text{ceil},r} - R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}}),\, 0.10)$ and the normalized incremental skill as
     $$\Delta R^2_{\text{rel},r} = \frac{R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}} + c_e) - R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}})}{V_{\text{rem},r}}.$$
     Set the prespecified SESOI to **$\delta_{\text{rel}} = 0.08\text{–}0.15$ ($8\%\text{–}15\%$ of remaining explainable variance)** subject to a minimum raw absolute floor of **$\delta_{\text{abs}} = 0.02$** and cap of $0.05$:
     $$\delta = \max\!\Big(0.02,\; \min\!\Big(0.05,\; 0.15 \times \big[\text{Ceiling}_{\text{G1}} - \bar{R}^2_{\text{cal}}(B_{\text{cheap}})\big]\Big)\Big),$$
     calibrated on calibration runs before freeze and paired with a headroom check in Gate G3 verifying that $\text{Ceiling}_{\text{G1}} - \bar{R}^2_{\text{cal}}(B_{\text{cheap}}) \ge 2.5\,\delta$ on F2–F4. Report raw $\Delta R^2_r$ alongside as a descriptive co-metric.

2. **Chunked D3 (Appendix Q2, p. 11 & §5, p. 6):**
   - **Evaluation:** Because $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ is a formal metric satisfying the triangle inequality (Endres & Schindelin, 2003, `doi:10.1109/TIT.2003.813506`), the chunked sum over $k = 10$ steps is a strict lower bound on the per-step functional path length: $\sum_m \text{D3}(S(mk), S((m+1)k)) \le \sum_\tau \text{D3}(S(\tau), S(\tau+1))$. However, evaluating D3 (and D4) at $k = 10$ while evaluating D1 and D2 at $k = 1$ introduces three substantive problems:
     1. *Asymmetric filtering of stochastic minibatch jitter:* The tightness of the triangle-inequality lower bound depends on trajectory curvature and directional persistence (autocorrelation) of updates within each $k$-step window. During high-stochasticity updates with low net drift (e.g., small batch size $B = 16$ or re-exposure to already-mastered data in F2, and high-LR post-restart steps in F3), consecutive mini-batch updates oscillate and partially cancel. Per-step D1 and D2 ($k = 1$) accumulate the full zigzag random-walk path length ($\propto k\sigma_{\text{step}}$), whereas a 10-step chunk measures the *net chord* over 10 steps, canceling out zero-mean back-and-forth oscillations ($\propto \sqrt{k}\sigma_{\text{step}}$). If $\text{D3}(k=10)$ outperforms $\text{D1}(k=1)$ and $\text{D2}(k=1)$ on calibration runs, it is impossible to distinguish whether functional divergence ($\sqrt{\text{JS}}$) is genuinely superior to parameter distance or whether $k = 10$ chord smoothing simply filters out non-destructive high-frequency step jitter.
     2. *Violation of the triangle inequality in D4:* Unlike $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$, the secondary distance D4 ($1 - \text{linear CKA}$) is *not* a formal metric and violates the triangle inequality: because $\text{CKA}(X, Y) = \cos(\theta_{XY})$ in the inner-product space of centered Gram matrices (Kornblith et al., 2019, `arXiv:1905.00414`), $1 - \cos(\theta) \approx \frac{1}{2}\theta^2$ scales quadratically for small angles, so its chunked sum is neither an upper nor a lower bound on its per-step sum.
     3. *Insufficient specification of the sensitivity arm, compute cost at $k=1$, and non-multiple segment boundaries:* Evaluating $k \in \{1, 10, 50\}$ solely on D3 without a matched $k$-chunked parameter baseline does not isolate the chunking effect, lacks a prespecified quantitative acceptance rule for freezing $k = 10$, does not specify how $c_e^{\text{D3}}(t)$ is evaluated if an exposure segment or idle interval ends at a step $s_0$ that is not an exact multiple of $k \in \{10, 50\}$, and at $k = 1$ on $|R| = 256$ requires 8 forward batches of size 32 at every single training step (increasing calibration runtime by $\approx +267\%$ rather than $+30\%$).
   - **Concrete Replacement:**
     1. *Orthogonalize metric space and chunk size $k$ on calibration runs:* Compute and log **chunked versions of D1 and D2 at the exact same stride $k = 10$** ($\text{D1}^{(k)}(\tau, \tau+k) = \|\theta(\tau+k) - \theta(\tau)\|_2$ and $\text{D2}^{(k)}(\tau, \tau+k) = (\sum_i \hat{v}_i(\tau)(\theta_i(\tau+k) - \theta_i(\tau))^2)^{1/2}$) alongside per-step D1 and D2 ($k = 1$) on calibration runs (caching $\theta(\tau)$ in RAM for 10 steps costs zero extra forward passes and zero extra storage per $t_0$).
     2. *Expand sensitivity arm with a prespecified acceptance gate:* Evaluate $k \in \{1, 5, 10, 20, 50\}$ across D1–D3 on a 5-run calibration subset (over a 2,000-step window for $k = 1$) and adopt $k = 10$ for D3 only if the within-run Spearman rank correlation between $\Delta c_e^{\text{D3}, k=1}$ and $\Delta c_e^{\text{D3}, k=10}$ across evaluation intervals is $\ge 0.98$ and $|\Delta R^2(k=1) - \Delta R^2(k=10)| \le 0.01$ (otherwise reduce $k$ to $5$, halving $|R|$ from $256$ to $128$ if needed to hold CPU overhead at $+30\%$).
     3. *Enforce boundary alignment, single-target-token extraction, and metricize D4:* Restrict $p_{S(\tau)}(\cdot \mid x)$ in D3 to the **single target/final prediction token position** per reference sequence $x \in R$, constrain all exposure segment lengths and the 200-step evaluation grid in §§4.3–4.4 to be integer multiples of $\max(k) = 50$ optimizer steps (indexing chunks strictly by committed optimizer steps $N$ rather than ticks $c_a$, or explicitly evaluating a fractional remainder chunk at segment boundaries), and replace $1 - \text{linear CKA}$ in D4 with the proper chordal metric $\sqrt{1 - \text{linear CKA}(S(\tau), S(\tau+k))}$ or angular metric $\arccos(\text{clip}(\text{linear CKA}, -1, 1))/\pi$.

3. **Inference Structure (Appendix Q3, p. 11 & §6, §8, pp. 6–7):**
   - **Evaluation:** Taking the run as the unit of inference (computing one summary statistic $R^2_r$ per run $r$ and bootstrapping across independent runs) validly prevents Type I error inflation from within-run nesting without requiring a parametric hierarchical mixed model (Field & Welsh, 2007, `doi:10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00593.x`). However, two structural concerns arise within and across runs:
     1. *Within-run horizon and exposure weighting bias:* Because probes are evaluated every 200 steps and at the end of every exposure segment (§4.3, §6), a task learned early in the life phase (e.g., step $1,000$) with a long gap before re-exposure generates up to $\approx 85\text{–}95$ overlapping triplets $(k, t_0, t_1)$ sharing the exact same baseline evaluation $\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ and overlapping sub-intervals, whereas a late or frequently rehearsed exposure generates $\le 10$ triplets. Unweighted pooling of all triplets $(k, t_0, t_1)$ in $\text{SSE}_r$, $\text{SST}_r$, and model training weights early, unrehearsed exposures $10\times\text{–}20\times$ more heavily than late exposures.
     2. *Fixed-model conditioning bias (Dietterich, 1998, `doi:10.1162/089976698300017197`):* Fitting a single regression model on pooled training runs and bootstrapping only over the $n$ test runs conditions inference on the realized training split, omitting training-sample variance from the bootstrap confidence intervals.
   - **Concrete Replacement:**
     1. Weight each triplet $(k, t_0, t_1)$ in both model fitting (`sample_weight` in `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` and `Ridge`) and within-run $R^2_r$ computation using **inverse task-exposure episode weighting**:
        $$w_{k,t_0,t_1} = \frac{1}{E_r \cdot M_{r,k,t_0}} \quad \text{(or } \frac{1}{K_r \cdot E_{r,k} \cdot J_{r,k,t_0}}\text{)}, \qquad R^2_r = 1 - \frac{\sum_{k, t_0, t_1} w_{k,t_0,t_1} \left(F_k(t_0, t_1) - \hat{F}_k(t_0, t_1)\right)^2}{\sum_{k, t_0, t_1} w_{k,t_0,t_1} \left(F_k(t_0, t_1) - \bar{F}_{w,r}\right)^2},$$
        where $E_r$ is the number of valid task-exposure episodes $(k, t_0)$ in run $r$ and $M_{r,k,t_0}$ is the number of evaluation times $t_1 > t_0$ for that exposure segment. Every learned task exposure then contributes equally to $R^2_r$.
     2. Incorporate training-sample variability by implementing a **two-sample run bootstrap** (resampling training runs with replacement, refitting the predictor, and resampling test runs with replacement) for the secondary Ridge model and $K = 5$ training-run fold averaging (or bootstrap bagging) for the primary GBDT.

4. **Baseline Strength (Appendix Q4, p. 12 & §7, pp. 6–7):**
   - **Evaluation:** **FATAL for Q1/H1 in F2, F3, and F5.** As currently defined in §7, $B_{\text{cheap}} = X_{\text{cov}} \cup \{\text{C0}, \dots, \text{C7}\}$ is artificially handicapped because it omits elementary, $O(1)$-storage, agent-available within-step multiplicative summaries and state-scale norms that any standard training loop has immediately available in memory at zero extra forward/backward compute:
     1. *Missing within-step product of learning rate and (clipped) gradient norm ($\sum \eta_\tau \|\tilde{g}_\tau\|_2$):* $B_{\text{cheap}}$ logs cumulative learning rate $\text{C3} = \sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \eta_\tau$ and cumulative raw gradient norm $\text{C5} = \sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \|g_\tau\|_2$ only as **separate marginal interval sums**. Because
        $$\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \eta_\tau \|g_\tau\|_2 = \frac{1}{\Delta N}(\text{C3})(\text{C5}) + \Delta N \cdot \text{Cov}_{(t_0, t_1]}(\eta_\tau, \|g_\tau\|_2),$$
        neither gradient-boosted trees nor ridge regression applied to interval sums can recover the within-step product $\sum \eta_\tau \|g_\tau\|_2$ when $\eta_\tau$ and $\|g_\tau\|_2$ covary within $(t_0, t_1]$ (as in F3 cosine annealing with warm restarts and F2 variable novelty/batch size $\in \{16, 32, 64\}$). Furthermore, §3 specifies gradient clipping at norm $1.0$, which caps the effective gradient at $\tilde{g}_\tau = \min(1, 1.0/\|g_\tau\|_2) g_\tau$, whereas C5 sums the unclipped $\|g_\tau\|_2$. Because parameter steps scale directly with $\eta_\tau$ at every iteration, $c_e^{\text{D1}}$ and $c_e^{\text{D2}}$ can beat $B_{\text{cheap}}$ on F3 and F5 simply because they multiply $\eta_\tau$ step-by-step before summing rather than summing $\eta_\tau$ and $\|g_\tau\|_2$ separately.
     2. *Missing Synaptic Intelligence (SI) path-integral optimization work and squared gradient accumulators (Zenke et al., 2017, `arXiv:1703.04200`):* The online scalar path integral of first-order optimization work $\sum_\tau |\langle g_\tau, \Delta\theta_\tau \rangle|$ and cumulative squared gradient norm $\sum_\tau \eta_\tau^2 \|\tilde{g}_\tau\|_2^2$ (trace of the empirical Fisher) cost 1 scalar per $t_0$.
     3. *Missing absolute run progress and parameter/optimizer moment norms at $t_0$ and $t_1$:* Under AdamW ($\lambda = 0.01$) and Pre-LN layers, total parameter norm $\|\theta(\tau)\|_2$, second-moment trace $\text{Tr}(v(\tau))$, and network plasticity drift systematically with absolute step $N(t_0)$ (Zhang et al., 2019, `arXiv:1810.02338`; Dohare et al., 2024, `doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7`). Notice that $X_{\text{cov}}$ (§7) includes `"task age at t_0"` (which is 0 for every newly introduced task regardless of whether it enters at step 3,500 or step 19,000), but omits global step $N(t_0)$.
   - **Concrete Replacement (Add to $B_{\text{cheap}}$ in §7, staying well within the $\le 32$ scalar budget per $t_0$):**
     - **C5a (Cumulative clipped gradient norm):** $\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \|\tilde{g}_\tau\|_2$, where $\tilde{g}_\tau = \min(1, 1.0/\|g_\tau\|_2) g_\tau$ (1 scalar).
     - **C5b (LR-weighted raw/clipped gradient norms & loss):** $\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \eta_\tau \|g_\tau\|_2$, $\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \eta_\tau \|\tilde{g}_\tau\|_2$, and $\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \eta_\tau L_\tau$ (3 scalars).
     - **C5c (Cumulative squared gradient norm & SI optimization work):** $\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \eta_\tau^2 \|\tilde{g}_\tau\|_2^2$ and $\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} |\langle g_\tau, \Delta\theta_\tau \rangle|$ (or $\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} \eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$) (2 scalars).
     - **C2b (Noise-scale / batch-size weighted steps):** $\sum_{\tau=N(t_0)+1}^{N(t_1)} (\eta_\tau / B_\tau)$, where $B_\tau \in \{16, 32, 64\}$ is the minibatch size at step $\tau$ (1 scalar).
     - **Explicit interval rates:** $\bar{\eta} = \text{C3}/\Delta N$, $\bar{L} = \text{C4}/\Delta N$, and $\bar{g} = \text{C5}/\Delta N$ (0 extra stored scalars).
     - **State covariates in $X_{\text{cov}}$ & C6b:** `agent_step_at_t0` $= N(t_0)$, `task_tenure_at_t0` $= N(t_0) - N(t_{\text{first},k})$, active learned task count $K_{\text{active}}(t_0)$, $\|\theta(t_0)\|_2$, $\sqrt{\text{Tr}(v(t_0))}$, and interval changes $\Delta\|\theta\|_2 = \|\theta(t_1)\|_2 - \|\theta(t_0)\|_2$ and $\Delta\sqrt{\text{Tr}(v)}$ (2 stored scalars per $t_0$).

5. **Stream Families (Appendix Q5, p. 12 & §4.4, p. 5):**
   - **Evaluation:** Yes. Stream families F2–F4 are one-sided decoupling designs that favour $c_e$ by construction: every mechanism in F2 (re-exposure to already-mastered data where $L_t \to 0$ and $\Delta\theta \to 0$ while steps/tokens advance), F3 (LR warm restarts where $\Delta\theta \propto \eta_t$), and F4 (idle intervals where $\Delta\theta = 0$ while $c_a$ advances) injects low-change or zero-change steps where step/tick counts advance while parameter movement shrinks or stops. Note also that in F4, during idle intervals where $\theta$ is frozen, $\text{C1}$ ($\Delta N$) through $\text{C7}$ are all identically zero just like $\Delta c_e$, meaning F4 only decouples wall-clock ticks ($\text{C0}$) from optimizer steps ($\text{C1}$) and provides zero decoupling between $c_e$ and $\text{C1}$–$\text{C7}$. Because F5 is a mixture of F2–F4, it inherits this exact directional bias. None of F1–F5 tests the converse regime (**high parameter/output path length $c_e$ without functional forgetting on target tasks**—i.e., false-positive drift or unproductive trajectory churn).
   - **Concrete Replacement (Add Adversarial Stream Family F6 — "Orthogonal Subspace Churn and Scale-Invariant Drift" in §4.4):**
     Treat F4 solely as a sanity check (not pooled into the primary H1 average), and add adversarial stream family **F6**:
     - *Generator for F6:* Interleave standard target-task exposure segments (arriving at a regular step/event rate tracked by C1 $\Delta N$ and C7 semantic task counts) with variable-length "high-step-norm distractor segments" consisting of:
       1. *Disjoint-subspace / high-noise distractor tasks:* Training on a reserved distractor key/value pool with zero overlap with any target task ($\pi_{\text{overlap}} = 0$, $P_{\text{distractor}} \cap P_{\text{target}} = \emptyset$) under small batch size ($B = 8$ or $16$) and high label noise / label smoothing ($\epsilon = 0.3$), generating persistent large gradients and oscillatory parameter updates $\|\Delta\theta_\tau\|_2$ (rapidly inflating D1 and D2) in a subspace orthogonal to the target tasks.
       2. *Pre-LN weight-decay / scale-drift bursts:* Varying AdamW weight decay $\lambda_\tau \in \{0.0, 0.01, 0.10\}$ or isotropic LayerNorm-invariant multiplicative weight rescaling pulses $\theta_\ell \leftarrow (1 + \alpha)\theta_\ell$ on Pre-LN weights during low-loss reference segments (which inflate parameter-space path length D1 and D2 while Pre-LN normalization keeps functional outputs on target tasks invariant; Zhang et al., 2019, `arXiv:1810.02338`).
     - In F6, $\Delta c_e^{\text{D1/D2}}$ is heavily contaminated by orthogonal churn and scale drift, whereas C7 (count of overlapping target-domain task exposures), C4 (cumulative loss), and C1 ($\Delta N$) remain accurate predictors of target forgetting.

6. **Identification (Appendix Q6, p. 12 & §2, §6, §8, pp. 4, 6–7):**
   - **Evaluation:** Section 2 states: *"Within a run, at a fixed evaluation time $t_1$, the ranking of tasks by $\Delta c_e = c_e(t_1) - c_e(t_{0,k})$ is identical to their ranking by $t_{0,k}$, the time since the last exposure. The reason is that $c_e$ is non-decreasing. A global scalar index therefore carries no task-specific information at a fixed time."* Two key analytical points apply:
     1. *Ordinal Rank Equivalence vs. Cardinal Metric Spacing at Fixed $t_1$:* Because $c_e(t)$ is non-decreasing, for any two tasks $j, k$ with exposure completion steps $t_{0,j} < t_{0,k} \le t_1$, we have $\Delta c_e(t_{0,j}, t_1) \ge \Delta c_e(t_{0,k}, t_1)$ (with ties under F4/F5 idle intervals where $D = 0$). However, only the **ordinal ranking** of tasks at a fixed $t_1$ matches their ranking by elapsed steps $\Delta N = N(t_1) - N(t_{0,k})$. Their **cardinal spacing** across tasks, $\Delta c_e(t_{0,j}, t_1) - \Delta c_e(t_{0,k}, t_1) = c_e(t_{0,k}) - c_e(t_{0,j}) = \sum_{\tau=t_{0,j}}^{t_{0,k}-1} \rho(\tau)$, varies directly with the adaptation rate $\rho(\tau) = dc_e/dN$ in the intervening sub-interval $[t_{0,j}, t_{0,k}]$. Because the prediction models (`HistGradientBoostingRegressor` and ridge regression) take continuous cardinal inputs and can form interactions with task covariates $X_{\text{cov}}$, $\Delta c_e$ carries rate information across distinct intervals $(t_{0,k}, t_1]$ even at a fixed $t_1$. What $\Delta c_e$ cannot do on its own is differentiate two tasks sharing the exact same exposure boundary $t_0$ and evaluation time $t_1$.
     2. *Confounding Cross-Task Heterogeneity with Longitudinal Within-Task Variance:* H1 is well-posed on pooled pairs $(k, t_0, t_1)$ (because both compared models include $B_{\text{cheap}}$, so $\Delta R^2_r$ isolates rate variation $\rho(t)$ across intervals), and restricting the target solely to variation across $t_1$ within a task would discard valid cross-interval rate variation across different exposure windows $[t_{0,k}, t_1]$. However, when within-run $R^2_r = 1 - \text{SSE}_r / \text{SST}_r$ is computed around the grand mean of run $r$, $\text{SST}_r$ pools static cross-task heterogeneity ($X_{\text{cov}}$: fact vs. rule tasks, baseline loss, where a global scalar $c_e$ carries zero task-specific information by §2) with longitudinal temporal variation across $(t_0, t_1]$. When static cross-task differences account for $50\%\text{–}70\%$ of $\text{SST}_r$, pooling both components compresses the achievable $\Delta R^2_r$ of any interval predictor by $2\times\text{–}3\times$.
   - **Concrete Replacement:** Retain the episode-weighted pooled within-run $R^2_r$ (with $N(t_0), K_{\text{active}}(t_0) \in X_{\text{cov}}$) as the primary H1 metric, revise §2 to distinguish *ordinal rank equivalence* from *cardinal interval spacing*, and report a mandatory **two-part longitudinal decomposition of $R^2_r$** in §6 and §8:
     1. **Within-exposure longitudinal skill $R^2_{\text{within-}(k,t_0), r}$:**
        $$R^2_{\text{within-}(k,t_0), r} = 1 - \frac{\sum_{k, t_0, t_1} w_{k,t_0,t_1} \left(F_k(t_0, t_1) - \hat{F}_k(t_0, t_1)\right)^2}{\sum_{k, t_0, t_1} w_{k,t_0,t_1} \left(F_k(t_0, t_1) - \bar{F}_{k, t_0}\right)^2},$$
        where $\bar{F}_{k, t_0}$ is the mean forgetting of exposure episode $(k, t_0)$ across its evaluation times $t_1 > t_0$ (alongside first-differenced sub-intervals across consecutive 200-step grid points where $\Delta N \equiv 200$ is constant).
     2. **Post-covariate partial interval skill $\Delta R^2_{\text{partial}, r} = \frac{\text{SSE}_r(B_{\text{cheap}}) - \text{SSE}_r(B_{\text{cheap}} + c_e)}{\text{SSE}_r(X_{\text{cov}})}$:** isolating the fraction of post-covariate temporal variance explained by $\Delta c_e$.

7. **H2 Ratio Estimator (Appendix Q7, p. 12 & §10, p. 8):**
   - **Evaluation:** **Definitive statistical flaw (FATAL for H2).** In §10 (row H2), the confirmatory test statistic is defined as the ratio of incremental variances explained:
     $$\hat{\theta}_{\text{H2}} = \frac{R^2(B_{\text{cheap}} + c_e) - R^2(B_{\text{cheap}})}{R^2(B_{\text{cheap}} + \text{Disp}^{D^*}) - R^2(B_{\text{cheap}})},$$
     evaluated via a 95% BCa bootstrap confidence interval over test runs and declared supported if the lower bound is $\ge 0.80$. While an $80\%$ retention threshold is substantively meaningful for compressing $\approx 8 \times 10^5$ stored parameters per $t_0$ into a single scalar, the ratio estimator exhibits three severe statistical defects:
     1. *Fieller–Creasy instability (Fieller, 1954, `doi:10.1111/j.2517-6161.1954.tb00159.x`; Franz, 2007, `arXiv:0710.2024`; Efron, 1987, `doi:10.1080/01621459.1987.10478410`):* Whenever the denominator $\Delta R^2(\text{Disp}^{D^*}) = R^2(B_{\text{cheap}} + \text{Disp}^{D^*}) - R^2(B_{\text{cheap}})$ is small or has non-negligible probability density near or below zero across bootstrap resamples (which will occur if H1 is equivalent to zero or in stream family F1 where step counts suffice), the sampling distribution of the ratio becomes heavy-tailed (infinite variance), bimodal, or unbounded ($\pm \infty$), causing BCa bias-correction and jackknife acceleration parameters to fail completely. Furthermore, §10 omits run subscripts $r$ and overbars in H2, leaving it ambiguous whether the statistic is the ratio of across-run means or the mean of per-run ratios (the latter diverges if even a single test run has $\Delta R^2_r(\text{Disp}^{D^*}) \le 0$).
     2. *Degenerate null ratios:* H2 has no gate requiring that $\text{Disp}^{D^*}$ itself beats $B_{\text{cheap}}$; if neither $c_e$ nor $\text{Disp}^{D^*}$ adds meaningful skill over $B_{\text{cheap}}$ (e.g., $\overline{\Delta R^2}(c_e) = +0.004$ and $\overline{\Delta R^2}(\text{Disp}^{D^*}) = +0.0045$, or $-0.004 / -0.005 = +0.80$), the raw ratio exceeds $0.80$ despite zero practical predictive value.
     3. *Undefined TOST equivalence bound:* Section 10 specifies that every confirmatory hypothesis has a three-way outcome (supported, equivalent, inconclusive), but H2 specifies only a one-sided support threshold ($\ge 0.80$) without defining an equivalence interval.
   - **Concrete Replacement:**
     1. **Prerequisite denominator gate (Gate G2b in §11):** Evaluate H2 confirmatorily *only* if the run-mean incremental validity of net displacement $\overline{\Delta R^2}(\text{Disp}^{D^*}) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^n [R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}} + \text{Disp}^{D^*}) - R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}})]$ passes the minimal-effect test (Holm-adjusted lower confidence bound $> \delta = 0.05$, or at minimum $> 0.02$). Otherwise, report H2 as *not identifiable (insufficient displacement incremental signal to retain)*.
     2. **Linearized Fieller difference statistic (§10, H2):** Replace the non-linear ratio in the bootstrap with Fieller's linearized per-run contrast at $\gamma_0 = 0.80$:
        $$Z_r(0.80) = \Delta R^2_r(c_e) - 0.80\,\Delta R^2_r(\text{Disp}^{D^*}) = R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}} + \Delta c_e^{D^*}) - 0.80\,R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}} + \text{Disp}^{D^*}) - 0.20\,R^2_r(B_{\text{cheap}}).$$
        Compute the Holm-adjusted BCa bootstrap CI on the run-level mean $\bar{Z}(0.80) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{r=1}^n Z_r(0.80)$. Declare H2 **supported** if the lower bound of $\bar{Z}(0.80)$ is $\ge 0$ (conditional on passing Gate G2b), and **equivalent** if the TOST CI for $\bar{Z}(0.80)$ lies within $(-\delta_{\text{H2}}, +\delta_{\text{H2}})$ with $\delta_{\text{H2}} = 0.01$. Because $Z_r(0.80)$ is a linear combination of bounded $R^2_r$ scores, it has strictly finite variance on every resample, never divides by zero, and is algebraically equivalent to $\overline{\Delta R^2}(c_e) / \overline{\Delta R^2}(\text{Disp}^{D^*}) \ge 0.80$ whenever the denominator is positive. Report the plug-in ratio of means $\overline{\Delta R^2}(c_e) / \overline{\Delta R^2}(\text{Disp}^{D^*})$ and its Fieller confidence set descriptively alongside $\bar{Z}(0.80)$.

8. **H3 Order-Block Identifiability (Appendix Q8, p. 12 & §1 Q3, §4.4, §10 H3, §11 G9):**
   - **Evaluation:** **Definitive mathematical identifiability flaw (FATAL for Q3/H3 as currently designed).** In Q3 (§1, p. 4), Family O (§4.4, p. 5), and H3 (§10, p. 8), task $k$ is learned up to $t_0$, followed by segment $X$ (high key overlap with $k$) and segment $Y$ (low overlap with $k$) in orders $XY$ vs. $YX$ with the same seed and multiset, and $\Delta F_k = F_k^{\text{XY}}(t_0, t_1) - F_k^{\text{YX}}(t_0, t_1)$ and $\Delta(\Delta c_e) = \Delta c_e^{\text{XY}}(t_0, t_1) - \Delta c_e^{\text{YX}}(t_0, t_1)$ are compared at the end of the two-segment block $t_1$. This design faces three coupled identification failures:
     1. *Task-Identity Invariance vs. Opposite Interference Signs:* By §1 and §2, $c_e(t) = \sum_{\tau < t} D(S(\tau), S(\tau+1))$ is a **task-agnostic global scalar** over the entire network state $S$. Because "high key overlap with $k$" is a relational property between $X$ and $k$, the accumulated trajectory length $\Delta c_e(t_0, t_1) = \Delta c_e(X) + \Delta c_e(Y)$ over the completed block $(t_0, t_1]$ does not depend on which earlier task $k$ is being probed. If two tasks are learned prior to $t_0$—task $k_1$ (high overlap with $X$, low with $Y$) and task $k_2$ (low overlap with $X$, high with $Y$)—the global scalar difference $\Delta(\Delta c_e) = \Delta c_e^{XY} - \Delta c_e^{YX}$ over $[t_0, t_1]$ is **identical** for both $k_1$ and $k_2$, whereas reversing the order from $XY$ to $YX$ reverses the recency of high-overlap retroactive interference, giving $\Delta F_{k_1}$ and $\Delta F_{k_2}$ **opposite signs**. Furthermore, if $X$ and $Y$ have comparable marginal novelty and length, $\Delta c_e(X) + \Delta c_e(Y)$ is approximately commutative ($\Delta(\Delta c_e) \approx 0$ up to optimization noise), yet Gate G9 (§11) checks only whether $\Delta F$ has split-half reliability $\ge 0.50$, omitting any check on whether $\Delta(\Delta c_e)$ has non-zero variance or reliability.
     2. *Sign Reversal Between Primacy of Gradient Conflict and Recency of Overwriting:* Even when only a single task $k$ (overlapping with $X$) is learned at $t_0$, training high-overlap segment $X$ immediately after $k$ (order $XY$) induces strong immediate gradient conflict on $k$'s freshly written keys ($\Delta c_e^{\text{XY}} > \Delta c_e^{\text{YX}}$, so $\Delta(\Delta c_e) > 0$), whereas in order $YX$ high-overlap $X$ occurs immediately before $t_1$ (maximizing recency of retroactive overwriting on $k$, so $F_k^{\text{YX}} > F_k^{\text{XY}}$ and $\Delta F_k < 0$). This induces a *negative* correlation ($\rho_S < 0$), causing a one-sided test for $\rho_S > +0.10$ to fail even when $c_e$ tracks path dependence.
     3. *Weak threshold and missing TOST bounds:* A Spearman correlation lower bound of $\rho_S > 0.10$ corresponds to only $1\%$ shared rank variance, and §10 specifies no TOST equivalence bounds or resampling unit for H3.
   - **Concrete Replacement:**
     1. **Redesign the O blocks for global path-length asymmetry (§4.4, §10 H3):**
        - *Option A (Global Regime / Curriculum Non-Commutativity Blocks at $t_1$):* Hold task-$k$ key overlap equal across $X$ and $Y$ (or pair a high-novelty/conflict segment $A$ and a low-novelty mastered re-exposure segment $B$) across a two-stage decaying learning-rate schedule $(\eta_{\text{high}}, \eta_{\text{low}})$ or batch-size plasticity regime under a fixed multiset of batches, steps, tokens, and cumulative learning rate $\sum \eta_t$: compare order $(A \text{ at } \eta_{\text{high}}, B \text{ at } \eta_{\text{low}})$ vs. $(B \text{ at } \eta_{\text{high}}, A \text{ at } \eta_{\text{low}})$, where order alters both global trajectory length $\Delta c_e(t_0, t_1)$ and forgetting $\Delta F_k$ in the same positive direction while C0–C3 and C7 remain strictly invariant at $t_1$.
        - *Option B (Midpoint / Trajectory-AUC Evaluation or Recency-Discounted Index for Overlap Blocks):* Evaluate $F_k$ and $\Delta c_e$ at the block **midpoint $t_{\text{mid}}$** (or compare the midpoint-to-endpoint trajectory area under the curve $\text{AUC}(F_k)$ vs. $\text{AUC}(\Delta c_e)$), or replace unweighted $\Delta c_e(t_0, t_1)$ in H3 with a prespecified recency-weighted exponential moving average index $\tilde{c}_e(t) = \gamma \tilde{c}_e(t-1) + D(S(t-1), S(t))$ (costing 1 stored scalar), paired with a low-overlap control task $k_{\text{low}}$.
     2. **Expand Gate G9 (§11):** Require *both* the split-half reliability of $\Delta F$ (computed between disjoint probe halves $\Delta F_A$ and $\Delta F_B$ and across seed pairs) to be $\ge 0.50$ *and* the cross-seed/split-half reliability of $\Delta(\Delta c_e^{D^*})$ to be $\ge 0.50$ with coefficient of variation $\text{SD}(\Delta(\Delta c_e^{D^*})) / \overline{\Delta c_e^{D^*}} \ge 0.05$.
     3. **Raise the H3 SESOI (§10):** Increase the H3 support threshold from $0.10$ to a lower confidence bound of **$\rho_S > 0.20$** ($4\%$ rank variance explained), with TOST equivalence bounds $(-0.20, +0.20)$ and the matched run-pair as the explicit bootstrap resampling unit.

9. **Stage B Rehearsal Scheduling (Appendix Q9, p. 12 & §1 Q4, §2, §9, §10 H4):**
   - **Evaluation:** As shown in §2 (Consequence 2), ranking tasks purely by $\Delta c_e(t_{0,k}, t_1)$ at a fixed decision time $t_1$ reduces identically to least-recently-rehearsed (LRR). Consequently, separating temporal allocation (`S-c_e` vs. single-scalar triggers using LRR task selection to test *when* to rehearse) from covariate-informed task selection (`S-pred(B_cheap + c_e)` vs. `S-pred(B_cheap)` to test *joint when-and-which* decisions via non-linear interactions between $\Delta c_e$ and $X_{\text{cov}}$) is the right conceptual test of Criterion (d). However, §§9–10 contain three mechanical flaws that threaten H4:
     1. *Uncontrolled rehearsal budget expenditure on stochastic test runs:* Section 9 specifies a fixed budget of $Q$ rehearsal steps per run (in segments of $r = 50$ steps, i.e., $K_{\text{reh}} = Q / 50$ rehearsal triggers) and states that all trigger thresholds ($\theta_e$) are calibrated on validation runs *"to spend exactly $Q$."* Because test runs in F2–F5 have stochastic novelty, variable batch sizes, random warm restarts, random idle intervals, shifted F5 task/overlap mixtures (§12), and closed-loop rehearsal feedback on $\theta(t)$, a static open-loop threshold $\theta_e$ calibrated on validation averages cannot spend *exactly* $Q$ on every individual test run: on high-change runs it will **exhaust all $K_{\text{reh}}$ rehearsals early**, and on low-change or yoked G8 runs it will **underspend $Q$** relative to `S-uniform`, `S-oracle`, and `S-random`. Because T3 retention increases monotonically with total rehearsal steps executed, comparing T3 across different realized budgets violates the "at equal budget" requirement of H4.
     2. *Post-hoc max-of-three competitor bias and missing trigger baselines in H4:* Requiring `S-c_e` in H4 to beat *"the best of S-C3, S-C4 and S-C7"* on the test set introduces positive order-statistic (winner's-curse) bias ($\mathbb{E}[\max_j \bar{T}_{3,j}] > \max_j \mathbb{E}[\bar{T}_{3,j}]$), while omitting **`S-uniform` (`S-C1`)**, **`S-C2` (tokens)**, and **`S-C5` (cumulative gradient norm $\sum \|g_t\|_2$)** from the trigger comparator pool. Furthermore, §10 does not state whether H4 is an intersection-union conjunction test ($M = 4$ hypotheses) or two separate tests ($M = 5$).
     3. *Online availability of $\bar{L}_{A,k}(t_0)$ in `S-pred`:* Section 9 does not specify how `S-pred` updates baseline loss and accuracy covariates in $X_{\text{cov}}$ after a rehearsal segment without making privileged online test-probe queries.
   - **Concrete Replacement:**
     1. **Online proportional budget-pacing controller (§9):** Enforce a hard cap of $K_{\text{reh}} = Q / 50$ rehearsal segments per run, and modulate the validation-calibrated base threshold $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ online at step $N$ by the ratio of remaining budget fraction to remaining life steps:
        $$\theta_{\text{eff}}(N) = \theta_{\text{cal}} \cdot \frac{(K_{\text{reh}} - k_{\text{spent}}(N)) / K_{\text{reh}}}{\max\!\big(\epsilon,\; (N_{\text{life}} - N) / N_{\text{life}}\big)},$$
        with a terminal flush forcing any remaining rehearsals when $N_{\text{life}} - N \le (K_{\text{reh}} - k_{\text{spent}}(N)) \times 50$. Log realized rehearsal steps $Q_{\text{actual}, r}$ per run and assert $Q_{\text{actual}, r} = Q$ in Gate G7.
     2. **Eliminate post-hoc max bias and expand trigger comparators (§9, §10):** Expand the trigger competitor pool to `{S-uniform, S-C2, S-C3, S-C4, S-C5, S-C7}`, select and freeze the single best trigger competitor `S-C*` on **validation runs** before test evaluation, and split H4 explicitly into **H4a** (`S-pred(B_cheap + c_e) - S-pred(B_cheap)`) and **H4b** (`S-c_e - S-C*`) inside a 5-hypothesis Holm procedure across `{H1, H2, H3, H4a, H4b}` (or explicitly define H4 as an Intersection-Union conjunction test keeping $M = 4$).
     3. **Online covariate hygiene and logging (§9):** In `S-pred`, compute post-exposure baseline loss and accuracy covariates in $X_{\text{cov}}$ from the **final training mini-batches of exposure/rehearsal segment $s_0$** (or explicitly declare probe half A an agent-held self-test buffer in Stage B, since T3 is evaluated on disjoint probe half B), and log both the Wasserstein distance between rehearsal trigger timestamps (measuring *when*) and the task-selection agreement rate at matched timestamps (measuring *which* task).

10. **Placebo / Yoked Run (Appendix Q10, p. 12 & §9, §11 Gates G4 & G8, pp. 8–9):**
    - **Evaluation:** A yoked-run $c_e^{\text{yoked}}(t)$ drawn from another run of the **same stream family** (different seed) is **not a valid zero-information placebo** for Gate G4 (§11, p. 9), which halts the entire study (*"Stop; leakage or a design artefact"*) if the 90% CI of placebo $\Delta R^2$ over $B_{\text{cheap}}$ does not fall within $\pm 0.01$:
      1. *Cross-family regime identification in pooled models:* In §8, Stage A models are fitted on pooled pairs across families F1–F4 and evaluated across F1–F5, while categorical `stream_family` is not in $X_{\text{cov}}$ or $B_{\text{cheap}}$ (§7). Because F1–F4 have systematically different mean adaptation rates $\bar{\rho}_{\text{family}} = \mathbb{E}[dc_e/dN \mid \text{family}]$ and variance profiles (e.g., F4 has $\Delta c_e = 0$ during idle ticks while $\Delta c_a > 0$; F2 has mastered re-exposures), a yoked $\Delta c_e^{\text{yoked}}(t_0, t_1)$ drawn from the *same family* acts as a proxy for `stream_family`. A GBDT can exploit this family-level regime identity to achieve placebo $\Delta R^2 > 0.01$.
      2. *Shared macro-schedule phase and tick-vs-step alignment:* In F1 (fixed segment lengths), or in F2–F5 if stream-generator schedule seeds are not independent, matching a yoked run by step timestamps transfers periodic task-boundary or warm-restart phase; furthermore, in F4/F5, two seeds have different mappings between tick time $c_a$ and optimizer step $N$, and §9/§11 do not state whether yoked streams are aligned by $N(t)$ or $c_a(t)$.
      3. *Unachievable two-sided $\pm 0.01$ TOST margin in Gate G4:* Section 12 sizes $n$ for a margin of $\delta = 0.05$, where the standard error of $\Delta R^2$ is $\approx 0.015$ (90% CI half-width $\approx \pm 0.025 > 0.01$). Random sampling or 50-draw hyperparameter-search split dilution (e.g., $\Delta R^2 = -0.012$) will routinely push a true-zero placebo outside $\pm 0.01$ and trigger a false protocol stop.
    - **Concrete Replacement:**
      1. **Within-run rate-permuted placebo (Primary for G4 & G8):** Construct $c_e^{\text{perm}}(t)$ within the *same* run $r$ by partitioning run $r$'s active step increments $\{\delta c_e(\tau)\}_{\tau=1}^N$ into contiguous 200-step blocks (keeping $\delta c_e = 0$ on F4 idle ticks), randomly permuting those blocks within run $r$, and re-accumulating $c_e^{\text{perm}}(t) = \sum_{\tau < t} \delta c_e^{\text{perm}}(\tau)$. This preserves the exact run-level and family-level mean rate $\bar{\rho}_r$, marginal distribution, and short-range autocorrelation (zero family-ID leakage) while destroying temporal alignment with $(t_0, t_1]$. For any cross-seed yoked run, assert independent generator schedule seeds, align by optimizer step $N(t)$, and evaluate G4 within each stream family separately.
      2. **One-sided Gate G4 criterion with locked hyperparameters (§11):** Evaluate Gate G4 holding the tuned hyperparameters of $B_{\text{cheap}}$ fixed and replace the two-sided $\pm 0.01$ band with a **one-sided non-superiority bound**: require point estimate $\overline{\Delta R^2}_{\text{placebo}} \le 0.005$ and the 90% bootstrap upper confidence bound $< +0.01$ (or $< +0.015$).

11. **Power and Sample-Size Rule (Appendix Q11, p. 12 & §10, §12, §15, pp. 8–10):**
    - **Evaluation:** **Definitive statistical inconsistency.** Sections 10, 12, and 15 contain three coupled mathematical mismatches:
      1. *One-sided $\alpha$ mismatch between MET (95% CI) and TOST (90% CI):* Section 10 declares a hypothesis **supported** if the lower bound of the two-sided **95%** bootstrap CI exceeds $\delta$ (a one-sided minimal-effect test at $\alpha_{\text{MET}} = \mathbf{0.025}$, $z_{0.975} = 1.9600$), and **equivalent** if the two-sided **90%** bootstrap CI lies within $(-\delta, \delta)$ (two one-sided tests each at $\alpha_{\text{TOST}} = \mathbf{0.05}$, $z_{0.95} = 1.6449$; Lakens et al., 2018, `doi:10.1177/2515245918770963`). This leaves an artificial dead zone when the 90% lower bound exceeds $\delta$ but the 95% lower bound does not, and sizing MET at $\alpha = 0.05$ in §12 while testing it with a 95% two-sided CI leaves MET underpowered ($\approx 71\%$ power instead of $80\%$).
      2. *Central TOST vs. MET power formula and omission of Holm multiplicity:* For a paired difference with per-run SD $\sigma$ and margin $\delta$ at target power $1 - \beta = 0.80$, one-sided MET at true effect $\Delta R^2 = 2\delta$ (distance $\delta$ above threshold $\delta$) places all Type II error $\beta = 0.20$ in one tail ($z_{1-\beta} = z_{0.80} = 0.8416$). By contrast, central TOST at true effect $\Delta R^2 = 0$ (distance $\delta$ from both $-\delta$ and $+\delta$) must reject *both* upper and lower one-sided tests simultaneously, splitting Type II error symmetrically ($\beta / 2 = 0.10$ in each tail, $z_{1-\beta/2} = z_{0.90} = 1.2816$). Using $z_{0.80}$ instead of $z_{0.90}$ for TOST at $\Delta R^2 = 0$ yields only $\approx 60\%$ actual power. Furthermore, §10 specifies fixed 95%/90% CIs alongside *"Multiplicity: Holm across H1–H4, with family-wise $\alpha = 0.05$"* without stating how Holm step-down adjusts the CI coverage levels, and §12 ignores the Holm divisor ($\alpha_1 = 0.05 / 4 = 0.0125$, or $0.05 / 5 = 0.010$) in the sample-size calculation.
      3. *Underpowered F5 subgroup test:* Section 15 (Rows 1 & 3) requires H1 to be supported **on F5 alone**, but §12 sizes $n$ only for the pooled mean across all runs.
    - **Concrete Replacement:**
      1. **Harmonize MET/TOST with Holm step-down CIs (§10):** Order the $M$ confirmatory hypotheses by their one-sided bootstrap $p$-values (separately for the MET family and TOST family) and at Holm rank $k \in \{1, \dots, M\}$ set $\alpha_k = 0.05 / (M - k + 1)$ (for $M = 4$, $\alpha_k \in \{0.0125, 0.0167, 0.025, 0.05\}$). Construct the two-sided $(1 - 2\alpha_k)$ BCa confidence interval $[L_k, U_k]$ (i.e., $97.5\%, 96.67\%, 95.0\%, 90.0\%$ CIs): declare **supported** if $L_k > \delta$ and **equivalent** if $[L_k, U_k] \subset (-\delta, \delta)$ (Campbell & Gustafson, 2018, `doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0195145`).
      2. **Explicit Holm-protected sample-size formula (§12):** Replace the verbal sample-size rule in §12 with the explicit formula at $\alpha_1 = 0.05 / 4 = 0.0125$ ($z_{0.9875} = 2.2414$) and $1 - \beta = 0.80$ using the upper 80% chi-square confidence bound $\hat{\sigma}_{80\%, \text{cal}}$ from calibration runs (to guard against calibration variance underestimation):
         $$n = \max\!\left( \underbrace{\left\lceil (z_{1-\alpha_1} + z_{1-\beta/2})^2 \left(\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{80\%, \text{cal}}}{\delta}\right)^2 \right\rceil}_{\text{TOST at } \Delta = 0:\; \lceil 12.42\,(\hat{\sigma}_{80\%, \text{cal}}/\delta)^2 \rceil},\; \underbrace{\left\lceil (z_{1-\alpha_1} + z_{1-\beta})^2 \left(\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{80\%, \text{cal}}}{\delta}\right)^2 \right\rceil}_{\text{MET at } \Delta = 2\delta:\; \lceil 9.51\,(\hat{\sigma}_{80\%, \text{cal}}/\delta)^2 \rceil} \right) = \left\lceil 12.42\,\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{80\%, \text{cal}}^2}{\delta^2} \right\rceil$$
         (computed separately for Stage A using $\hat{\sigma}_{\Delta R^2}, \delta$ and Stage B using $\hat{\sigma}_{\Delta \text{T3}}, \delta_B$), and allocate at least $40\%\text{–}50\%$ of the test-run budget ($n_{\text{F5}} \ge \lceil 8.56\,(\hat{\sigma}_{80\%, \text{cal}}/\delta)^2 \rceil$) to F5 so the standalone F5 test achieves $\ge 0.80$ power.

12. **Scaling to Stage C (Appendix Q12, p. 12 & §13, §15 Row 9, pp. 9–10):**
    - **Evaluation:** **FATAL for Stage C transfer.** Section 13 claims that the protocol transfers to Stage C (pretrained Pythia 70M–1.4B on public text/instruction domains, with full fine-tuning and LoRA) *"identical, with only the configuration file changed."* In reality, **eight concrete elements** of the Stage A protocol break or change mathematical meaning in Stage C unless unified in the shared protocol now:
      1. *Inclusion criterion (`probe-A accuracy >= 0.90`, §6):* On continuous open-ended text domains (e.g., Pile subsets in Stage C; Biderman et al., 2023, `arXiv:2304.01373`) over a $50,304$-token vocabulary, next-token top-1 accuracy is typically $0.30\text{–}0.55$, never $\ge 0.90$. Applying the literal Stage A rule excludes **100% of pairs** in Stage C.
      2. *Covariate $\pi_{\text{overlap}}$, `task type`, and C7 novelty labels (§4.1, §7):* Natural text domains have no synthetic key-pool sharing parameter $\pi_{\text{overlap}}$, binary `fact vs. rule` label, or oracle "new vs. re-exposure" labels.
      3. *Childhood phase (§4.3):* Stage A begins with a 3,000-step childhood phase training reference tasks $R$ from random initialization. Pretrained LMs already have a 300B-token pretraining prior; running 3,000 steps on $R$ before Life would fine-tune the pretrained model onto the reference battery itself and induce catastrophic forgetting of pretraining knowledge before domain 1 begins.
      4. *LoRA $B = 0$ initialization asymmetry and gauge non-invariance (§5, §7, §13):* Under LoRA ($\Delta W = \frac{\alpha}{r} B A$ with $B(0) = 0$ and $A(0) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$; Hu et al., 2022, `arXiv:2106.09685`), §13 states: *"Under LoRA, D is computed on the adapter parameters."* However, computing Euclidean distance D1/D2/C6 on the concatenated adapter vector $\phi = \text{vec}([A, B])$, $\sqrt{\|\Delta A\|_F^2 + \|\Delta B\|_F^2}$, violates functional equivalence: at early steps when $B_\tau \approx 0$, updates $\Delta A_\tau$ produce large $\|\Delta\phi\|_2 > 0$ while producing **near-zero change in the effective weight matrix** $\Delta W_\tau = \frac{\alpha}{r}(B_{\tau+1}A_{\tau+1} - B_\tau A_\tau) \approx 0$, and the bilinear product $BA$ has a continuous scale symmetry $(B R^{-1}, R A)$ that changes $\|A\|_F^2 + \|B\|_F^2$ without changing $BA$.
      5. *D3 ($\sqrt{\text{JS}}$) memory and compute explosion over 50,304 vocabulary tokens (§5, §13):* In Stage A, $|R| = 256$ over $V \approx 512$ tokens at single target positions costs $256 \times 512 \times 4\text{ B} = 0.5\text{ MB}$. In Pythia ($V = 50,304$), if D3 is computed across all sequence positions $L = 64$ of $|R| = 256$ texts, storing $p_{S(\tau)}(\cdot \mid x)$ between step $\tau$ and $\tau + 10$ requires $256 \times 64 \times 50,304 \times 4\text{ B} \approx \mathbf{3.30\text{ GB}}$ of RAM per snapshot (or $132\text{ GB}$ to cache 40 anchor distributions for $\text{Disp}^{\text{D3}}(t_0, t_1)$)—making $\text{Disp}^{\text{D3}}$ vastly larger than the model parameters themselves.
      6. *Reference predictors C8b/C9 at 1.4B scale (§5, §7, §13):* Under full fine-tuning of Pythia-1.4B, storing parameter snapshots $S(t_0)$ (for $\text{Disp}^{D^*}$ and C8b) and task gradients $\nabla_\theta L_k(S(t_0))$ (for C9) across $\approx 40$ segments requires $\approx 224\text{–}448\text{ GB}$ of storage per run.
