# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This document presents a draft preregistration and technical protocol (v0.2) for an empirical audit of an agent-held "experiential index" of adaptation ($c_e$)—defined as the accumulated step-wise or chunk-wise change of a persistent neural network's state under candidate parameter-space and function-space distance metrics—in continual learning. While acknowledging that prior work already introduced cumulative $L_2$ update norms as a model-centric clock for replay scheduling, the protocol formulates a deconfounded identification audit to test whether $c_e$ provides genuine predictive validity for catastrophic forgetting beyond a comprehensive baseline of cheap online optimizer telemetry ($B_{\text{strong}}$), retains the predictive signal of full endpoint displacement snapshots at $O(1)$ per-task storage, and causally improves memory rehearsal scheduling when audited against block-permuted counterfactual placebo streams.

## Strengths
* The protocol addresses an important confounding gap in continual learning evaluation hygiene: rather than comparing a model-centric cumulative update clock solely against raw step counts, it frames the study as a strict identification audit against an exhaustive union of cheap online optimizer statistics, including cumulative learning rate, learning-rate-weighted gradient and momentum norms, path-integral work, local window statistics, and state-scale drift.
* The statistical and experimental design incorporates rigorous safeguards against false discovery and baseline under-specification, combining Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for equivalence and Minimal-Effect Tests (MET) with a synthetic positive-control sensitivity gate (G4b), a baseline hyperparameter-saturation gate (G3b), and explicit storage-efficiency comparisons between $O(1)$ running scalar path integrals and $O(|\theta|)$ task-endpoint snapshots.
* The protocol cleanly operationalizes the distinction between statistical decodability and causal reliance by pairing Stage B rehearsal schedulers with within-run block-permuted and cross-seed yoked placebo streams (Gate G8), ensuring that any downstream retention gain is attributable to the temporal alignment of $c_e$ rather than uniform pacing artefacts.

## Weaknesses
* In §10.2 and §12, the primary confirmatory statistic for H1 pools test runs across stream families F1, F2, F3, F5, and F6—directly contradicting the §4.4 role table and averaging target decoupling regimes (F2, F3) with a stationary null control (F1) and an opposite-sign adversarial regime (F6), which risks diluting a genuine effect or manufacturing a false equivalence outcome while creating an algebraic sample-size conflict with the standalone $2 \times n_{\text{MET}}$ allocations for F5 and F6.
* While $B_{\text{strong}}$ (§7, §8) supplies every cumulative telemetry competitor as both a `+1` monotonically constrained sum and an unconstrained (`0`) $\Delta N$-normalised rate (alongside unconstrained log-step terms in C1 that bypass $\Delta N$'s monotonicity constraint), the augmented set $B_{\text{strong}} + c_e$ includes only the raw increment $\Delta c_e^{D^*}$ (`+1`) and omits the mean interval rate $\bar{\rho}^{D^*} = \Delta c_e^{D^*} / \max(\Delta N, 1)$ that §2 identifies as the sole source of information in $c_e$ beyond counts.
* When $D^* = \text{D3}$ (accumulated chunk-wise $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ divergence on the reference battery $R$), comparing $\Delta c_e^{\text{D3}}$ against competitor C10 (signed net endpoint loss difference $L_R(t_1) - L_R(t_0)$) confounds path accumulation with the choice of functional discrepancy measure on $R$, and for parameter-space distances ($D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2$), asymmetric inclusion of decoupled weight decay, within-segment $\hat{v}(s)/\hat{v}(\tau)$ drift, and omitted scalar preconditioner ratios in $B_{\text{strong}}$ could manufacture an advantage unrelated to true trajectory geometry (Reference Paper: Measuring and Regularizing Networks in Function Space, Ari S. Benjamin, David Rolnick, Konrad P. Kording, 2019; Reference Paper: Continual Learning Through Synaptic Intelligence, Friedemann Zenke, Ben Poole, Surya Ganguli, 2017; Reference Paper: Overcoming catastrophic forgetting in neural networks, James Kirkpatrick et al., 2017).
* In Stage B (§9), the online pacing multiplier $\theta_{\text{eff}}(N)$ is inverted—lowering the trigger threshold when rehearsal expenditure runs ahead of schedule and creating a runaway positive feedback loop—while `S-pred` uses a non-resetting cross-task maximum trigger and suffers from a train–deployment covariate shift because Stage A models are trained on post-segment held-out probe-B metrics rather than the online training mini-batch losses fed at Stage B runtime.
* The Stage 0 go/no-go checklist (§12a), compute estimates (§14), and interpretation table (§15) contain structural gaps, including an undefined out-of-sample split on the 24 calibration runs for G2/G3b/G4b, non-mutually-exclusive rows in §15 when both F5 and F6 trigger, a $3\text{–}5\times$ underestimation of Stage B CPU-hours ($\approx 545\text{–}590$ total validation, test, and G8 placebo runs), and an incompatible 256-item task size that cannot complete full balanced epochs in 50-step segments at batch sizes $\{16, 32, 64\}$.
* For the Stage C transfer to pretrained language models (§13) and the GBDT inference pipeline (§8, §19), the protocol leaves the confirmatory decision unit across LoRA versus full fine-tuning, the $O(r^2(d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}}))$ trace computation of $\Delta W_{\text{eff}}$, and the tail-bucketed support for top-$K$ $\text{D3}$ underspecified, and relies on 5-fold cross-fitting over a fixed 30-run training split for GBDT instead of propagating training-sample variance via a two-sample bootstrap (Reference Paper: FOREVER: Forgetting Curve-Inspired Memory Replay for Language Model Continual Learning, Yujie Feng et al., 2026; Reference Paper: Pythia: A Suite for Analyzing Large Language Models Across Training and Scaling, Stella Biderman et al., 2023; Reference Paper: Accounting for Variance in Machine Learning Benchmarks, Xavier Bouthillier et al., 2021).

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Programme Context, Research Questions, and Identification Logic (§§0–2)] (Pages: 1-5)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[MAJOR] §0.7 (Glossary) and §2 (Identification note) — Division-by-zero ambiguity in $\bar{\rho} = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$ and imprecision in the task-ranking equivalence statement during idle intervals.**
   * **Section:** §0.7 (page 4) and §2 (paragraph 1, paragraph 2, and Consequence 1, page 5).
   * **Problem:** A potential concern regarding mathematical well-definedness occurs in §0.7 and §2, where the mean interval rate is defined as $\bar{\rho} = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$ and the instantaneous rate as $\rho(\tau) = dc_e / dN$. Under the inclusion rule in §6, triplets in stream families containing idle intervals (F4 and the held-out confirmatory family F5) are admitted whenever $\Delta c_a \ge 50$ ticks even if *no optimizer step is taken* ($\Delta N = 0$). On such purely idle intervals, $\Delta c_e = 0$ and $\Delta c_e / \Delta N = 0/0$ is undefined unless written as $\Delta c_e / \max(\Delta N, 1)$ (as done later in §7 and §11 Gate G2). Relatedly, §2 paragraph 1 states: *"Within a run, at a fixed evaluation time $t_1$, the ranking of tasks by $\Delta c_e = c_e(t_1) - c_e(t_{0,k})$ is identical to their ranking by $t_1 - t_{0,k}$, the time elapsed since their last exposure (ties occur where $D = 0$, for example during idle intervals)."* Under the sequential single-task segment structure in §4.3–§4.5, no two distinct tasks can finish their exposure segments at the same step or inside an idle interval; rather, if two evaluation timestamps $t_0 < t_0'$ are separated only by an idle interval, elapsed ticks $c_a(t_1) - c_a(t_0)$ strictly differ while committed steps $\Delta N$ and $\Delta c_e$ are tied.
   * **Why it matters:** Because F5 includes idle intervals and serves both as part of the pooled H1 test and as a mandatory standalone generalisation check (§15), any inconsistency between $\Delta c_e / \Delta N$ (`NaN` when $\Delta N = 0$), $\Delta c_e / \max(\Delta N, 1)$ ($0$ when $\Delta N = 0$), and $\Delta c_e / \Delta c_a$ directly affects feature computation and Gate G2 on F5.
   * **Concrete fix:** In §0.7 (row `ρ̄`) and §2 (Consequence 1), replace $\bar{\rho} = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$ with $\bar{\rho} = \Delta c_e / \max(\Delta N, 1)$ (yielding $0$ when $\Delta N = 0$). In §2 (paragraph 1), revise the first sentence to read: *"Within a run, at a fixed evaluation time $t_1$, the ranking of tasks by $\Delta c_e = c_e(t_1) - c_e(t_{0,k})$ induces the same weak ordering as committed steps $\Delta N_k = N(t_1) - N(t_{0,k})$ and elapsed time $\Delta c_{a,k} = c_a(t_1) - c_a(t_{0,k})$, with ties wherever intervening steps produce $\Delta c_e = 0$ (such as across idle intervals where $c_a$ advances while $N$ and $\theta$ are frozen)."*

2. **[MAJOR] §1 (Q4) and §2 (Consequence 2) — Potential collapse of learned covariate-aware rehearsal predictors to least-recently-rehearsed (LRR) task selection.**
   * **Section:** §1 (Q4, page 4) and §2 (Consequence 2, page 5), in relation to §9 (Stage B) and §10.2 (H4a).
   * **Problem:** Section 2 (Consequence 2) notes that because unconditional ranking of tasks by $\Delta c_e$ at a fixed evaluation time $t_1$ is equivalent to least-recently-rehearsed (LRR), Q4 must either test *when* to rehearse or *"use $c_e$ together with task covariates inside a learned predictor (§9)."* A potential concern is twofold:
     1. Mathematically, inside a learned predictor (`S-pred(B_strong)` vs. `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)`), different active tasks $k \neq j$ evaluated at time $t_1$ were last exposed at different historical times $t_{0,k} \neq t_{0,j}$ and thus experience different interval change rates $\bar{\rho}_k \neq \bar{\rho}_j$. Consequently, the cardinal spacing $c_e(t_{0,k}) - c_e(t_{0,j})$ can change the predicted argmax task at $t_1$ by trading off elapsed adaptation against non-monotonic $t_0$ covariates in `X_cov` (such as competence at $t_0$ or task tenure) or against competing interval summaries—even in an additive model without explicit $\Delta c_e \times X_{\text{cov}}$ interactions.
     2. Empirically, however, because all cumulative interval predictors in GBDT have a `+1` monotone constraint (§8) and vary over orders of magnitude across a 20,000-step life phase, whereas competence covariates at $t_0$ are restricted to already-mastered tasks (probe-B accuracy $\ge 0.90$ in Stage A; final training mini-batches of a full-epoch segment in Stage B), the variance of predicted forgetting across active tasks at a fixed $t_1$ may be completely dominated by recency ($\Delta N$). If both `S-pred(B_strong)` and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` select nearly the same LRR task as `S-uniform` / `S-c_e`, then H4a stops testing covariate-aware task selection and degenerates into a noisy duplicate of the timing trigger test H4b.
   * **Why it matters:** If the learned predictors in Stage B fail to deviate meaningfully from LRR task selection on validation runs, an `equivalent` result on H4a would be uninterpretable as evidence about task-selective scheduling.
   * **Concrete fix:** In §2 (Consequence 2), clarify that while *unconditional* ranking by $\Delta c_e$ is equivalent to LRR, *conditional* ranking inside a learned predictor uses the cardinal spacing $c_e(t_{0,k}) - c_e(t_{0,j})$ to trade off accumulated change against $t_0$ covariates and competing rates. Add a requirement in §1 (Q4) / §2 (Consequence 2) that the task-agreement diagnostic logged in §9 must be checked on validation runs to verify that `S-pred(B_strong)` and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` show non-trivial disagreement with LRR task selection (e.g., selecting a non-LRR task on at least 10% of rehearsals) before interpreting H4a as a distinct test of task selection.

3. **[MINOR] §1 (Storage) — Ambiguity between the marginal storage cost of $c_e$ and the standalone storage cost of the shared covariates `X_cov` and baseline `B_strong`.**
   * **Section:** §1 ("Storage", page 4), cross-referencing §7 (pages 9–10).
   * **Problem:** Section 1 states: *"Maintaining $c_e$ costs one running scalar. Answering a query about task $k$ needs the snapshot $c_e(t_{0,k})$ taken at its last exposure: one scalar per tracked task exposure, at most $K + 1$ scalars for $K$ active tasks (here $K \le 40$). This is $O(1)$ per update and $O(K)$ for queries, against a full snapshot per task for displacement (§5.2)."* It is slightly ambiguous whether this $K + 1$ figure describes the *marginal* storage of adding $c_e$ to a model, the *standalone* storage of the Stage B trigger `S-c_e`, or the total storage of the Stage A predictor sets. In Stage A (§7), every model is given `X_cov` (which requires caching several $t_0$ scalars per task plus a global 512-length input-token counter `q_hist(t)` to evaluate token overlap at $t_0$), and `B_strong` caches roughly 20 scalars per active task exposure (including initial-window statistics for C6).
   * **Why it matters:** Distinguishing the marginal storage of $c_e$ (1 running scalar plus 1 scalar per task exposure, or 2 scalars per task exposure alongside $N(t_{0,k})$ to compute $\bar{\rho}$ standalone) from the baseline telemetry footprint prevents readers from misinterpreting the storage accounting when `X_cov` includes a 512-counter.
   * **Concrete fix:** Revise the second sentence of the "Storage" paragraph in §1 to read: *"The marginal storage cost of adding $c_e$ to any predictor set (and the total storage cost of the standalone trigger `S-c_e`) is one running scalar plus one snapshot $c_e(t_{0,k})$ per tracked task exposure: at most $K + 1$ scalars for $K$ active tasks (here $K \le 40$, or $2(K + 1)$ scalars together with step count $N$ to form the rate $\bar{\rho}$ standalone), compared with $\approx 20$ scalars per task (plus one global 512-counter for token history in `X_cov`) for the full union of competitors in `B_strong` and $0.03\text{M}–1.9\text{M}$ numbers per task for net displacement (§5.2)."*

### [Agent Architecture, Stream Families, Distances, Targets, and Predictor Baselines (§§3–7)] (Pages: 5-11)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[FATAL] §3 (Agent), §5.1 (Candidate Distances), & §7 ($B_{\text{strong}}$), cross-checked with §19 ("Same-step D2 cancels the Adam preconditioner (verified algebraically); D2 uses a segment-lagged $\hat{v}$; C5d added; $\text{Disp}^{D2}$ anchored at $\hat{v}(t_0)$" and "D1_rel against scale and weight-decay drift; $\Delta\theta$ logged before and after decay") — Asymmetric Treatment of Decoupled Weight Decay, Preconditioner Scaling, Inverse-Curvature Downweighting, Segment-Boundary Leakage, and Layer-Norm Weighting Between $D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2$ and $B_{\text{strong}}$:**
   - **Problem:** Comparing the logged step quantities in §3 (page 6), the candidate distances in §5.1 (page 8), the competitor set $B_{\text{strong}}$ in §7 (page 10), and the revision log in §19 reveals five specification asymmetries that represent a critical concern for the identification audit:
     1. *Inconsistent inclusion of decoupled weight decay:* In §3, $\|\Delta\theta_\tau\|_2$ is logged both *before* and *after* the decoupled weight-decay step ($-\eta_\tau \lambda \theta(\tau)$). In §5.1, $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ explicitly uses $\Delta\theta(\tau)$ **before** the decoupled weight-decay term because "AdamW shrinks $\theta$ at every step independently of the data," whereas $D_1 = \|\theta(\tau+1) - \theta(\tau)\|_2$ uses the full post-decay update $\Delta\theta_{\text{post}}(\tau) = -\eta_\tau \hat{m}_\tau / (\sqrt{\hat{v}_\tau} + \varepsilon) - \eta_\tau \lambda \theta(\tau)$, and $D_2 = \left(\sum_i \hat{v}_i(s)(\Delta\theta_i(\tau))^2\right)^{1/2}$, $C_{5\text{c}}$ ($\sum |\langle \tilde{g}_\tau, \Delta\theta_\tau\rangle|$), and $C_6$ (windowed mean/SD of $\|\Delta\theta_\tau\|_2$) do not specify whether they use the pre-decay or post-decay $\Delta\theta_\tau$. If post-decay $\Delta\theta_i(\tau)$ is used in $D_2$, then during low-gradient or already-mastered re-exposure segments (e.g., in F2) where $\hat{m}_i(\tau) \approx 0$ and $C_{5\text{d}}$ ($\sum \eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$) vanishes, $D_2$ continues to accumulate a pure weight-decay norm term $\eta_\tau \lambda \left(\sum_i \hat{v}_i(s) \theta_i(\tau)^2\right)^{1/2}$ that tracks data-independent shrinkage. Meanwhile, in $B_{\text{strong}}$ (§7), all cumulative optimizer competitors ($C_5, C_{5\text{a}}\text{--}C_{5\text{d}}$) exclude decoupled weight decay, and $C_{6\text{b}}$ records only the net endpoint difference $\Delta\|\theta\|_2 = \|\theta(t_1)\|_2 - \|\theta(t_0)\|_2$, omitting the cumulative weight-decay path length $\sum_\tau \eta_\tau \lambda \|\theta(\tau)\|_2$.
     2. *Incomplete preconditioner cancellation, inverse-curvature downweighting, and missing scalar preconditioner controls:* Section 5.1 notes that under Adam, $\Delta\theta_i \approx -\eta \hat{m}_i / \sqrt{\hat{v}_i}$, so a same-step $D_2$ reduces to $\eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$ ($C_{5\text{d}}$). However, substituting the actual pre-decay AdamW step into segment-lagged $D_2$ gives:
        $$D_2(\tau) = \eta_\tau \sqrt{\sum_i \frac{\hat{v}_i(s)}{\left(\sqrt{\hat{v}_i(\tau)} + \varepsilon\right)^2} \hat{m}_{i,\tau}^2}.$$
        Even at $s = \tau$, if $\Delta\theta_i(\tau)$ in $D_2$ includes decoupled weight decay or omits $\varepsilon$ in the numerator factor, $D_2(\tau)$ mixes weight-decay cross-terms and $\varepsilon$-floor deviations absent from $C_{5\text{d}}$. Furthermore, because $\beta_2 = 0.999$ has a half-life of $\approx 693$ steps and segments are multiples of 50 steps (§4.3), $\hat{v}_i(s)/\hat{v}_i(\tau) \approx 1$ across most steps ($0.999^{50} \approx 0.951$), making $D_2(\tau)$ nearly collinear with $C_{5\text{d}}$. Worse, when a new task causes gradients on active coordinates to surge, $\hat{v}_i(\tau)$ increases relative to $\hat{v}_i(s)$, so the ratio $\hat{v}_i(s)/\hat{v}_i(\tau) < 1$ systematically *downweights* the parameters currently undergoing large updates, while parameters with zero gradient in the current segment decay as $\hat{v}_i(\tau) = 0.999^{\tau-s}\hat{v}_i(s)$ and receive an increasing weight $0.999^{-(\tau-s)} > 1$ that acts as a proxy for steps elapsed since the segment boundary $(\tau - s)$. Meanwhile, $D_1(\tau)$ divides by $\sqrt{\hat{v}_i(\tau)} + \varepsilon$ and $D_{1,\text{rel}}(\tau)$ divides by $\|\theta^{(\ell)}(\tau)\|_2$, yet **no interval sum in $B_{\text{strong}}$ incorporates $\hat{v}_\tau$ or $\|\theta_\tau\|_2$**, and §7 omits the unweighted cumulative momentum norm $\sum \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$ even though §3 logs $\|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$ per step.
     3. *Privileged access to segment boundaries in $D_2$:* Freezing $\hat{v}(s)$ at the start $s$ of each segment injects exact segment-boundary timing into $D_2$ (which is unavailable in boundary-free streams such as Stage C).
     4. *Ambiguity of layer index $\ell$ in $D_{1,\text{rel}}$:* In $D_{1,\text{rel}}(\tau) = \bigl(\sum_\ell \|\Delta\theta^{(\ell)}(\tau)\|_2^2 / (\|\theta^{(\ell)}(\tau)\|_2^2 + 10^{-12})\bigr)^{1/2}$, §5.1 does not define what $\ell$ indexes. Because the §3 agent includes biases in all linear layers and LayerNorms, if $\ell$ indexes individual tensors in `model.parameters()`, a 128-element bias or LayerNorm gain vector receives the same unit relative-norm weight as a $128 \times 512$ ($65{,}536$-element) MLP weight matrix, causing 1D vectors to dominate $D_{1,\text{rel}}$.
     5. *Boundary between telemetry ($C_6$) and experiential index ($D_1$), plus missing step-level logs in §3:* Section 3 logs $\|\Delta\theta_\tau\|_2$ per step and §7 uses it inside $C_6$ as a cheap online competitor, whereas §5.1 defines the running sum of $\|\Delta\theta_\tau\|_2$ as the candidate experiential index $c_e^{D_1}$. Conversely, $B_{\text{strong}}$ (§7) requires $|\langle \tilde{g}_\tau, \Delta\theta_\tau \rangle|$ (in $C_{5\text{c}}$) and $\sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}(t)}$ (in $X_{\text{cov}}$ and $C_{6\text{b}}$), which are omitted from the "Logged per step" row of the §3 table (page 6).
   - **Why it matters:** If $D_1$, $D_{1,\text{rel}}$, or $D_2$ beats $B_{\text{strong}}$ in $H_1$, it will be unclear whether the advantage reflects genuine trajectory information or simply (a) cumulative weight-decay shrinkage during low-gradient re-exposures, (b) dividing $C_{5\text{d}}$ by the running global parameter norm $\|\theta(\tau)\|_2$ or global preconditioner $\sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_\tau}$ (or tracking within-segment $\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}$ growth and segment-boundary resets $0.999^{-(\tau-s)}$), or (c) over-weighting 1D bias/LayerNorm tensors in $D_{1,\text{rel}}$.
   - **Concrete fix:**
     1. In §3 ("Logged per step"), add $|\langle \tilde{g}_\tau, \Delta\theta_\tau \rangle|$ (pre- and post-decay) and $\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_\tau$. In §5.1 and §7 ($C_{5\text{c}}, C_6$), state explicitly that $\Delta\theta_\tau$ in $D_{1,\text{rel}}$, $D_2$, $C_{5\text{c}}$, and $C_6$ (plus a pre-decay variant of $D_1$) uses the **pre-weight-decay** update $\Delta\theta^{\text{pre}}(\tau)$ (and if post-decay $D_1$ is retained, include both pre- and post-decay versions in $C_6$).
     2. In §5.1, define $D_2$ on the pre-decay step with exact denominator matching and a boundary-free lagged EMA $\hat{v}_{\text{lag}}(\tau) = \hat{v}(\tau - L)$ ($L = 200$ steps, or childhood-end $\hat{v}(t_{\text{childhood}})$):
        $$D_2(\tau) = \left( \sum_i \big(\sqrt{\hat{v}_{i,\text{lag}}(\tau)} + \varepsilon\big)^2 \big(\Delta\theta_i^{\text{pre}}(\tau)\big)^2 \right)^{1/2},$$
        so that at zero lag it reduces algebraically to $\eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$ ($C_{5\text{d}}$). In §5.1 ($D_{1,\text{rel}}$), specify that $\ell$ indexes module-level grouped parameters (or weights each tensor by its parameter fraction $p_\ell / P$, excluding 1D LayerNorm/bias vectors from separate $1/\|\theta^{(\ell)}\|_2^2$ weighting), and state in §3 whether weight decay $\lambda = 0.01$ applies to all tensors or only 2D weights.
     3. In §7 ($B_{\text{strong}}$), add the zero-storage running scalar accumulators (and their $\Delta N$-normalised rates) computed from already-logged norms: unweighted momentum $\sum_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2$, cumulative weight-decay path norm $\sum_\tau \eta_\tau \lambda \|\theta(\tau)\|_2$, globally norm-scaled momentum $\sum_\tau \eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2 / (\|\theta(\tau)\|_2 + 10^{-12})$, scalar-preconditioned momentum $\sum_\tau \eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2 / (\sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_\tau / P} + \varepsilon)$, and scalar curvature-lag ratio $\sum_\tau \eta_\tau \|\hat{m}_\tau\|_2 \sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_{\text{lag}}(\tau) / (\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}(\tau) + 10^{-12})}$.

2. **[MAJOR] §4.3 (Run Structure), §5.1 (Candidate Distances), §5.2 (Net Displacement), & §7 ($C_{7\text{a}}, C_{8\text{b}}$) — Metric-Anchor Mismatch Between Path Index $c_e^{D_2}$ (and $c_e^{D_{1,\text{rel}}}$) and Net Displacement $\text{Disp}^{D_2}$ (and $\text{Disp}^{D_{1,\text{rel}}}$), Plus Unspecified Segment-Boundary Rules:**
   - **Problem:** A substantive methodological concern arises in comparing $c_e^D$ against $\text{Disp}^D(t_0, t_1)$ in §5.1 and §5.2 (page 8), alongside an operational ambiguity in how segment boundaries $s$ are defined:
     1. *Metric-anchor mismatch in $D_2$ and $D_{1,\text{rel}}$:* In §5.1, $c_e^{D_2}$ weights each step $\tau$ by $\hat{v}(s)$, the second moment frozen at the start $s$ of the *current* segment (which resets discontinuously at every segment boundary inside $(t_0, t_1]$), and $c_e^{D_{1,\text{rel}}}$ normalizes each step by the current layer norm $\|\theta^{(\ell)}(\tau)\|_2$. In contrast, §5.2 and §7 ($C_{8\text{b}}$) define net displacement $\text{Disp}^{D_2}(t_0, t_1) = \bigl(\sum_i \hat{v}_i(t_0)(\theta_i(t_1) - \theta_i(t_0))^2\bigr)^{1/2}$ with the curvature metric anchored strictly at $\hat{v}(t_0)$—the end of task $k$'s exposure—and $\text{Disp}^{D_{1,\text{rel}}}(t_0, t_1)$ caches layer norms at $t_0$.
     2. *Missing chunked formulas for $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ and $D_2$:* In §5.1 ("Chunking control"), chunked versions of $D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}$, and $D_2$ at stride $m_{\text{ch}}$ are computed alongside per-step versions, but the exact formula for chunked $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ and $D_2$ over $[\tau, \tau + m_{\text{ch}}]$ (whether pre-decay updates are summed over the chunk and which step's $\|\theta^{(\ell)}\|_2$ or $\hat{v}$ weights the chunk) is not written out.
     3. *Agent-observable segment boundaries ($s$ in $D_2$ and $C_{7\text{a}}$):* Section 5.1 freezes $\hat{v}(s)$ at *"the start $s$ of the current segment"*, and $C_{7\text{a}}$ (§7) counts *"number of segment boundaries and of agent-triggered rehearsals"*, while moving oracle segment labels to $C_{7\text{b}}$. However, §§4.3–4.4 and §5.1 do not specify how the agent detects a "segment boundary" $s$ online without oracle metadata (e.g., when consecutive segments in $F_2$ or $F_6$ train the same task or distractor pool, when a 50-step rehearsal segment is interleaved, or when a warm restart occurs at a random time in $F_3$).
   - **Why it matters:** Because $\hat{v}(t_0)$ captures task $k$'s specific parameter sensitivity immediately after training task $k$ (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017, doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114), whereas $\Delta c_e^{D_2}$ weights intervening updates by the rolling curvatures $\hat{v}(s)$ of subsequent, unrelated segments, $\Delta c_e^{D_2}(t_0, t_1)$ is not the path-length sum of the metric used in $\text{Disp}^{D_2}(t_0, t_1)$ and does not satisfy the triangle inequality $\Delta c_e^{D_2}(t_0, t_1) \ge \text{Disp}^{D_2}(t_0, t_1)$. If $D^* = D_2$, testing $Q_2$/$H_2$ ($\Delta R^2(c_e) \ge 0.80 \cdot \Delta R^2(\text{Disp}^{D^*})$) conflates path-vs.-displacement compression with task-$k$-specific curvature anchoring ($\hat{v}(t_0)$) versus evolving segment-lagged curvature ($\hat{v}(s)$). Furthermore, if segment boundaries $s$ rely on generator metadata rather than observable token/schedule changes, $D_2$ and $C_{7\text{a}}$ leak world metadata.
   - **Concrete fix:**

#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **[FATAL] §4.4 (Stream Families), §10.2 (Confirmatory hypotheses, H1), §11 (Gate G4b), §12 (Data splits and sample size), & §19 ("F4 not pooled; adversarial F6 added...; Power formulas with $z_{1-\beta/2}$ for TOST...") — Contradictory Pooling Specification, Out-of-Distribution Confounding and Treatment Dilution of H1 by F1 and F6, Unspecified Distractor `[TASK]` Tokens, Underpowered Standalone F5 Gate, and Sample-Size Allocation Conflict.**

     1. *Contradiction between §4.4 and §10.2 on pooling F1, F5, and F6:* In the §4.4 table ("Role" column), only $F_2$ and $F_3$ are assigned the Role *"Pooled"*, whereas $F_1$ is *"Positive control: counts are expected to suffice"*, $F_4$ is *"Sanity check only; not pooled into H1"*, $O$ is *"H3 only; not pooled into H1, H2, H4"*, and both `F5, held-out` and `F6, adversarial` explicitly state: *"Never used for fitting or selection; test only; reported alone"*.      2. *Algebraic Inconsistency in Run Allocation (§12):* §12 sets the total test sample size for H1 to $n = \max(n_{\text{TOST}}, n_{\text{MET}}) = \lceil 10.51 (\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}/\delta)^2 \rceil$, and mandates at least $n_{\text{MET}} = \lceil 7.85 (\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}/\delta)^2 \rceil$ runs *each* in F5 and in F6 so that their standalone reports have $0.80$ power for the Minimal-Effect Test (MET).      3. *Signal Dilution, Sign Cancellation, and Out-of-Distribution Extrapolation on F6:* By construction (§4.4, §15), F1 is a stationary positive control where step counts suffice ($\mathbb{E}[\Delta R^2_{\text{F1}}] \approx 0$), and F6 is an out-of-distribution adversarial regime (*"High parameter change with little interference: favours the competitors"*, never seen during training on F1–F3) where distractor segments generate large $\Delta c_e^{D^*}$ (for $D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2$) with $+1$ monotonicity enforced in GBDT when true target forgetting $T_1$ is near zero ($\mathbb{E}[\Delta R^2_{\text{F6}}] < 0$, as anticipated in §15 row 3). Moreover, $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}$ (§12) and Gate G4b (§11) are calibrated on F1–F3 only, where F6 is absent.
     4. *Missing `[TASK]` token specification for F6 distractors (§4.1, §4.4):* Section 4.1 allocates 64 `[TASK_k]` tokens for $\approx 40$ target tasks and the reference tasks.      5. *Underpowered Standalone F5 Gate (§12, §15):* In §15 (rows 1–2), claiming general empirical support for Level 2 requires **both** *"H1 supported, G2 and G5 passed, and F5 supported alone"*; if F5 is not supported alone, the result is downgraded to *"a construction effect, not a general result."* Allocating $n_{\text{MET}} = \lceil 7.85 (\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}/\delta)^2 \rceil$ runs to F5 achieves 0.80 power for MET (lower 97.5% CI bound $> \delta$) only if the true effect in F5 is at least **$2\delta$** (at a true effect of $1.5\delta$, power is only $\approx 0.29$), and requiring two independent 80%-powered tests (H1 and F5) to pass reduces joint power to $\approx 0.64$.

3. **[FATAL] §9 (Stage B: Pacing and Schedulers), cross-checked with §19 ("Stage B: hard budget cap, pacing, terminal flush...") — Inverted feedback ratio in the online pacing rule $\theta_{\text{eff}}(N)$, unspecified calibration rule for $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ and $\varepsilon$, and non-resetting burst triggers in `S-pred`.**
   * **Problem:** Three mechanical issues affect the rehearsal trigger rules in §9:
     1. *Inverted Pacing Ratio:* In §9 ("Pacing"), threshold schedulers trigger a rehearsal when their monitored statistic exceeds $\theta_{\text{eff}}(N)$, defined as:
        $$\theta_{\text{eff}}(N) = \theta_{\text{cal}} \cdot \frac{(K_{\text{reh}} - k_{\text{spent}}(N)) / K_{\text{reh}}}{\max(\varepsilon, (N_{\text{life}} - N) / N_{\text{life}})}.$$
        Suppose early in a run a high-change segment causes a scheduler to spend rehearsals *faster* than the uniform clock rate, so the remaining budget fraction $(K_{\text{reh}} - k_{\text{spent}}(N)) / K_{\text{reh}}$ drops *below* the remaining life fraction $(N_{\text{life}} - N) / N_{\text{life}}$. Under the formula as written, the ratio is **$< 1$**, which **lowers** $\theta_{\text{eff}}(N)$, making it *even easier* to trigger the next rehearsal. This creates a runaway positive feedback loop where spending budget lowers the threshold, triggering back-to-back rehearsals until $k_{\text{spent}}(N) \to K_{\text{reh}}$ and $\theta_{\text{eff}}(N) \to 0$. Conversely, if a scheduler under-spends early, the ratio exceeds $1$, **raising** $\theta_{\text{eff}}(N)$ so it becomes harder to trigger until the terminal flush dumps all remaining rehearsals at the end of life.
     2. *Resetting vs. Non-Resetting Trigger in `S-pred`:* For `S-c_e` and `S-C*`, the trigger statistic is the cumulative increment $\Delta c_e^{D^*}$ (or $\Delta C^*$) *since the last rehearsal*, which resets to $0$ after every rehearsal segment. By contrast, for `S-pred(B_strong)` and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)`, the rule is: *"score every task; rehearse the argmax when the predicted maximum $\ge \theta_{\text{eff}}$."* Rehearsing the single argmax task $k^*$ resets only $\hat{F}_{k^*}$, while the second-most-forgotten task $k_{(2)}$ (and up to 38 other active tasks) was not rehearsed and still has $\hat{F}_{k_{(2)}} \approx \max_k \hat{F}_k \ge \theta_{\text{eff}}$ at the very next check, causing `S-pred` to fire rehearsals in consecutive bursts.
     3. *Unspecified calibration objective for $\theta_{\text{cal}}$, $\varepsilon$, and evaluation cadence:* Section 9 does not state the value of $\varepsilon > 0$, the step cadence at which triggers are checked (given that §4.3 requires all segments to lie on a 50-step grid and §6 restricts Stage A training triplets to $\Delta N \ge 50$), nor the mathematical criterion used to calibrate $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ on validation runs. If $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ is set slightly too high for a competitor trigger, it will hoard rehearsals and dump them in a terminal flush at the end of the run, artificially depressing its time-averaged accuracy T3.
   * **Why it matters:** Runaway burst firing or premature budget exhaustion/terminal hoarding is a feedback-loop artefact of the inverted pacing formula and cross-task maximum rather than a property of $c_e$, confounding both H4a and H4b even when Gate G7 ("realised rehearsal steps = Q") passes via terminal flush.
   * **Concrete Fix:**
     * **Invert the pacing multiplier in §9** so that spending ahead of schedule *raises* the trigger threshold (conserving budget) and falling behind schedule *lowers* it, and specify $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2 K_{\text{reh}}}$ (or $\varepsilon = 0.01$):
       $$\theta_{\text{eff}}(N) = \theta_{\text{cal}} \cdot \frac{(N_{\text{life}} - N) / N_{\text{life}}}{\max\!\big(\varepsilon,\; (K_{\text{reh}} - k_{\text{spent}}(N)) / K_{\text{reh}}\big)}.$$
     * Specify that triggers are evaluated on the **50-step segment boundary grid** (matching $L_{\text{reh}} = 50$ and $\Delta N \ge 50$), and define $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ *independently for each threshold scheduler* on the 15 validation runs to minimise the L1 deviation between cumulative rehearsal expenditure $k_{\text{spent}}(N)/K_{\text{reh}}$ and uniform pacing $N/N_{\text{life}}$ such that mean cumulative rehearsals spent by $N = 0.95 N_{\text{life}}$ equals $0.95 K_{\text{reh}}$ (requiring terminal-flush segments to average $\le 5\%$ of $K_{\text{reh}}$, logged per run in Gate G7).
     * For `S-pred(B_strong)` and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)`, trigger rehearsal when the **increase in mean (or total) predicted forgetting across active tasks since the last rehearsal** reaches $\theta_{\text{eff}}(N)$ (which resets to $0$ after every rehearsal, matching `S-c_e`), and then select the argmax task $\arg\max_{k:\, \Delta N_k \ge 50} \hat{F}_k$ to rehearse.

   * **Concrete Fix:** Specify in §7 (Ceiling), §10.1, and §11 (G1) that $r_{AB,r}$ is the **episode-weighted** correlation computed with the exact weights $w = 1 / (E_r \cdot M_{r,k,t_0})$ from §6 (or define the ceiling directly in weighted $R^2$ units as $R^2_{\text{ceil},r} = 1 - \frac{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i \in r} w_i (F_{A,i} - F_{B,i})^2}{\sum_{i \in r} w_i (F_{A,i} - \bar{F}_{A,r,w})^2}$), and state explicitly in §10.1 that $\bar{r}_{AB}$ and $\bar{R}^2_{\text{cal}}(B_{\text{strong}})$ in the SESOI formula are averaged over the F2–F3 calibration runs (matching Gate G3).

7. **[MAJOR] §10.2 (Confirmatory hypotheses, H2), §11 (Gate G2b), & §12 (Sample size for H2, H4a, H4b), cross-checked with §19 ("H2 as the linear contrast $Z_r$ with denominator gate G2b and an equivalence bound") — Overlapping Decision Regions, Conflation of Non-Inferiority with Point-Equivalence, and Unscaled Sample-Size Margin for H2.**
   * **Problem:** In §10.2 (Table row H2), H2 tests $\bar{Z}$, the mean of $Z_r = \Delta R^2_r(c_e) - 0.80 \cdot \Delta R^2_r(\text{Disp}^{D^*})$, with the decision rule: *"Supported if the lower bound of $\bar{Z}$ is $\ge 0$; equivalent (retention indistinguishable from 80%) if the interval lies within $\pm 0.01$."* Three statistical issues arise:
     1. *Overlapping Decision Outcomes:* If the BCa confidence interval for $\bar{Z}$ lies entirely inside $[0,\, 0.01)$ (for example, $[0.002,\, 0.008]$), its lower bound is $\ge 0$ **and** the entire interval lies within $(-0.01, +0.01)$, satisfying both *supported* and *equivalent* simultaneously.
     2. *Conflation of non-inferiority ($\ge 80\%$) with point-equivalence ($= 80\%$):* Because the 20% non-inferiority margin is *already built into* the linear contrast $Z_r = \Delta R^2_r(c_e) - 0.80 \cdot \Delta R^2_r(\text{Disp}^{D^*})$, $\bar{Z} \ge 0$ already means $c_e$ is non-inferior to $\text{Disp}^{D^*}$ at the 80% retention threshold. Conversely, when $c_e$ retains clearly *less* than 80% (upper confidence bound of $\bar{Z} < 0$), it falls outside $(-0.01, +0.01)$ and has no negative/refuted label in §10.1–§10.2, while an interval inside $(-0.01, +0.01)$ merely means retention happens to sit right on the 80% boundary.
     3. *Mismatched Margin $\delta$ in the H2 Sample-Size Formula:* In §12 ("Sample size for H2, H4a, H4b"), the required sample size is written as $n = \lceil (2.394 + z_{0.90})^2 (\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}/\delta)^2 \rceil = \lceil 13.5 (\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}/\delta)^2 \rceil$, plugging in H1's $\delta \in [0.02, 0.05]$ rather than H2's margin $\delta_{\text{H2}} = 0.01$ and $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}(Z_r)$. Because $\delta_{\text{H2}} = 0.01$ is $2\times$ to $5\times$ smaller than $\delta$, TOST on H2 within $\pm 0.01$ requires $(\delta / 0.01)^2 \in [4, 25]$ times as many runs as the formula with $\delta$ prescribes. Additionally, for H4a and H4b, $13.5$ uses $z_{0.90}$ (the TOST formula for power $0.80$ at true difference $0$), whereas the one-sided MET at $2\delta_B$ uses $(2.394 + z_{0.80})^2 = 10.47$.
   * **Why it matters:** A confidence interval inside $[0, 0.01)$ produces a contradictory dual decision on H2, and powering H2 with $\delta$ instead of $\delta_{\text{H2}}$ leaves H2 severely underpowered.
   * **Concrete Fix:**
     1. In §10.2 (H2), make the decision regions mutually exclusive and aligned with non-inferiority: define H2 as **supported (retains $\ge 80\%$)** if the lower bound of $\bar{Z}$ is $\ge 0$ (or $> -0.005$); **refuted (retains $< 80\%$)** if the upper bound of $\bar{Z}$ is $< 0$; and **inconclusive** if the CI spans $0$ (or, if retaining a $\pm 0.01$ boundary zone, require *equivalent to 80% retention* to have lower bound $< 0$ and the full interval inside $(-0.01, +0.01)$).
     2. In §12, state explicitly that $n_{\text{H2}}$ uses $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}(Z_r)$ from calibration runs and the H2 margin (scaling the margin proportionally as $\delta_{\text{H2}} = \max(0.01, 0.20 \cdot \overline{\Delta R^2}_{\text{cal}}(\text{Disp}^{D^*}))$ and raising Gate G2b's lower bound in §11 from $0.02$ to $0.05$ so that $0.20 \times 0.05 = 0.01$), while H4a/H4b use $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{up}}(\Delta T3)$ and $\delta_B = 0.02$ across paired Stage B seeds.

8. **[MINOR] §8 (Prediction models, Equal tuning budget & Fitting) & §11 (G3b) — Missing hyperparameter search space for `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` and group-level CV leakage during the two-sample ridge bootstrap.**
   * **Problem:**
     1. *Unspecified Hyperparameter Search Space:* Section 8 specifies `max_features = 1.0, max_bins = 255` and *"every predictor set receives the same random search, 50 configurations, on validation runs, with the same seeds"*, and Gate G3b (§11) doubles this search to 100 configurations. ##### 3. [MAJOR] §13 (Stage C: transfer to pretrained language models): Underspecified confirmatory decision unit, resampling unit under fixed pretrained weights, resolution rule between LoRA and full fine-tuning, and mathematical definitions for LoRA D1/D2 and top-K D3

* **Section:** §13 (page 16).

* **Problem:** While §13 places Stage C outside this preregistration and into a separate adaptation document, §13 establishes a binding cross-stage generalisation rule right here: *"The Stage A conclusions are claimed to generalise only if Stage C reproduces the three-way outcome of H1"* (echoed in §15, final row). For that reproduction criterion to be well-defined and free of post-hoc degrees of freedom when drafting the adaptation document, four structural specifications are currently missing:
  1. **Confirmatory decision unit and disagreement resolution:** §13 refers to *"the Pythia family"*, states that *"the confirmatory model size and optimizer arm are named in the adaptation document"*, and also includes a separate bullet on **LoRA** ($\Delta W_{\text{eff}}$).   2. **Resampling unit under a deterministic pretrained checkpoint:** In Stage A, each run $r$ has an independent random weight initialisation $\theta(0)$ and randomly generated synthetic tasks. In Stage C, a pretrained Pythia checkpoint (`Biderman et al., 2023`) has a single fixed weight initialisation $\theta_{\text{pre}}$, and real-text corpora have a finite set of domains. If runs only vary mini-batch order over the same domain sequence, between-run variance of $\Delta R^2_r$ will collapse and violate the independent-run assumption of the BCa bootstrap in both MET and TOST.
  3. **Computational and mathematical specification of LoRA $\Delta W_{\text{eff}}$ and $D_2$:** §13 specifies computing $D$ on the effective weight update $\Delta W_{\text{eff}} = (\alpha/r)(B_{\tau+1}A_{\tau+1} - B_\tau A_\tau)$ rather than on the concatenated adapter matrices. Naively multiplying $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r}$ and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\text{in}}}$ at every step reconstructs dense $d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}$ matrices in $O(d_{\text{out}} d_{\text{in}})$ memory and FLOPs, defeating LoRA's compute efficiency on CPU. Furthermore, **$D_2$** requires element-wise weighting by the second-moment tensor $\hat{v}_i(s)$, which **does not exist** for the dense matrix $W_{\text{eff}}$ under LoRA because AdamW only maintains moments $\hat{v}_A, \hat{v}_B$ for the low-rank factors $A$ and $B$.
  4. **Metric preservation for top-$K$ / sketched $D_3$ and loss-based inclusion:** §13 states that $D_3$ is restricted to *"top-$K$ or sketched distributions; the vocabulary of $\approx 50,000$ tokens makes full distributions infeasible."* Truncating a 50,000-token softmax distribution to top-$K$ without putting the residual probability mass into a tail bucket on a common support violates the probability simplex and destroys the metric / triangle-inequality property of $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ relied upon in §5.1. Similarly, replacing $\text{Acc}_{B,k}(t_0) \ge 0.90$ with an unspecified *"loss-based learned criterion"* introduces post-exposure selection ambiguity unless anchored to a deterministic cross-entropy reduction threshold on domain half B.

* **Why it matters:** Without locking the confirmatory decision unit, the resolution rule between LoRA and full fine-tuning, the source of run-level stochasticity, and the mathematical form of LoRA $D_1/D_2$ and top-$K$ $D_3$, the Stage C transfer contract in §13 is under-determined.

* **Concrete Fix:** Expand §13 to require that the Stage C adaptation document freeze the following exact specifications before any Stage C run:
  1. **Confirmatory decision unit and disambiguation rule:** Designate **one primary confirmatory arm** in Stage C: a single named Pythia checkpoint (e.g., `Pythia-160M` or `Pythia-70M`, fixed strictly by the CPU timing check before seeing any forgetting outcomes) under **LoRA ($r=16, \alpha=32$) with AdamW** (directly testing the FOREVER regime of §0.3), with full fine-tuning on the smallest feasible Pythia model treated as a secondary generalisation arm (or, if both LoRA and full fine-tuning are declared co-primary, tested as a 2-arm Holm family at one-sided $\alpha_{\text{adj}} = 0.0125$). If both are run and yield discordant three-way outcomes (e.g., supported in LoRA, equivalent in full fine-tuning), Stage C must be reported as *regime-conditional transfer (adapter-only or full-parameter-only)* rather than unconditional reproduction.
  2. **Resampling unit ($r$):** Define a Stage C *run* $r$ as an independent random seed controlling: (i) the draw and ordering of domain segments from a pool of $\ge 15$ distinct corpus domains, (ii) disjoint document subsamples drawn from each domain pool for training and probes (halves A and B), (iii) the stream schedule parameters (LR restarts, batch sizes, re-exposure timings), and (iv) the initialization seed of LoRA adapter matrix $A$.
  3. **Exact $O(r^2(d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}}))$ LoRA distance algebra:** For $D_1$ and $D_{1,\text{rel}}$, compute $\|\Delta W_{\text{eff}}^{(\ell)}\|_F^2 = (\alpha/r)^2 \, \text{Tr}(\tilde{B}^\top \tilde{B} \tilde{A} \tilde{A}^\top)$ via the rank-$2r$ factors $\tilde{B} = [B_{\tau+1}, -B_\tau] \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times 2r}$ and $\tilde{A} = [A_{\tau+1}; A_\tau] \in \mathbb{R}^{2r \times d_{\text{in}}}$ in $O(r^2(d_{\text{in}} + d_{\text{out}}))$ flops without materialising $d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}$ matrices (normalised for $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ by $\|W_0^{(\ell)} + (\alpha/r)B_\tau A_\tau\|_F$, updated via low-rank trace cross-terms with precomputed $W_0^\top B_\tau$). For $D_2$ and $C_{5d}$ under LoRA, define them strictly in the trainable adapter coordinate space $\Theta_{\text{LoRA}} = \text{concat}(\text{vec}(A), \text{vec}(B))$ where AdamW's $\hat{v}(s)$ and $\hat{m}_\tau$ natively reside.
  4. **Metric-preserving top-$K$ $D_3$:** At each fixed target token position on $R$, compute $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ over the union of the top-$K$ ($K=128$) vocabulary indices at $\tau$ and $\tau + m_{\text{ch}}$ (or anchored at $t=0$) plus a single aggregated tail bucket $p_{\text{tail}} = 1 - \sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{top}}} p(v|x)$, ensuring every evaluated distribution is a valid probability vector on a common support of size $\le 2K+1$ so that $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ remains an exact metric satisfying the triangle inequality.
  5. **Inclusion criterion and covariates:** Replace accuracy inclusion with a deterministic relative cross-entropy reduction threshold on domain half B over the exposure segment ending at $s_0$ (or relative to $S(0)$): $(\bar{L}_{B,k}(t_{\text{start}}) - \bar{L}_{B,k}(t_0)) / \bar{L}_{B,k}(t_{\text{start}}) \ge \gamma_{\text{inc}}$ (with $\gamma_{\text{inc}}$ calibrated in Stage C Stage-0 so that $\bar{r}_{AB} \ge 0.70$), and compute input-token overlap in $X_{\text{cov}}$ over a fixed top-$4,096$ frequent token histogram (plus 1 tail bin) rather than all $50,304$ BPE tokens.

##### 4. [MAJOR] §14 & §12a (Compute estimate and feasibility): Arithmetic inconsistency between the 40 min/run limit and 150 CPU-hour Stage A cap, 3–4$\times$ underestimation of Stage B CPU-hours, and unthrottled Stage B `S-pred` / Stage A snapshot overheads

* **Section:** §14 (pages 16–17) and §12a (page 16), in relation to §§5, 7, 8, 9, and 12.

* **Problem:** A quantitative audit of the forward-pass counts, memory footprint, and run totals reveals three potential concerns in §14 and §12a:
  1. **Stage A run-count omissions and inconsistency between §12a's two limits:** §14 estimates Stage A at *"$\le 200$ test runs + $\approx 70$ calibration, training and validation runs"* ($\le 270$ runs $\rightarrow \approx 50\text{ CPU-hours}$ at $11\text{ min/run}$). Furthermore, in §12a, the two go/no-go cost limits (*"$\le 40\text{ min}$ single-threaded"* per run and *"projected Stage A $\le 150\text{ CPU-hours}$"*) are arithmetically incompatible at full sample size: if a run takes $30\text{–}40\text{ min}$, 325 runs require **$162\text{–}217\text{ CPU-hours}$** (plus $\approx 5\text{–}8\text{ CPU-hours}$ to fit $>3,000$ 5-fold cross-fitted GBDT configurations across $B_{\text{standard}}$, $B_{\text{strong}}$, G3b's 100 configs, 5 candidate distances $D$, $\text{Disp}^D$, G4 placebos, G4b, and G6, plus the ridge two-sample bootstrap), violating the $150\text{ CPU-hour}$ cap. For 325 runs plus model fitting to fit within $150\text{ CPU-hours}$, the single-threaded run time cannot exceed **$\approx 26\text{ min}$**.
  2. **Probe evaluation, $D_3/D_4$, and $C_9$ forward/backward pass and RAM arithmetic:**
     * *Training:* $23,000\text{ steps} \times 19.9\text{ ms} = 458\text{ s} \approx 7.6\text{ min}$, matching §14's $\approx 8\text{ min}$.
     * *Probes:* Evaluations occur at the end of every segment ($\approx 40\text{–}80$ segments) and on a 200-step grid ($100$ grid points in 20,000 steps), giving $\approx 120\text{–}160$ evaluations per run. Across an average of $20$ active learned tasks (growing from $1$ to $40$), plus reference battery $R$ (needed at every $t_0, t_1$ for C10) and behavioural battery $B_{\text{beh}}$ (needed for T2), each with 256 items ($8$ batches of 32), one run executes $\approx 130 \times (20 + 2) \times 8 = 22,880$ forward batches of 32 items. Since a forward-only pass takes $\approx 1/3$ of a training step ($\approx 6.6\text{ ms}$ per batch of $32 \times 4\text{–}6$ tokens), probe evaluation takes $22,880 \times 6.6\text{ ms} \approx \mathbf{2.5\text{ min}}$, **provided** probe items are evaluated in contiguous batches of $\ge 32$ with `torch.inference_mode()`. If evaluated per-task without batching or with small batches, Python dispatch overhead will push this to $> 10\text{ min}$.
     * *$D_3$ and $D_4$:* At $m_{\text{ch}} = 10$ over $23,000$ steps ($2,300$ chunks $\times 8$ batches of 32), $D_3$ (and $D_4$ representations extracted from the same forward pass) requires $18,400$ forward batches $\times 6.6\text{ ms} \approx \mathbf{2.0\text{ min}}$ (plus $588,800$ 512-way $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ evaluations). If Stage 0 triggers the fallback $m_{\text{ch}} = 5$ (§5.1), this doubles to $\approx 4.0\text{ min}$; on the 5 calibration runs where $m_{\text{ch}} = 1$ is tested over a 2,000-step window (§5.1), that window adds $16,000$ forward batches ($\approx 1.8\text{ min}$).
     * *C9 and displacement snapshot memory contention:* Simultaneously caching active snapshots across $K=40$ tasks for $\text{Disp}^{D1}$ ($0.93\text{M}$ floats), $\text{Disp}^{D2}$/C8b ($1.9\text{M}$ floats), $\text{Disp}^{D3}$ ($0.13\text{M}$), $\text{Disp}^{D4}$ ($0.03\text{M}$), and C9 ($\nabla_\theta L_{\text{train},k}(S(t_0))$, $0.93\text{M}$ floats, which also requires 8 backward passes per exposure segment end $t_0$ and $\approx 2,400$ dot products of length $0.93\text{M}$ at evaluation points $t_1$) requires $40 \times 3.92\text{M} \times 4\text{ bytes} \approx \mathbf{627\text{ MB}}$ of RAM per worker, or **$\approx 5.0\text{ GB}$ across 8 workers**. Furthermore, §14 benchmarks 1 process with 8 threads ($17.3\text{ ms}$) vs. 1 thread ($19.9\text{ ms}$), which does not measure DRAM bandwidth contention and thermal throttling when **8 independent single-threaded processes** run concurrently for 7–14 hours.
  3. **Severe underestimation of Stage B run counts and `S-pred` / `S-oracle` per-step overhead:** §14 estimates Stage B at *"$\approx 20\text{–}30\text{ CPU-hours}$."* However, §9 and §12 specify:
     * *Validation runs (15 seeds):* Selecting `S-C*` and calibrating $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ across the 7 trigger candidates (`S-uniform`, `S-C2`, `S-C3`, `S-C4`, `S-C5`, `S-C5b`, `S-C7a`) plus calibrating $\theta_{\text{cal}}$ for `S-c_e`, `S-pred(B_strong)`, and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` requires $10 \times 15 = \mathbf{150\text{ validation runs}}$ (or at minimum $7 \times 15 = 105$ runs just for `S-C*`).
     * *Test runs (40 paired seeds per scheduler, §12):* Running the active test schedulers—`S-c_e`, `S-C*`, `S-pred(B_strong)`, `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)`, `S-oracle`, `S-random`, the exploratory `S-FOREVER` arm, plus the **4 placebo/yoked reliance arms for Gate G8** (`S-c_e` and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` under both within-run block-permuted and cross-seed yoked streams, §9)—requires $11 \times 40 = \mathbf{440\text{ test runs}}$ (plus G6 doubled-training-budget checks and 5% G7 reruns).
     * In total, Stage B requires **$\approx 545\text{–}590$ full life-phase runs**. At $10\text{–}12\text{ min}$ per run, 545–590 runs take **$91\text{–}118\text{ CPU-hours}$** ($\approx 11\text{–}15\text{ wall-clock hours}$ on 8 workers), which is **$3\times$ to $5\times$ larger** than the $20\text{–}30\text{ CPU-hours}$ stated in §14.
     * Worse, in `S-pred`, if the 5-fold GBDT ensemble scores all $K \le 40$ active tasks at *every single optimizer step* ($20,000\text{ steps} \times 5\text{ folds} = 100,000$ calls to scikit-learn `.predict()`), Python/Cython dispatch overhead alone adds $3\text{–}8\text{ min}$ per run; similarly, if `S-oracle` evaluates half-B probes on all active tasks at every step rather than at segment/grid boundaries, `S-oracle` will take hours per run.

* **Why it matters:** The per-run ceiling ($40\text{ min}$) and Stage A budget ($150\text{ CPU-hours}$) in §12a conflict at full sample size, Stage B is underestimated by $\sim 70\text{–}90\text{ CPU-hours}$, and unbatched probes or per-step `S-pred`/`S-oracle` checks will create severe runtime bottlenecks.

* **Concrete Fix:**
  1. In §12a, replace the static $\le 40\text{ min}$ per-run ceiling with: *"Measured time per full run $\le 25\text{ min}$ single-threaded with all 8 worker processes running concurrently (and $\le 140\text{ CPU-hours}$ for all Stage A runs + $\le 10\text{ CPU-hours}$ for model fitting $\le 150\text{ CPU-hours}$ total)."*

##### 5. [MAJOR] §16 (Threats to validity and mitigations): Unmitigated covariate-definition shift and closed-loop policy shift between Stage A and Stage B for `S-pred`

* **Section:** §16 (page 18), in relation to §7 and §9.

* **Problem:** It is a potential concern that §16 omits two distribution shifts affecting `S-pred(B_strong)` and `S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` in Stage B (which test $H_{4a}$):
  1. **Feature measurement shift on competence covariates at $t_0$:** In Stage A (§7), the model's competence covariates $\bar{L}_{B,k}(t_0)$ and $\text{Acc}_{B,k}(t_0)$ in $X_{\text{cov}}$ are computed on clean post-segment evaluations of **probe half B**. In Stage B (§9, "Online covariates"), the protocol states: *"In Stage B, competence covariates at $t_0$ are computed from the loss and accuracy on the last training mini-batches of the segment, not from probes."* Because training mini-batch losses are computed while weights are changing (and for rule tasks, training items are in-sample whereas probes are held-out generalisation items, §4.1), feeding training mini-batch metrics into a GBDT fitted on post-segment probe-B metrics creates a direct covariate shift at $t_0$.
  2. **Closed-loop scheduling shift:** The Stage A predictor is trained offline on open-loop trajectories ($F_1$–$F_3$), but deployed in Stage B in a closed loop where $\arg\max_k \hat{F}_k \ge \theta_{\text{eff}}$ actively alters the inter-exposure intervals and selects extreme positive prediction errors (optimizer's curse).

* **Why it matters:** If $H_1$ and $H_{4b}$ (`S-c_e` vs. `S-C*`) are supported, but $H_{4a}$ (`S-pred(B_strong + c_e)` vs. `S-pred(B_strong)`) is null or inconclusive, it will be impossible to tell whether $c_e$ fails to help a covariate-aware scheduler or whether the Stage A GBDT was miscalibrated by the probe-to-training-batch feature mismatch and closed-loop extrapolation.

* **Concrete Fix:** Add a row to §16 (and align §9):
  * **Threat:** `Covariate shift (probe-B vs. training-batch competence at t₀) and closed-loop trajectory shift for S-pred in Stage B.`
  * **Mitigation:** `Log last-mini-batch training loss/accuracy at every segment end s₀ during Stage A training runs and fit the Stage B S-pred models using those exact online training-batch covariates instead of probe-B covariates; log in-loop prediction calibration (MAE and ρ_S against half-A probes) during Stage B runs.`

#### 2. Rejected or Modified Suggestions Verification (§19 Table 2)

* **[MAJOR] §19 ("Two-sample bootstrap with refitting for all models (A) $\to$ Modified: ridge only; cross-fitting for GBDT (C)"), cross-checked with §8, §10.1, §10.2 — 5-Fold Cross-Fitting on Fixed Training Runs Does Not Propagate Training-Sample Variance into the Test-Run Bootstrap CI:**
  * **Problem:** A potential concern with modifying the two-sample bootstrap to apply only to ridge regression while using 5-fold cross-fitting over the 30 training runs for the primary GBDT model (§8) is that the two procedures address different statistical quantities. Averaging 5 GBDT models fitted on 4/5 subsets of a *fixed* set of 30 training runs stabilizes prediction variance conditional on those 30 runs, but when the confirmatory BCa bootstrap (§10.1, §10.2) resamples **only test runs** while holding the 5-fold GBDT ensemble fixed, the sampling variance of the 30 training runs ($\text{Var}_{\text{train}}$) is treated as zero.
    Because the training split has only 30 runs (10 per family F1–F3), adding $\Delta c_e^{D^*}$ to `B_strong` in a greedy tree model (`max_features = 1.0`) can produce a shared positive or negative shift in out-of-sample $\Delta R^2_r$ across all test runs due to random variation in the 30 training runs (cf. Nadeau & Bengio, 2003; Bouthillier et al., 2021, arXiv:2103.03098). Resampling test runs alone cannot detect variance coming from the training sample, yielding an artificially narrow confidence interval that inflates both false positives under MET and false equivalence under TOST. Moreover, §8 provides no rule for how H1 is reported if the two-sample bootstrap on ridge (or a refitted GBDT bootstrap) disagrees with the test-only GBDT bootstrap.
  * **Why it matters:** Fitting a single locked-hyperparameter `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` on 30 runs takes $< 0.5\text{ s}$ on a laptop CPU, so refitting $B_{\text{train}} = 200\text{–}500$ bootstrap training samples once after Stage A finishes takes only $2\text{–}8$ CPU-minutes—making the computational rationale for omitting training-run resampling in GBDT unnecessary.
  * **Concrete Fix:** In §8 and §10.1, pre-compute $B_{\text{train}} = 250$ bootstrap-refitted GBDT model pairs (`B_strong` and `B_strong` + $\Delta c_e^{D^*}$, with locked hyperparameters) by resampling the 30 training runs with replacement, evaluate each pair on the $n$ test runs to form an $n \times B_{\text{train}}$ matrix of $\Delta R^2_{r,b}$, and draw jointly over $(r, b)$ in the confirmatory BCa bootstrap loop.

* **[MINOR] §19 ("Packed 64-token sequences with block-diagonal masks (A) $\to$ Rejected: examples are unpacked, which is simpler and, as measured, $\approx 4\times$ cheaper per step"), cross-checked with §3, §5.1, §7 — Dead Positional Embeddings $5 \dots 63$ Undergo Pure Decoupled Weight-Decay Drift Under Unpacked Short Sequences:**
  * **Problem:** While the per-step speed rationale holds (since run length is fixed in optimizer steps rather than epochs, unpacked 4–6 token sequences are $\approx 4.4\times$ faster per step than packed 64-token sequences), keeping `maximum context = 64` with learned positional embeddings (§3, Table on p. 5) while only ever feeding unpacked sequences of length 4–6 tokens means positional embedding vectors at indices $5 \dots 63$ ($59 \times 128 = 7,552$ parameters) never receive a gradient during Childhood or Life. Under AdamW with weight decay $\lambda = 0.01$ applied to all parameters, those 7,552 unused parameters shrink deterministically at every step as $\theta(\tau) = (1 - \eta_\tau \lambda)\theta(\tau-1)$.
  * **Why it matters:** This deterministic exponential decay on dead parameters injects a noiseless function of cumulative learning rate $\sum \eta_\tau$ directly into raw $D_1$ (`‖θ(τ+1) − θ(τ)‖₂`) and into $\|\theta(t_0)\|_2$ in `X_cov`.
  * **Concrete Fix:** In §3, either reduce `maximum context` from `64` to `8` (matching the 4–6 token unpacked example length) or explicitly exclude `pos_emb` (along with LayerNorm weights and biases) from decoupled weight decay in AdamW.
