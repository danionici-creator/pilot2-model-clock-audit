## Referee summary and overall assessment

The paper asks which evidence about elapsed time and state history remains available to a persistent artificial agent after its checkpointable state has been restored. It distinguishes platform-only checks, authenticated claims supplied by a trusted monitor, and raw trusted readings interpreted by a predictive self-model. It separates a continuity anchor from an accumulated-change index, and proposes six independently testable questions with conventional competitors. Pilot 2 is introduced as an unsuccessful search for an index sufficiently distinct from optimizer telemetry under a stipulated reconstruction gate, alongside narrower replay-scheduling comparisons.

**Recommendation: major revision for a Perspective/position venue.** The defensible contribution is a problem framing, a taxonomy of trust and evidence access, and an evaluation agenda. It is not a new impossibility theorem, clock, continuity protocol, unlearning method, or attribution algorithm. That scope can support a Perspective, but the present argument does not establish why raw evidence should outperform an adequately informative trusted interface. Its central empirical value proposition remains untested. The absence of new data is not itself a reason to reject a Perspective. Whether the distinctions generate a fair, discriminating test, rather than rename existing security interfaces, is the decisive issue.

The principal required revisions are: **(i)** replace the inference from high reconstruction R² to lack of detectable predictive information with the actual operational screening decision; **(ii)** distinguish statistical equivalence in the ports from descriptive outcomes in the authors' code; **(iii)** make crash recovery and the narrower observable continuity events explicit; and **(iv)** specify a matched comparison against a rich authenticated monitor interface. Version 1.4 materially improves v1.3's anchor cases, records the second study's negative screening results and openly dates its rule amendment. Those improvements do not resolve the four issues above. No fatal objection to the bounded conceptual programme was established. My confidence is high in the mathematical counterexamples and checked numerical/reporting distinctions, and moderate in the judgment of residual conceptual novelty, which rests on a targeted rather than exhaustive literature review.

## Strengths

- **Narrow priority statement (§1, pp. 3–4).** The paper explicitly acknowledges its overlap with rollback protection, unlearning, attribution and FOREVER. It labels the proposition elementary and previously known (§3, p. 7). These concessions make a synthesis contribution assessable without crediting it with inventions it does not contain.
- **Trust and checkpoint scope are explicit (§2, pp. 4–6).** Threat models A–C, authenticated binding and a protected readout path prevent the argument from resting on the mere existence of a physical signal.
- **Conventional mechanisms can win (§5, pp. 11–13).** Independent closure of levels and substitution of a winning conventional mechanism are useful disciplines for a research agenda. The paper admits that full evaluation contracts have not been supplied.
- **The empirical studies are not presented as positive validation (abstract, pp. 1–2; §§4–6, pp. 9–14).** Pilot 1 is explicitly outside the programme's tests. Pilot 2 is distinguished from a refutation of predictive value. The reconstruction values quoted in §6 agree with the checked records; the qualifications below concern what those values and the replay comparisons establish.

## Weaknesses and findings

Severity: **fatal** would defeat a central conclusion without a viable local revision; **major** changes an argument, interpretation or required evaluation; **minor** is a local correction or presentation issue. No fatal finding was established. The main weaknesses concern construct validity, the scope of continuity detection, the strength of the competitor and compression of distinct empirical outcomes. A major-revision recommendation does not imply research misconduct or that every proposed use is unsound.

### Point 2 — Arguments and logic

**The proposition (§3, p. 7).** The deterministic equality is correct. With the same state and identically distributed fresh randomness, output distributions also agree. The relevant comparison is between histories with identical present observations, not every uninterrupted trajectory and every restored trajectory. This is an indistinguishability lemma; it does not establish that a particular two-counter design is necessary, or that external evidence will always suffice. Non-restored observations must actually differ between the histories. Authentication protects interpretation under an adversarial threat model; it is not part of the mathematical state-equality identity.

The proposition's final “only if” is stronger than its proof: a trusted, fresh, lineage-addressed notification that the monitor executed a restore can itself identify that event without either of the two specific state/readout commitments enumerated there. Some trusted attribution of the event to the instance is still required. The proof establishes the need for discriminating observations outside the restored state, not the exhaustive necessity of those two binding constructions.

**The corrected anchor (§4, p. 8; compare v1.3, p. 8).** Version 1.3 said both that an advanced counter means restoration and that both quantities advancing means uninterrupted operation, and asserted necessity and sufficiency without the new restrictions. Version 1.4 correctly separates a load-time comparison from renewal at running commits, replaces necessity with conditional sufficiency, and allows execution without a commit. I do not repeat the superseded criticism against the corrected text. Under the stated single-lineage invariant, `N_live > N_sealed` identifies loading a state behind the latest committed version; equal counters with elapsed time identify no additional committed steps. They do not identify whether the machine was physically suspended, merely idle, executing an uncommitted step, or reloading the latest checkpoint. Short intervals within time-reference error remain unresolved. These are limitations already partly acknowledged in the body but absent from its strongest summaries.

The missing implementation obligation is crash consistency **including the persistent counter**, not just the state and its seal. Starting at counter 7, incrementing to 8 and crashing before installing the new sealed state leaves the old valid seal at 7 and the live counter at 8. Step (3) classifies this as restoration. Preventing reordering does not prevent that crash. A recoverable transaction or an explicit assumed atomic primitive can solve this; the review does not claim that existing continuity protocols cannot solve it. A seal mismatch establishes failure of verification, not uniquely malicious forgery: corruption, partial writes or a mismatched lineage must have defined outcomes too. Fork contention likewise requires checking the expected predecessor at each commit; two branches loaded at 7 can otherwise submit states successively and be sealed at 8 and 9 without the sketch explicitly rejecting the stale branch.

**Dual clock (§4, pp. 9–10).** Accumulation distinguishes path length from endpoint displacement, but by construction loses direction and event identity. For a nonnegative distance it is non-decreasing on one forward trajectory. Neither a scalar clock nor a clock pair can by itself identify which experience caused a change; provenance and task information do that work. The paper recognises the need for provenance in §2 and weight/function differences in §4. Its open choice of distance is not itself an error. However, it must define which state components enter the distance, and what happens at rollback: including counters or the accumulator itself makes bookkeeping contribute to the purported experience signal. Restoring the accumulator can make it decrease while the anchor increases. A lineage-local index, with cross-restoration intervals excluded, is a coherent solution. So is an externally maintained accumulator, with its additional state and cost declared; they are different proposals.

The displacement/path ratio is an interpretable straightness measure only with a suitable fixed metric and nonzero denominator. An arbitrary divergence or signed competence/influence change need not satisfy those conditions. Positive elapsed time beyond calibration uncertainty is likewise needed for the rate. Costly candidates are not automatically valid measures of adaptation. A larger update can be harmless, a smaller one catastrophic, and changing LoRA factor scale can change parameter distance without changing the represented function. The manuscript acknowledges the general problem but should stop describing the expensive end of its candidate list as already “valid.”

**Table 2 and closure (§5, pp. 11–13).** Independent substitution of conventional winners is coherent; the six rows are not a logical dependency ladder. Several questions nevertheless need better alignment with their metrics:

The Pareto-style closure rule is conservative, not logically inconsistent. Real trade-offs may leave it unresolved indefinitely; the paper honestly says that unresolved is not support. It does not need a universal cost scalar. It does need finite prespecified candidate/regime boundaries and uncertainty/tolerances for “no worse,” or an unmeasurably small disadvantage on one of many costs can prevent closure. This belongs in the promised contract rather than a demand for every contract in a Perspective.

**The new non-identifiability exit is not justified as an information theorem (§5, p. 11).** Let independent balanced signs be X and Y, with candidate rate `C = 2 + sqrt(0.95) X + sqrt(0.05) Y` and telemetry X. The rate is positive and its population reconstruction R² from X is 0.95. Yet for a later target Y, X alone has no predictive skill and X plus C predicts Y exactly. Thus a small candidate-reconstruction residual can contain all target-relevant information. The phrase “no information beyond them that the test could detect” does not follow from exceeding 0.90. Nor does the R² threshold establish inadequate statistical power without a target model and noise/sample-size assumptions. The caveat on p. 14 (“the remainder ... could still matter”) is right but contradicts the categorical sentence on p. 11. A prespecified decision not to test a residual is legitimate; calling that decision empirical non-identification without qualification is misleading.

**Method (§6, pp. 13–14).** Information matching, competent controls, reduced-input diagnostics and separating onset from eventual skill are relevant. An independent decoder's achieved accuracy is an empirical benchmark for accessible signal, not an upper bound on it. Training on more data does not make it an oracle. “Half the ceiling” is not a generally transferable competence criterion; it must be relative to a defined null and justified for each metric. Reset/order interventions require controls for changes to the target and task distribution; Pilot 2's protocol itself recognises that resetting the trained task would erase what forgetting is to be measured. Equivalence by two one-sided tests is appropriate; the confidence level, paired unit and multiplicity must be fixed in the individual contract. The text does not misstate non-significance as equivalence. Onset-conditioned skill also needs the unconditional success fraction and failures reported, to avoid selecting only successful runs.

**Safety inference (§7, pp. 14–15).** Denying lifecycle authority is a concrete privilege boundary. It does not prove behavioural corrigibility. Evidence used “only” to invalidate cached facts or revise uncertainty can still change an action downstream; re-verification is explicitly an action in Level 5. The paper already acknowledges refusal and evidence virtualisation, so this is a request to state an information-flow/mediation boundary, not an allegation that it ignores governance altogether.

### What the Level 2 status should mean

“Not identifiable for its cheapest candidates in the regimes tested” is justified **as the protocol's label for failing a predeclared reconstruction screen**. It is inflated if read as empirical or mathematical proof of no incremental predictive information. It is also too broad if “cheapest candidates” means every possible cheap candidate rather than the finite list tested. Conversely, calling the work merely inconclusive would discard its positive result: several particular rates were predictably related to ordinary telemetry, and the port scheduling comparisons satisfy their specified margins. The useful result is this bounded redundancy/screening evidence, not a refutation of the experiential-index idea or an observation of forgetting prediction.

The Perspective's distinction between ending a candidate test and closing the whole level is appropriate. The second study does not test the continuity anchor, raw versus adjudicated access, a non-restorable physical reference, or online self-prediction. It does not validate them by elimination. What changes from v1.3 is that a cheap update-norm implementation can no longer be presented as an unexamined promising route: it now faces specific telemetry baselines and unfavourable results in those settings. That shifts the paper's scientific centre toward the evidence-interface question, while leaving the wider predictive hypothesis unresolved.

### The post-result rule amendment

The amendment is openly dated in §5 and is explicitly prospective. The existing v0.4 protocol contains G2 with both linear and flexible reconstruction thresholds, and the outcome label. That supports consistency with the study's recorded design, not independent proof of when the files were fixed. The closure report is more careful: preregistration was local, with hashes rather than a pre-run public deposit; later tests were an adaptive sequence, each fixed before its own computation according to the author's records. Add that short qualifier here. There is no basis in the inspected evidence to accuse the author of retrospectively inventing G2, but no independent timestamp evidence supports stronger claims either.

A separate consistency issue is §5's instruction that completed secondary comparisons are reported “by the rule above.” The general rule requires non-inferiority plus no worse declared costs. Pilot 2's authors-code comparison used descriptive ±2-point margins, no statistical equivalence interval, and did not measure a full set of costs. T-A and S2 had statistical intervals but did not implement the whole new multi-cost contract either. These are reported outcomes under their own local rules, not closure under the complete Perspective rule. The manuscript can say this in one sentence without withdrawing any measured result.

### What must be added, and what need not be imported

The scheduling summary needs one explicit limitation: **the authors-code aggregate comparison is descriptive, with one run per crossed cell and inconclusive individual-task effects**. The first dbpedia difference is +5.33 points; the two shared-start contrasts are −0.51 and +3.29. Opposite signs fail the stated robust-reproduction criterion; they do not demonstrate absence of a clock effect. Evaluation determinism, established by the report's post hoc checks, does not eliminate training nondeterminism or starting-state interaction.

The port also needs a brief sensitivity qualification: in T-A retention was about 0.24 overall and the small replayed subset was recalled almost perfectly under every clock; in S2 triggers coincided. Those facts make lack of a timing advantage less informative than the unqualified abstract suggests. The abstract need not include every number, but it should say “no demonstrated scheduling advantage” and distinguish the descriptive original-code result.

The Perspective does **not** need the full chronology of colloquia, all hardware details, post hoc noise tables, launch commands or all archive omissions. Those belong in [24]. A compact evidence-status note (candidate screen/H1 not run; port equivalence; authors-code descriptive result; local preregistration) is enough. Avoid treating low-level fidelity defects found in an audit as reasons to expand a conceptual paper into a second reproduction report.

Suggested replacement for the core of §4, p. 9:

> In the tested synthetic and small-model implementations, the interval rates of the selected change indices were reconstructed out of sample from inexpensive optimizer-derived summaries or same-probe competitors, exceeding the protocol's prespecified reconstruction threshold. The planned test of incremental forgetting prediction was therefore not run; predictive value in the residual remains unknown. Replay comparisons met the specified equivalence margins in our port, where replay times nearly coincided for the momentum-based clock. With the adapted authors' code, aggregate differences were within descriptive margins, but individual-task effects remained unresolved. These results restrict the tested candidates and regimes; they do not close Level 2 or test the continuity anchor.

Suggested replacement for the amendment's inferential sentence (§5, p. 11):

> If a candidate exceeds a prespecified out-of-sample reconstruction threshold, the protocol may stop its incremental-prediction test and report a failed reconstruction gate. This is an operational stop, not proof that the residual contains no useful information. Pilot 2 used the label “not identifiable” for this outcome; its locally recorded rule preceded the relevant analyses, while its adoption into this Perspective is retrospective and applies prospectively.

## Point 4 — Honesty, calibration and reference checking

### Calibration of the narrative

The paper discloses its negative outcomes, prior omissions, changes of rule and extensive AI assistance. Its acknowledgment of established mechanisms is substantive. I found no evidence of concealed positive/negative result switching. The strongest problems are overstatements in otherwise qualified prose: the abstract and conclusion sometimes drop the distinctions maintained in the main text (P05, P09, P14). An honest caveat in one place does not make an incompatible categorical sentence elsewhere correct.

“The hypothesis is unchanged” (version notice, p. 1) is defensible for the broad conditional question, but the set of admissible candidates and the rule for terminating tests have changed. It would be more informative to say “the overarching question is unchanged; its candidate set, status and evaluation rules have been revised.” “Not identifiable” repeatedly risks functioning as a protective label for an unperformed prediction test. The author should keep the recorded label for historical accuracy while using plain operational wording in the conclusions.

Self-citation is legitimate here: [24] is the data source for Pilot 2; [25] is the source of the illustrative control failures. Neither study provides evidence for the distinctive continuity-interface hypothesis. Section 6 mostly observes that boundary. The conclusion's “the accompanying exploratory study ... had pointed the same way” (p. 16) invites a stronger sense of convergence than the differing targets warrant. Replace it with the particular lessons learned about controls and local statistics. The Pilot 1 effect size and claim of 100 bit-identical runs were not independently checked in this task.

The necessity claim in the motivating intuition (P02) and “costly and valid” (P07) remain exceptions. “Knowledge age” also needs the timestamp of acquisition or last verification and a model of how the relevant world facts change; elapsed time alone is not a universal measure of staleness. ## Point 5 — Presentation

The PDF has 20 pages, with references beginning on p. 17. Visual inspection of the opening, both tables, the protocol page and the final reference page found no clipping or illegible mathematical symbols. Table headers repeat across the page break. These are substantive reading and prioritisation problems, not a broken document export. Layout preferences below are **editorial judgments**, distinct from the scientific corrections already identified.

**Structure and length.** The argumentative order is understandable, but the opening makes readers work through a long version history before reaching the question. The metadata table occupies a substantial part of p. 1 and the abstract spills onto p. 2. The “What this perspective offers” list then repeats the abstract before an introduction that repeats the motivation. For a strong interdisciplinary venue, move the change log and detailed revision provenance to supplementary/version notes; retain a short statement identifying the two accompanying studies. Open with one concrete case—loading a stale state and deciding whether to re-verify a fact—followed by the exact information available to the agent and to the conventional monitor.

The citation-dense introduction on p. 3 establishes breadth but does not yet make the residual question vivid. Group security continuity, timing/change measures, and attribution/unlearning into three short contrasts. The biology discussion (§3, pp. 7–8) can be substantially compressed: its analogical status is already explicitly limited, and it does not establish the claimed benefit. Retain the distinction between learned timing and checkpoint-resistant evidence. Compress repeated disclaimers about consciousness (§§1, 7–8) into a short scope statement and a focused risk paragraph. Keep the operational safety trade-off; it is materially relevant. The detailed AI-assisted revision chronology (p. 16) can be shortened subject to the venue's disclosure rules, while retaining roles and author responsibility. None of these cuts requires suppressing an unfavourable result or the post-result amendment.

**Abstract and terminology.** “Dual clock” remains defensible as a defined pair of coordinates; Pilot 2 does not make that definition incoherent. It is misleading if it suggests that an accumulated-change coordinate has already been validated as an agent's adaptation clock. Prefer “continuity evidence and an optional accumulated-change index” in the substantive claim, reserving “dual clock” for the proposed representation. Similarly, distinguish four uses of “continuity”: current checkpoint freshness, a gap without committed progress, branch/device lineage, and historical information used for prediction. A reader should not have to infer which one a claim concerns. “Non-identifiable” needs its protocol-specific definition at first use, not only the residual caveat on p. 14. The opening abstract currently gives the strongest anchor and replay claims without the qualifications needed to assess them (P05, P09, P14).

One possible shorter abstract, offered as replacement wording rather than a required style:

> Checkpoint restoration can erase an artificial agent's internal evidence of elapsed time and subsequent state changes. This Perspective classifies temporal evidence by its trust assumptions and checkpoint scope, and asks whether exposing authenticated evidence to an agent's predictive self-model improves decisions beyond a conventional trusted monitor. We distinguish a continuity anchor from an optional accumulated-change index and propose six evaluation questions with conventional competitors. Under a single-lineage, recoverable-commit model, trusted version and time readings distinguish a stale committed state from an interval without new commits; they do not generally identify suspension or silent copying. A separate study found that selected change-index rates were reconstructible from optimizer-derived or same-probe summaries and therefore stopped before testing incremental forgetting prediction. Replay comparisons found no demonstrated advantage, with statistical equivalence confined to the tested port comparisons and descriptive results in the authors' code. The continuity and downstream self-model proposals remain untested. The contribution is an evidence taxonomy and evaluation agenda, not a new security primitive or a validated measure of adaptation.

## Point 6 — Priorities that would most increase scientific value

1. **Make one fair downstream comparison concrete (§5, pp. 12–13; P01/P10).** The highest-value addition is one fully specified test of whether exposing raw continuity evidence improves a decision beyond a competent, richly informative trusted monitor. Detection against an impoverished or artificially delayed flag will not resolve the distinctive claim. Before running it, provide the contract below, or an equally explicit analytic worked example showing the proposed difference. This would add more to the Perspective than another list of possible clocks or a larger model using the same reconstruction screen.
2. **Repair the evidential language (§§4–6, pp. 9–14; P09/P13–P17).** State what was reconstructed, on what split, and what was not tested. Use the protocol's “not identifiable” label only with its operational meaning. Say that residual predictive utility is unknown. Keep port equivalence, the authors-code descriptive aggregate result and non-replicated task effects distinct; do not imply that the complete multi-cost closure contract was met. Give the short local/adaptive-preregistration qualifier. These changes are necessary before publication and require no new experiment.
3. **Make the continuity claim technically defensible (§§3–4, pp. 7–9; P03–P05/P18).** Separate the elementary indistinguishability lemma from one particular binding construction. Replace the sketch's implicit atomicity with a named recovery primitive or an explicit assumed recoverable transaction over the counter and state. Add a stale-predecessor check. Restrict detection claims to committed-state freshness and intervals without commits; retain the limits on silent forks, same-version reloads and readout races. Correct the attribution of the exact clock/counter pair to prior protocols. A concise state-machine contract with one normal trace, one crash trace and one stale-branch trace would be enough for this Perspective; a new security protocol is unnecessary.
4. **Define the optional change index and align the programme's quantities (§4, pp. 9–10; Table 2, pp. 11–12; P06–P08/P11).** Choose which persistent application state is measured and how accumulation behaves across restoration/migration. Separate physical duration from sequential work. State prediction quality constraints for history tests and a task-specific competence criterion. Do not require every level's full preregistration in this paper, but make the first contract complete and the remaining questions internally consistent.
5. **Narrow the novelty and safety claims, then shorten (§1, pp. 3–4; §§7–8, pp. 14–16; P02/P12/P19–P23).** Claim an interface/evaluation programme; avoid universal claims about what all current self-models lack. Make behavioural recovery a separate policy/mediation problem. Consolidate the front matter and analogies once the scientific distinctions are settled. The shortest version should retain the strongest conventional competitor and the unfavourable Pilot 2 outcome.

### The single test I would prioritise

Use the stale-fact re-verification example already on p. 13 as **one Level 5 test, with Level 4 detection as a competence check**. Begin in an instrumented simulation, making no hardware-security claim. A completed study is not required merely to submit a Perspective; a reproducible design and a worked example would make the agenda materially stronger.

| Contract component | Required specification |
|---|---|
| World and target | Facts change under a prespecified process. Agents can act using cached facts or pay a stated cost to verify them. Primary outcome: cumulative decision loss/regret including verification cost, over whole independent trajectories. Do not use clock reconstruction R² as the utility endpoint. |
| Events | Randomised, paired trajectories containing ordinary execution, no-commit intervals, stale-checkpoint restoration, latest-checkpoint reloads and crash/recovery. Define truth from an independent event log unavailable to the evaluated agents. Label observationally indistinguishable cases as such rather than requiring impossible classification. |
| Trusted evidence | Identical time/version/lineage readings and uncertainty bounds, identical checkpoint boundary, same freshness and availability guarantees. Use an explicitly specified recoverable-commit state machine. A simulated monitor cannot demonstrate resistance to a real compromised host; implementation assurance is a separate question. |
| Interfaces/competitors | A competent binary event flag; a rich authenticated conventional interface reporting relevant time/version differences, uncertainty and provenance; raw readings supplied to the predictive agent. Give all arms the same ordinary timestamps, cached-fact provenance and task context that the task permits. Include no-new-evidence and trusted-log controls to establish sensitivity. |
| Fairness | Match training data, action/verification budget and access latency. If a flag is delayed or withheld, apply the same source/path failure to raw evidence unless a concrete architectural reason prevents it; declare and cost that reason. Let the rich monitor use an adequate calibrated predictor rather than prescribing an artificially weak summary. |
| Competence and splits | First verify correct handling of the event classes that are observable. Tune on separate trajectories; test on held-out trajectories, with a separately labelled transfer regime if relevant. Preserve task competence in ablations. Count independent trajectories, not correlated decisions within a trajectory, as the basic replication unit. |
| Decision rule | Prespecify the smallest worthwhile regret improvement, non-inferiority/equivalence margins, cost tolerances, planned sample size/power and interval procedure. Give both quality and resource costs with uncertainty. Do not pick numerical margins after looking at the results. |
| Interpretation | A raw-over-binary gain alone shows the cost of compressing evidence into a bit. A gain over a competent rich interface under matched budgets could support a representation/learning advantage. Non-inferiority of the conventional interface at no greater declared cost closes that particular use/regime. It does not close all six levels or prove that change indices can never help. |

A short analytic boundary should precede this test: if a monitor's authenticated output is a sufficient statistic for the target given the available task context, then raw observations offer no additional target information. If it supplies the same readings, the two interfaces are informationally equivalent. Differences can still arise from finite computation, learning, latency or robustness. State which of those the experiment is designed to test. This is the argument most likely to prevent the research programme from becoming a sequence of weak-baseline demonstrations.

### What can be retained after revision

The relative-to-checkpoint definition of non-restorability, the distinction between physical duration and accumulated change, independent closure/substitution of programme components, information-matched controls, and the negative candidate-screening result all remain usable. The six-level organisation is a choice of presentation, not an empirical discovery; it need not be abandoned if its metrics and dependencies are made explicit. Pilot 2 warrants setting aside the tested cheap implementations under the chosen rule, not declaring either the complete programme successful or its conceptual question refuted.
