# HIGH LEVEL SUMMARY
---
## Summary
This colloquium dossier presents exploratory technical results ("technical rounds" R1–R9) for Pilot 2, evaluating whether an AI agent's accumulated internal change (an "experiential index" $c_e(t) = \sum_{\text{steps}} D(S(t), S(t+1))$, including the parameter-update path length $\tau$ of FOREVER) carries information beyond cheap online optimizer telemetry ($B_{\text{strong}}$) for predicting catastrophic forgetting ($T_1$). Across both a 4-layer synthetic Transformer world on CPU (6 schedule families F1–F6) and a Qwen3-0.6B LoRA continual learning setup on GPU following the FOREVER schedule, the study applies a blind identifiability gate (Gate G2: held-out $R^2 \ge 0.90$ for reconstructing the change rate $\rho = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$ from telemetry closes confirmatory hypothesis H1). The results show that all parameter-distance clocks ($D_1$, $D_{1,\text{rel}}$, $D_2$, and FOREVER's $\tau$) are reconstructed with held-out $R^2 \in [0.94, 0.99]$ by $B_{\text{strong}}$ and largely by a single first-moment telemetry scalar ($\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|$), leaving only the functional Jensen–Shannon divergence clock ($D_3$) under 30%-label-noise distractors (Family F6) un-reconstructed ($R^2 = 0.59$ against same-cost probe loss changes $C_{10c}$; $0.81$ under GBDT) and framing eight strategic decisions for the confirmatory stage.

## Strengths
* The study introduces a principled, blind two-stage identifiability gate (Gate G2) requiring candidate internal clocks to prove non-collinearity ($R^2 < 0.90$) against logged optimizer telemetry before evaluating downstream forgetting ($T_1$), providing a rigorous methodological safeguard against false attribution in continual learning.
* The dossier delivers a thorough mechanistic and code-level audit of parameter-trajectory clocks across diverse synthetic schedules and LLM LoRA fine-tuning, demonstrating that apparent decouplings from the learning-rate schedule ($\sum \eta_t$) stem from per-phase optimizer moment resets while $\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|$ explains $94\%\text{–}99\%$ of the step-rate variance.
* By evaluating accumulated square-root Jensen–Shannon functional divergence ($D_3$) directly against a compute-matched per-item probe loss-change baseline ($C_{10c}$) using the exact same reference forward passes, the design cleanly isolates noise-free schedules where $D_3$ is redundant ($R^2 = 0.95\text{–}0.99$) from noisy distractor regimes (Family F6) where full-distribution drift diverges from target-token loss deltas.
* The experimental pipeline enforces bit-identical determinism across CPU and GPU runs, transparently separates pre-fixed from post-hoc analyses, and uses diagnostic runs to accurately isolate implementation artefacts such as FP16 dynamic loss-scaling step skips and retroactive interference from end-of-task replay.

## Weaknesses
* The high held-out $R^2$ ($0.94\text{–}0.99$) for reconstructing parameter-distance clocks ($D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2, \tau$) from $B_{\text{strong}}$ and $\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|$ is largely an expected algebraic consequence of the deterministic AdamW update rule and rotational equilibrium—most notably for $D_2$, where weighting steps by a lagged second-moment estimate algebraically cancels AdamW's $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ preconditioner—so the authors might consider closing confirmatory Hypothesis H1 for parameter clocks and reframing Gate G2 as a mechanistic falsification audit (Reference Paper: Rotational Equilibrium: How Weight Decay Balances Learning Across Neural Networks, Atli Kosson, Bettina Messmer, and Martin Jaggi, 2024; Reference Paper: Muon is Scalable for LLM Training, Jingyuan Liu et al., 2025; Reference Paper: Limitations of the Empirical Fisher Approximation for Natural Gradient Descent, Frederik Kunstner, Philipp Hennig, and Lukas Balles, 2019).
* Please consider computing the language-model analogue of the same-cost probe loss-change competitor $C_{10c}$ (alongside bounded probability and entropy shifts) offline from the already-stored 31-word log-probabilities on the 128 reference prompts in R5, R6, and R9, as comparing $D_3$ solely against zero-pass optimizer telemetry confounds the mathematical form of $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ divergence with the extra information gained from periodic reference forward passes (Reference Paper: Online Continual Learning with Maximally Interfered Retrieval, Rahaf Aljundi et al., 2019; Reference Paper: AIMMerging: Adaptive Iterative Model Merging Using Training Trajectories for Language Model Continual Learning, Yujie Feng et al., 2025).
* The apparent survival of the functional clock $D_3$ against $C_{10c}$ ($R^2 = 0.59$) in the 30%-label-noise family F6 may reflect a metric-scaling and out-of-domain calibration artefact—comparing an 8-step chunk-accumulated, bounded full-simplex metric ($\sqrt{\text{JSD}} \le \sqrt{\ln 2}$) against an unbounded single-target cross-entropy loss change under zero-shot extrapolation from clean families (F1–F5) where label noise softens logit temperatures—so the authors might consider testing 8-step chunk-accumulated and bounded probability competitors within multi-seed F6 runs across both loss and accuracy forgetting targets (Reference Paper: Measuring and Regularizing Networks in Function Space, Ari S. Benjamin, David Rolnick, and Konrad Kording, 2019; Reference Paper: A New Metric for Probability Distributions, Dominik M. Endres and Johannes E. Schindelin, 2003; Reference Paper: A Closer Look at Memorization in Deep Networks, Devansh Arpit et al., 2017; Reference Paper: Spurious Forgetting in Continual Learning of Language Models, Junhao Zheng, Xidi Cai, Shengjie Qiu, and Qianli Ma, 2025).
* Because R5–R9 only evaluate offline trajectory reconstruction and post-hoc trigger offsets on trajectories already generated under $\tau$-scheduled replay—where every triggered replay instantiates a fresh `Trainer` and resets AdamW moments—the authors might consider running a closed-loop interventional experiment that actively drives the replay scheduler with $\tau$, the telemetry scalar $\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|$, the schedule-only clock $\sum \eta_t$, and fixed step counts to compare downstream forgetting directly (Reference Paper: FOREVER: Forgetting Curve-Inspired Memory Replay for Language Model Continual Learning, Yujie Feng et al., 2026; Reference Paper: On the Variance of the Adaptive Learning Rate and Beyond, Liyuan Liu et al., 2020; Reference Paper: Resetting the Optimizer in Deep RL: An Empirical Study, Kavosh Asadi, Rasool Fakoor, and Shoham Sabach, 2023).
* Replacing heterogeneous instruction tasks with homogeneous synthetic facts, using a single batch of 32 instead of $4 \times 8$ accumulation (which compresses task length to 80–160 steps and leaves Ebbinghaus thresholds $d \ge 7$ unreachable), and omitting `memory_data_ratio = 2` and Intensity-Aware Replay (IAR) regularization confound both cross-task gradient variance and the post-replay accuracy drop in R8, which could be addressed by adopting `bf16`, measuring acquisition before end-of-task replay, and running a lightweight check on heterogeneous instruction tasks (Reference Paper: FOREVER: Forgetting Curve-Inspired Memory Replay for Language Model Continual Learning, Yujie Feng et al., 2026; Reference Paper: Super-NaturalInstructions: Generalization via Declarative Instructions on 1600+ NLP Tasks, Yizhong Wang et al., 2022; Reference Paper: Mixed Precision Training, Paulius Micikevicius et al., 2018).
* Because the synthetic evaluations in R1–R4 rely on a single seed per family ($n=1$), fit unregularized OLS to 58 collinear telemetry features in $B_{\text{strong}}$ (causing severe extrapolation failure in R2), and include composite family F5 in the training folds when holding out F2, F3, or F4 in R3, please consider expanding to multi-seed runs with disjoint mechanism holdouts and a parsimonious, cost-stratified baseline ladder.

## Potential Issues And Suggestions
### [Background, Protocol v0.4 Framing, FOREVER Audit, and AdamW Step Theory] (Pages: 1-2)
#### Potential Mistakes and Improvements

1. **Algebraic Redundancy of Parameter-Distance Clocks ($D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2, \tau$) with Optimizer Telemetry ($B_{\text{strong}}$), Underspecified Candidate Formulas, and Implications for the Perspective Paper's "Level 2" Framing (Page 1, Section 1, Bullets 1–4)**
   - **Potential Concern (Algebraic Dependence on Optimizer State):** In Section 1 (Bullets 1, 3, and 4), the Perspective paper's ("A Time of Their Own", Zenodo 2026, v1.3) "Level 2" experiential index $c_e(t) = \sum_{\text{steps}} D(S(t), S(t+1))$ and Pilot 2 protocol v0.4 frame parameter-space step norms—$D_1 = \|\Delta\theta_t\|_2$ (and FOREVER's $\tau$), $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ (per-layer relative update), and $D_2$ (update weighted by a lagged second moment, Fisher-like)—as candidate clocks for which it "remains open" whether they carry information beyond logged optimizer statistics ($B_{\text{strong}}$). However, under deterministic AdamW with decoupled weight decay $\lambda$, the single-step parameter update is
     $$\Delta\theta_t = \theta_t - \theta_{t-1} = -\eta_t \left(\frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} + \lambda \theta_{t-1}\right).$$
     Once $B_{\text{strong}}$ logs the learning rate $\eta_t$, first-moment norm $\|\hat{m}_t\|_2$, second-moment trace $\sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_t}$, parameter norms $\|\theta_{t-1}\|_2$, and weight-decay path, any norm of $\Delta\theta_t$ is not an independent state observable; it is a deterministic algebraic function of the optimizer state buffers already summarized in $B_{\text{strong}}$ and requires the same $O(d_{\text{param}})$ vector reduction inside `optimizer.step()`.
   - **Potential Concern (Exact Algebraic Cancellation in $D_2$):** Because Bullet 4 describes $D_2$ only verbally as an "update weighted by a lagged second moment (Fisher-like)" without writing out its mathematical formula, it obscures a direct algebraic cancellation. When each coordinate update $\Delta\theta_{t,i} \approx -\eta_t \hat{m}_{t,i} / \sqrt{\hat{v}_{t,i}}$ is weighted in the Mahalanobis norm induced by the lagged diagonal second moment $\hat{v}_{t-\delta, i}$ (where $\hat{v}_t = \beta_2 \hat{v}_{t-1} + (1-\beta_2)g_t^2$ with $\beta_2 = 0.999$ changes by at most $0.1\%$ per step except at reset boundaries), Adam's adaptive preconditioner $\sqrt{\hat{v}_{t,i}}$ cancels out algebraically:
     $$\|\Delta\theta_t\|_{D_2} = \sqrt{\sum_i \hat{v}_{t-\delta, i} (\Delta\theta_{t,i})^2} \approx \sqrt{\sum_i \hat{v}_{t,i} \frac{\eta_t^2 \hat{m}_{t,i}^2}{\hat{v}_{t,i}}} = \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2.$$
     This mathematical identity directly explains why the single scalar $\Delta N + \sum \eta \|\hat{m}\|$ reconstructs the rate of $D_2$ with held-out $R^2 = 0.98\text{--}0.99$ across all schedule families (including out-of-family extrapolation in R2 and R3). Moreover, treating Adam's second moment $\hat{v}_t$ (the uncentered moving average of squared stochastic gradients, or empirical Fisher) as a "Fisher-like" curvature metric overlooks established optimization theory showing that the empirical Fisher is a poor approximation of true Fisher or Hessian curvature under mini-batch gradient noise.
   - **Potential Concern (Missing Mathematical Definitions of Candidates):** Bullet 4 does not provide explicit mathematical formulas for $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ (how per-layer relative norms $\|\Delta\theta_{t,\ell}\|_2 / \|\theta_{t-1,\ell}\|_2$ are aggregated across layers $\ell$—e.g., sum, mean, or RMS—and how embedding matrices are treated), $D_2$ (the exact weighting exponent on $\hat{v}_{t-\delta}$, the lag $\delta$, and whether bias-corrected $\hat{v}$ is used), $D_3$ (the logarithm base of the Jensen–Shannon divergence—nats vs. bits—and whether the output distribution in the synthetic world spans the full 512-token vocabulary or the 64 value tokens), or $D_4$ (which layer representations and CKA kernel are specified, and why $D_4$ is omitted without explanation from all subsequent tables and open decisions).
   - **Potential Concern (Conceptual Implications for Pilot 1 and the Perspective Paper):** Bullet 1 motivates $c_e(t)$ from the premise that "a persistent AI agent whose state can be saved and restored holds no unforgeable evidence of elapsed time or of its own change." Conceptually, an additive path sum $c_e(t) = \sum_{\text{steps}} D(S(t), S(t+1))$ is not a state property computable from a single static checkpoint $S(t)$ alone; it must be accumulated online step-by-step and stored as a running counter alongside the state, making it just as forgeable or resettable upon checkpoint restoration as an external step counter $\Delta N$ or logged optimizer telemetry $\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$. Taken together with Pilot 1 (Bullet 2: "the signal was carried by local update statistics") and Pilot 2's Gate G2 reconstruction results, the Perspective paper's open question is answered negatively for parameter-space path lengths ($D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2, \tau$).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Provide explicit mathematical equations for $D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2, D_3$, and $D_4$ in Section 1; derive the algebraic reduction of $D_2$ to $\eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$ under AdamW; clarify that $c_e(t)$ is an online trajectory accumulator rather than an unforgeable property of a static checkpoint; and specify that both Protocol v0.4 and the next revision of the Perspective paper should withdraw parameter-distance clocks as independent "Level 2" experiential indices (reclassifying them as composite summaries of optimizer telemetry), restricting the open question to functional output divergence ($D_3$) in regimes where parameter motion decouples from net input–output change (such as noisy distractors in F6).

2. **Conflation of Computational Cost Tiers Inside $B_{\text{strong}}$ and Cross-Arm Asymmetry of Reference-Probe Telemetry (Page 1, Section 1, Bullets 3–4)**
   - **Potential Concern (Bundling Zero-Pass Optimizer Scalars with Multi-Pass Functional Probes):** Bullet 3 defines $B_{\text{strong}}$ as "the union of cheap online telemetry (elapsed steps $\Delta N$, tokens, $\sum$ learning rate, $\sum$ loss, gradient norms, Adam moment norms, weight-decay path, parameter and second-moment norms, local update statistics, loss changes on a fixed reference battery R)." Grouping "loss changes on a fixed reference battery R" (instantiated in R3 as competitor `C10c`, the mean over items of $|\text{change of the per-item loss}|$ on $R$) under "cheap online telemetry" conflates three distinct computational cost regimes:
     1. *Tier 0 (Schedule/duration telemetry, $O(1)$ per step):* $\Delta N$, tokens, $\sum \eta_t$, pause ticks.
     2. *Tier 1 (Zero-extra-pass optimizer telemetry, $O(d_{\text{param}})$ inside `optimizer.step()`):* $\sum \text{loss}$, gradient norms, Adam moment norms ($\|\hat{m}_t\|_2, \sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_t}$), mean-field $\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2 \sqrt{d}/\sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_t}$, weight-decay path, and parameter norms.
     3. *Tier 2 (Active reference-probe forward passes, $O(|R| \cdot C_{\text{fwd}})$ every 8 steps):* loss changes on the 256-item (synthetic) or 128-prompt (LM) reference battery $R$ (`C10c`), which incurs the exact same periodic evaluation forward-pass compute cost as computing the functional clock $D_3$.
     Bundling Tier 1 and Tier 2 into a single unstructured baseline obscures whether candidate clocks are being tested against free optimizer logs or against same-compute probe evaluations, and leads to a cross-arm asymmetry where `C10c` is included in R3 on the synthetic world but omitted from R5, R6, and R9 on the language-model arm (as noted in Section 6, Bullet 4) despite the 31-token probe log-probabilities already being stored.
   - **Potential Concern (Ambiguity of "Local Update Statistics" and Reference Loss Change):** Bullet 3 does not enumerate the exact mathematical features comprising the 58 features of $B_{\text{strong}}$ (referenced in R2), nor does it specify whether "loss changes on a fixed reference battery R" denotes the net signed endpoint difference $L_R(t_1) - L_R(t_0)$ over $(t_0, t_1]$ or the accumulated 8-step path length of absolute per-item loss changes (`C10c`). If "local update statistics" includes direct step-norm summaries of $\Delta\theta_t$, reconstructing $D_1$ or $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ from $B_{\text{strong}}$ becomes circular by definition; conversely, bundling 58 collinear features causes severe out-of-domain overfitting in OLS (giving held-out $R^2$ from $-1.5$ to $-52$ on left-out family F3 in R2).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Explicitly enumerate all 58 features of $B_{\text{strong}}$ in Section 1 (verifying that no direct parameter step norms are included in Tier 1) and stratify the baseline hierarchy into the three computational cost tiers above so that parameter clocks ($D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2, \tau$) are evaluated against zero-pass optimizer telemetry (centered on $\Delta N + \sum \eta \|\hat{m}\|$) and functional divergence $D_3$ is evaluated against mandatory same-cost probe-loss telemetry (`C10c`) on both arms.

3. **Methodological Specification of Gate G2 (Identifiability) and Confirmatory Hypothesis H1 in Protocol v0.4 (Page 1, Section 1, Bullet 3)**
   - **Potential Concern (Cross-Validation Split, Regressor Class, Rate Normalization, and Collinearity in Gate G2):** Bullet 3 defines Gate G2 as: "if the rate of $\Delta c_e$ is reconstructible from B_strong (held-out $R^2 \ge 0.90$), H1 is not identifiable as a test," while H1 tests the "held-out increase in within-run $R^2$ when $c_e$ is added" to $B_{\text{strong}}$ for predicting $T_1$. While evaluating the rate $\rho = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$ removes interval-duration confounding, three methodological specifications are missing from Section 1:
     1. *Out-of-distribution splitting unit and regressor class:* Bullet 3 does not state whether "held-out $R^2$" in G2 is evaluated across random seeds within seen schedule families (leave-one-seed-out, as in R1 and R5) or across unseen schedule families (leave-one-family-out, as in R2 and R3), nor does it fix the regression model class. High-dimensional models can fail or appear to pass due to extrapolation error (e.g., 58-feature OLS yielding $R^2 \in [-1.5, -52]$ in R2, or GBDT dropping to $0.79$ on $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ in F6 while the single mechanistic scalar $\Delta N + \sum \eta \|\hat{m}\|$ achieves $0.82\text{--}0.98$).
     2. *Normalization of cumulative predictors and pause ticks:* Bullet 3 does not specify whether extensive cumulative features in $B_{\text{strong}}$ ($\sum \eta, \sum \text{loss}, \sum \eta \|\hat{m}\|$) are divided by $\Delta N$ into rates when predicting $\rho = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$, or whether $\Delta N$ in the denominator counts active gradient steps or external clock ticks during idle pauses (families F4 and F5).
     3. *Collinearity ($\text{VIF} \ge 10\text{--}100$) vs. regime-conditional identifiability:* When a parsimonious telemetry scalar reconstructs the rate of $D_1, D_{1,\text{rel}}, D_2$, and $\tau$ at $R^2 \in [0.90, 0.99]$, the variance inflation factor in a joint H1 regression is $\text{VIF} = 1/(1 - R^2) \in [10, 100]$. Proceeding to H1 for parameter clocks would only test whether the $1\text{--}10\%$ reconstruction residual (which R2 shows is dominated by short-window and post-restart boundary approximation error) correlates with $T_1$. Conversely, framing G2 as a global binary gate per candidate overlooks regime-specific identifiability: in R3, $D_3$ is reconstructed by `C10c` at $R^2 = 0.95\text{--}0.99$ in families F2, F3, and F5, but passes Gate G2 in the noisy-distractor family F6 ($R^2 = 0.59$ from `C10c`; $0.81$ from GBDT).
   - **Potential Concern (Target $T_1$ Scale Mismatch, Probe Split Definition, and Missing Endpoint Displacement Control):** Bullet 3 defines target $T_1$ exclusively as the "increase in loss on half A of the task's probe items" over $(t_0, t_1]$.
     1. *Probe partition and metric scale mismatch:* Section 1 never defines how a task's 256 items are partitioned into "half A" and "half B" (e.g., whether both halves are trained identically and split into disjoint evaluation subsets to reserve half B for model selection or Stage B rehearsal scheduling). Furthermore, cross-entropy loss (used in both $T_1$ and `C10c`) is unbounded on $[0, \infty)$ and is sensitive to logit-norm inflation and output entropy shifts even after top-1 accuracy has saturated (as seen in R4 beyond $\Delta N \approx 1,280$ steps, where accuracy loss is nearly complete while cross-entropy loss keeps climbing to $6.8\text{--}9.2$ nats, and in F6 where $30\%$ label noise flattens output confidence). By contrast, $D_3$ accumulates the bounded metric $\sqrt{\text{JS}} \in [0, \sqrt{\ln 2}]$. Testing bounded $D_3$ against unbounded `C10c` solely against unbounded loss increase $T_1$ risks confounding genuine functional forgetting with log-loss calibration scaling.
     2. *Path length vs. net endpoint (chord) displacement:* Any interval accumulator $\Delta c_e(t_0, t_1) = \sum_{t=t_0}^{t_1-1} D(S(t), S(t+1))$ is blind to which specific task was trained at $t_0$ and sums transient 8-step back-and-forth jitter that partially cancels over oscillatory intervals (such as F6 noisy distractors or F3 restarts). Protocol v0.4 does not include the net endpoint (chord) divergence $D_{\text{JS}}(P(t_0), P(t_1))$ on battery $R$ as a control competitor to test whether path integration carries signal beyond net functional displacement between $t_0$ and $t_1$.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Specify in Section 1: (i) how a task's probe items are split into half A and half B; (ii) both leave-one-seed-out and leave-one-family-out Gate G2 criteria using pre-specified rate-normalized mechanistic predictors ($\sum \eta \|\hat{m}\| / \Delta N$ for parameter clocks; `C10c`$/ \Delta N$ for $D_3$); (iii) that H1 is restricted to candidate-regime pairs that pass Gate G2 (specifically $D_3$ vs. `C10c` in F6); (iv) a bounded functional retention co-target on half A (accuracy drop or probe $\sqrt{\text{JS}}$ divergence) alongside $T_1$; and (v) the net endpoint divergence $D_{\text{JS}}(P(t_0), P(t_1))$ on $R$ in the competitor ladder for $D_3$.

4. **Conflation of Steady-State Rotational Equilibrium with Post-Reset Transient Dynamics and Omission of Decoupled Weight Decay in "Known Theory" (Page 2, Section 1, Top Paragraph)**
   - **Potential Concern (Equilibrium Update-RMS vs. Phase-Reset Transients):** At the top of Page 2, Section 1 cites Kosson, Messmer & Jaggi (arXiv:2305.17212) and Liu et al. (arXiv:2502.16982: *"AdamW's update RMS is usually around 0.2 to 0.4"*) to motivate the mechanism $\|\Delta\theta\| \approx \eta \|\hat{m}\| / \sqrt{\bar{v}}$. This passage conflates two distinct optimization regimes that make opposite empirical predictions:
     1. *Steady-state rotational equilibrium (Kosson et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025):* In stationary training with nonzero weight decay, coordinate-wise normalization by $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ stabilizes the RMS of the preconditioned update $\hat{m}_t / \sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ near a constant $c \in [0.2, 0.4]$ governed by steady-state gradient signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). If this equilibrium held throughout training, the step norm would be proportional to the learning rate alone ($\|\Delta\theta_t\|_2 \approx c\sqrt{d}\,\eta_t$), implying that cumulative $\sum \eta_t$ would explain $\tau$ (which motivated the pre-fixed prediction in R5 that subsequently failed at $R^2 = 0.67 < 0.80$).
     2. *Transient non-equilibrium dynamics after moment resets:* As documented on Page 1 (Bullet 5), FOREVER sets **weight decay to 0** and instantiates a **fresh `transformers.Trainer` with zeroed Adam moments ($m_0 = 0, v_0 = 0$) at every calibration, task, and replay phase**. Immediately after a moment reset, the bias-corrected ratio $|\hat{m}_{t,i}|/\sqrt{\hat{v}_{t,i}}$ starts near $1.0$ per coordinate—producing steps $2.5\times\text{--}5\times$ larger per unit $\eta$ than in steady-state equilibrium and explaining the $48\text{--}85\%$ early-window underestimation by $\sum \eta$ reported in R5—before declining rapidly as synthetic facts reach $1.000$ accuracy and gradient SNR decays. Moreover, $\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$ alone (without dividing by $\sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_t}$) achieves the exact same $R^2 = 0.94$ as the mean-field ratio $\sum \eta \|\hat{m}\| \sqrt{d}/\sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}}$ in R5 because of the timescale separation between $\beta_1 = 0.9$ (10-step memory) and $\beta_2 = 0.999$ (1,000-step memory): within short 24–160 step phases, $\sqrt{\text{Tr}\,\hat{v}_t}$ acts as a slowly varying normalizer while $\|\hat{m}_t\|_2$ directly tracks transient gradient alignment.
   - **Potential Concern (Omission of Decoupled Weight Decay):** The approximation $\|\Delta\theta\| \approx \eta \|\hat{m}\| / \sqrt{\bar{v}}$ omits the decoupled weight-decay vector $-\eta_t \lambda \theta_{t-1}$ in AdamW. When $\lambda > 0$ ($\lambda = 0.01$ in the synthetic world), parameters receiving zero gradient (such as unvisited token embeddings or during low-gradient steps) still undergo deterministic weight-decay updates of norm $\eta_t \lambda \|\theta_{t-1}\|_2$, which directly contaminates $D_1$ and $D_{1,\text{rel}}$ unless embeddings are excluded from weight decay.
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Distinguish explicitly in Section 1 ("Known theory") between stationary weight-decayed rotational equilibrium (where $\|\Delta\theta_t\|_2 \propto \eta_t$) and phase-reset or rapid-memorization regimes without weight decay (where moment resets and decaying gradient SNR make $\eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$ the governing predictor), and include the decoupled weight-decay term $-\eta_t \lambda \theta_{t-1}$ in the step-norm equation.

5. **Technical Gaps in the Audit of FOREVER (`commit bbeb549`) (Page 1, Section 1, Bullet 5)**
   - **Potential Concern (Insensitivity of Factor-Space $\tau$ to LoRA Scaling $\alpha/r$):** Bullet 5 notes that $\tau = \sum_t \|\theta_t - \theta_{t-1}\|_2$ is computed over the LoRA factors $\theta = (B, A)$ with weight decay $0$, and notes the discrepancy between $\alpha = 16$ in the code and $\alpha = 32$ in the paper, without stating the mechanistic consequence. Because the effective weight update is bilinear, $\Delta W_t = \frac{\alpha}{r}(B_t A_t - B_{t-1} A_{t-1}) \approx \frac{\alpha}{r}((\Delta B_t)A_{t-1} + B_{t-1}(\Delta A_t))$, doubling $\alpha$ from 16 to 32 doubles the effective functional update $\Delta W_t$ and rescales the backpropagated factor gradients by a constant factor. Under AdamW with weight decay $0$, constant gradient rescaling cancels between $\hat{m}_t$ and $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$, leaving the factor-space step norm $\|\Delta\theta_t\|_2$ (and thus $\tau$ and its reconstruction by $\sum \eta \|\hat{m}\|$) invariant to $\alpha$ while ignoring both $\alpha/r$ and the growing factor norms $\|A_{t-1}\|_F, \|B_{t-1}\|_F$ (as confirmed in R9).
   - **Potential Concern (Distortion of the 24-Step "Model Day" Calibration by Fresh `Trainer` and `GradScaler` Resets):** Because FOREVER defines its fundamental unit (a "model day") as $\tau$ accumulated over the first 24 steps of a task, instantiating a fresh `transformers.Trainer` at every phase means the 24-step calibration window starts with a 10-step linear warmup, zeroed Adam moments ($m_0 = 0, v_0 = 0$), and a fresh `fp16` `GradScaler` initialized at its high default scale ($2^{16}$). Deterministic initial `fp16` gradient overflows cause skipped optimizer steps ($\Delta\theta_t = 0$ on $4.1\%\text{--}5.9\%$ of steps in R8/R9), directly distorting $\tau_{\text{day}}$ and all downstream trigger thresholds $d \times \text{day}$.
   - **Potential Concern (Post-Replay Reference Anchoring, Intensity-Aware Replay Regularization, and Interventional Replay Testing):** First, the compressed phrase "the reference point moves after replay" should explicitly state that the parameter anchor $\theta_{t-1}$ used to compute the single-step increment $\|\theta_t - \theta_{t-1}\|_2$ when current-task training resumes is updated to the post-replay weights so multi-step replay displacement is not counted as a single jump in $\tau$. Second, in Feng et al. (arXiv:2601.03938), parameter update norms $\|\theta_t - \theta_{t-1}\|_2$ drive not only replay timing via $\tau$ ("when to replay") but also an Intensity-Aware Replay (IAR) coefficient that weights replay regularization loss ("how to replay"). Bullet 5 does not clarify whether IAR weighting is active in `commit bbeb549` (which bears directly on diagnosing the end-of-task replay accuracy drop reported in R8), nor does it note that offline trajectory reconstruction of $\tau$ does not substitute for an interventional replay-scheduling comparison (driving FOREVER's scheduler with $\tau$ vs. $\sum \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$ vs. cumulative applied $\eta_t$).
   - **Suggested Improvement:** Expand Bullet 5 to note the invariance of factor-space $\tau$ to $\alpha$, the effect of per-phase moment and `GradScaler` resets on the 24-step calibration window, the exact meaning of moving the post-replay reference point, whether IAR loss weighting is active in `commit bbeb549`, and the distinction between passive reconstruction of $\tau$ and interventional replay scheduling.

     2. *Long-window low-pass smoothing masking short-horizon transients and post-restart EMA lag:* **R2** reports that despite a high pooled $R^2$ ($0.95\text{--}0.96$ for `D1`), the one-scalar model exhibits a **23% relative error on short intervals (64–127 steps) versus 8% on long ones**, alongside a systematic post-restart bias of **$-5\%$ (`D1`) and $-21\%$ (`D3`)** when a learning-rate restart occurs inside the interval. 1. **Observational Limit of the FOREVER Clock Audit, Incomplete Counterfactual Trigger Analysis in R7, and Absence of a Closed-Loop Interventional Replay Baseline (R5–R9)**
   - **Potential Concern:** All evaluations in Section 4 (R5–R9) are strictly observational and open-loop with respect to replay scheduling: optimization trajectories are generated exclusively under $\tau$-triggered replay, and the analyses measure either offline held-out $R^2$ for reconstructing the 8-step rate of $\tau = \sum_t \|\theta_t - \theta_{t-1}\|_2$ from logged optimizer telemetry (R5, R6, R9) or post-hoc counterfactual trigger-step offsets along the fixed $\tau$-driven trajectory (R7). What can fairly be claimed from R5–R9 at present is therefore restricted to offline reconstructibility of the $\tau$ rate on synthetic tasks: specifically, that the pre-fixed prediction that the learning-rate schedule $\Sigma\eta$ alone explains $\ge 0.80$ of the $\tau$ rate fails ($R^2 = 0.669$ in `lmtech1`, $0.58$ in `lmtech2`, and $0.648\text{--}0.686$ in R9) because re-instantiating `transformers.Trainer` with a fresh `AdamW` optimizer at every phase boundary resets optimizer moments ($m_0 = 0, v_0 = 0$), causing $48\text{--}85\%$ underestimation in early post-reset windows and $\sim 22\%$ overestimation later in current phases; whereas scaling $\eta$ by the bias-corrected first-moment norm ($\Sigma\eta\|\hat{m}\|$) reconstructs the $\tau$ rate at $R^2 = 0.941\text{--}0.958$ ($0.956\text{--}0.970$ with $\Sigma\eta + \Sigma\eta\|\hat{m}\|$, and $0.976\text{--}0.988$ with `GBDT, all telemetry`, failing the protocol's Gate G2 identifiability threshold of $R^2 \ge 0.90$).      1. *Omission of $\Sigma\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ from the R7 phase-rate and trigger-timing analyses:* R7 demonstrates that the high phase-level correlation between $\tau$ and $\Sigma\eta$ ($R^2 = 0.975\text{--}0.991$) is a duration artefact matched by phase step counts $\Delta N$ alone ($0.975\text{--}0.987$), that removing duration (rate per phase, $\Delta\tau / \Delta N$) drops $\Sigma\eta$ to $R^2 = 0.09\text{--}0.18$, and that a schedule-only clock (cumulative applied $\eta$, normalized by its 24-step calibration value) triggers $d = 2$ at a median offset of $-1$ step and $d = 4$ about $9$ steps earlier (median, out of $\sim 85$ steps, or $\sim 11\%$ of the phase duration).      3. *Absence of downstream forgetting evaluation and unstated Intensity-Aware Replay (IAR) status:* R5–R9 do not evaluate downstream task forgetting or retention across alternative scheduling clocks. Furthermore, in FOREVER (`arXiv:2601.03938`), step-wise parameter update norms $\Delta_t = \|\theta_t - \theta_{t-1}\|_2$ are used not only to accumulate $\tau$ for replay timing ("when to replay"), but also in an exponential moving average of update intensity to scale replay regularization loss ("how to replay"). Section 4 does not specify whether IAR loss weighting is active during replay in R5–R9 or held constant.

     1. Using the existing logs from `lmtech1`, `lmtech2`, and R9 (requiring 0 additional GPU hours), expand R7 to report both the duration-removed phase-level rate $R^2$ and the counterfactual replay trigger-step offsets (median and interquartile range at $d \in \{1, 2, 4\}$) for the 24-step calibration-normalized $\Sigma\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ clock alongside the schedule-only clock $\Sigma\eta$.

5. **Structural Decoupling Between Factor-Space Updates ($\tau$) and Effective Weight Updates ($\Delta W$) Across LoRA Scaling $\alpha$ (R5, R6, R9)**
   - **Potential Concern:** Across R5 and R9, the single-scalar predictor $\Sigma\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ explains $0.941\text{--}0.958$ of the variance in the factor-space clock $\tau$, but drops by 4–6 percentage points ($0.887\text{--}0.922$) on the effective weight update $\Delta W$—falling slightly below the protocol's $R^2 \ge 0.90$ Gate G2 identifiability threshold in `lmtech1` ($0.887$ in R9; $0.89$ in R5)—whereas `GBDT, all telemetry` reaches $0.992\text{--}0.995$ on $\Delta W$ (higher than its $0.986\text{--}0.988$ on $\tau$). Its Frobenius norm scales with the evolving norms of the opposite factor matrices $\|A_{t-1}\|_F$ and $\|B_{t-1}\|_F$ (which start at $B_0 = 0$ on Task 0 and grow across tasks). Unlike the synthetic-world `B_strong` telemetry, the LM per-step telemetry list in Section 2 omits the factor parameter norms $\|A_t\|_F, \|B_t\|_F$, and unweighted $\Sigma\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ summed over the concatenated factors lacks these evolving factor norms. ##### 1. Algebraic Redundancy of Parameter-Distance Clocks ($D_1, D_{1\_\text{rel}}, D_2, \tau$), Audit of Plausible Reconstruction Artefacts, and Unidentifiability of Confirmatory Hypothesis H1 (Section 6, Bullet 1 & Section 7, Decision 1 — Addressing Q1 & Q2)

* **Potential Concern (Why Parameter-Clock Reconstruction Is Mechanistically Expected Rather Than an Empirical Contingency):**
  Section 6 (Bullet 1) notes that whether any clock predicts task forgetting better than telemetry (H1) has never been analysed, and Section 7 (Decision 1, Options B and R) contemplates carrying the parameter-distance candidates ($D_1$, $D_{1\_\text{rel}}$, and $D_2$) forward into a multi-family Stage A study.
  * *Facts (verified against dossier):* Under Pilot 2 protocol v0.4 (Section 1), Gate G2 specifies that if the rate $\rho = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$ of a candidate clock is reconstructible from the cheap online telemetry union $B_{\text{strong}}$ at held-out $R^2 \ge 0.90$, confirmatory hypothesis H1 (testing whether adding $\Delta c_e$ to $B_{\text{strong}}$ increases held-out within-run $R^2$ for predicting half-A loss forgetting $T_1$) is not identifiable as a statistical test. Across every technical round, all parameter-distance clocks fail Gate G2:
    1. On the synthetic world in **R1** (`tech2`/`tech3`, $\Delta N \in [64, 1,280)$), OLS and GBDT on $B_{\text{strong}}$ predict the rate $\rho$ with held-out $R^2 = 0.98\text{–}0.99$ ($D_1$), $0.96\text{–}0.98$ ($D_{1\_\text{rel}}$), and $0.99$ ($D_2$). Even the single scalar predictor $\Delta N + \sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ achieves $0.94$ ($D_1$), $0.92$ ($D_{1\_\text{rel}}$), and $0.99$ ($D_2$).
    2. Under out-of-family extrapolation in **R2** (training without learning-rate restarts, testing on the two F3 runs), $\Delta N + \sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ yields $R^2 = 0.95\text{–}0.96$ ($D_1$), $0.83\text{–}0.95$ ($D_{1\_\text{rel}}$), and $0.98\text{–}0.99$ ($D_2$).
    3. On the corrected `tech4` code in **R3** (leave-one-family-out across F2 / F3 / F5 / F6), GBDT on all telemetry reconstructs $D_1$ at $R^2 = 0.96 / 0.99 / 0.98 / 0.95$, $D_{1\_\text{rel}}$ at $0.94 / 0.99 / 0.95 / 0.79$, and $D_2$ at $0.98 / 0.99 / 0.94 / 0.99$, while the single scalar $\Delta N + \sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ achieves $0.86 / 0.94 / 0.92 / 0.95$ on $D_1$ and $0.98 / 0.98 / 0.99 / 0.98$ on $D_2$.
    4. On the language-model (LM) arm across **R5** (`lmtech1`), **R6** (`lmtech2`), and **R9** (`bf16` and $\alpha = 32$), the single scalar $\sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ reconstructs the 8-step rate of FOREVER's LoRA-factor clock $\tau$ with held-out $R^2 = 0.941\text{–}0.958$ ($\sum\eta + \sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$: $0.956\text{–}0.970$; GBDT on all telemetry: $0.976\text{–}0.988$), and reconstructs the effective weight update $\Delta W = 2BA$ at $R^2 = 0.887\text{–}0.922$ ($\sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$) and $0.992\text{–}0.995$ (GBDT).
  * *Mechanistic Analysis (Opinion — High Confidence):* Reconstructing parameter-distance clocks from $B_{\text{strong}}$—and specifically from $\sum_t \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$—is not an empirical accident of the synthetic setup; it is an immediate algebraic consequence of the explicit `AdamW` update equation (Kingma & Ba, 2015, `arXiv:1412.6980` [unverified]; Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019, `arXiv:1711.05101` [unverified]):
    $$\Delta \theta_t = \theta_t - \theta_{t-1} = -\eta_t \left( \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} + \lambda \theta_{t-1} \right).$$
    1. *Exact algebraic cancellation in $D_2$:* Protocol v0.4 defines $D_2$ as the parameter update weighted by a lagged second moment (an empirical-Fisher proxy; Kunstner, Hennig & Balles, 2019, `arXiv:1905.12558` [unverified]). Because `AdamW` divides $\hat{m}_t$ coordinate-wise by $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon$, multiplying $\Delta \theta_t$ back by the square root of a lagged second moment ($\beta_2 = 0.999$) algebraically cancels the denominator $\sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$. Up to negligible weight decay ($\eta\lambda = 3 \times 10^{-6}$ on the synthetic world once embeddings are excluded), each step of $D_2$ collapses to $\eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$, explaining why $\Delta N + \sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ achieves $R^2 = 0.98\text{–}0.99$ in R1–R3.
    2. *Rotational-equilibrium concentration in $D_1$, $D_{1\_\text{rel}}$, and $\tau$:* In FOREVER (Feng et al., ACL 2026, `arXiv:2601.03938`; commit `bbeb549`), weight decay on the LoRA factors is $\lambda = 0$, so $\|\Delta \theta_t\|_2 = \eta_t \|\hat{m}_t / (\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon)\|_2$ holds identically. By rotational-equilibrium theory (Kosson, Messmer & Jaggi, `arXiv:2305.17212`; Liu et al., `arXiv:2502.16982`, verified in dossier §1), the coordinate-wise root-mean-square (RMS) of $\hat{m}_t / \sqrt{\hat{v}_t}$ is governed by the local gradient signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and stabilizes within $0.2\text{–}0.4$. Because $\sqrt{\text{Tr }\hat{v}_t}$ evolves slowly within task phases ($\beta_2 = 0.999$), $\|\Delta \theta_t\|_2$ is collinear with $\eta_t \|\hat{m}_t\|_2$.

* **Audit of Plausible Reconstruction Artefacts and Checks to Rule Them Out (Q1):**

  1. *Artefact 1 — Near-duplicate features and 58-feature overfitting inside $B_{\text{strong}}$:* Section 1 defines $B_{\text{strong}}$ as a 58-feature union containing "local update statistics" and "parameter and second-moment norms". Moreover, computing $\|\hat{m}_t\|_2$ requires an $O(P)$ reduction over the parameter buffer, just like $\|\Delta \theta_t\|_2$.

  2. *Artefact 2 — Cross-seed schedule leakage on the LM arm:* While R2 and R3 use leave-one-family-out splits on the synthetic world, R5, R6, and R9 use leave-one-seed-out cross-validation across three seeds that share the exact same task-epoch schedule (60 epochs on task 0, 10 epochs on tasks 1–9), allowing GBDT ($R^2 = 0.976\text{–}0.988$) to memorize phase-reset step indices.

  3. *Artefact 3 — Choice of rate ($\rho = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$) vs. cumulative value ($\Delta c_e$):* Predicting the rate $\rho$ rather than cumulative $\Delta c_e$ is not an inflation artefact; on the contrary, predicting cumulative $\Delta c_e$ over variable intervals creates a massive duration confound. However, including unscaled $\Delta N$ on the right-hand side when predicting $\rho = \Delta c_e / \Delta N$ in R1–R3 fits an implicit duration-boundary slope.
     * *Check (<0.1 CPU hours, offline on R3 logs):* Report both the rate $R^2$ (predicting $\Delta c_e / \Delta N$ from $1 + (\sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|)/\Delta N$) and the partial $R^2$ of cumulative $\Delta c_e$ on $\sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ after partialling out $\Delta N$.
  4. *Artefact 4 — Window-length smoothing and post-restart / post-reset transients:* R2 documents that the one-scalar model $\Delta N + \sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ has a $23\%$ relative error on short intervals ($\Delta N \in [64, 127]$) versus $8\%$ on long intervals, plus a $-5\%$ ($D_1$) and $-21\%$ ($D_3$) bias after an internal learning-rate restart; similarly, R5 shows $48\text{–}85\%$ underestimation by $\sum\eta$ in the first windows after `AdamW` moment resets (cf. Liu et al., 2020, `arXiv:1908.03265` [unverified]). Pooling intervals across $\Delta N \in [64, 1,280)$ smooths over short-window second-moment lag.

  Section 6 (Bullet 4) explicitly acknowledges that on the LM arm, no same-cost competitor of the functional clock $D_3$—specifically the analogue of `C10c` (mean absolute change of the per-item loss on the same 128 never-trained reference prompts)—has been computed, even though the log-probabilities of the 31 candidate answer words on all 128 prompts are already stored on disk every 8 counted steps. * **Analytical Impact and Zero-Cost Resolution:**

##### 3. Critical Evaluation of the Open Case: Functional Clock $D_3$ vs. C10c in Family F6 and the Decisive Preregisterable Test (Section 6, Bullet 3 & Section 7, Decisions 1 & 2 — Addressing Q3)

* **Potential Concern (Distinguishing Bounded Simplex Geometry from Synthetic Design Artefacts in F6):**
  In **R3** (`tech4`), Family F6 (regular target exposures interleaved with variable-length distractor segments carrying $30\%$ label noise) is the *only* regime where $D_3$ passes Gate G2: leave-one-family-out $R^2$ is $0.18$ for $\Delta N + \sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$, $0.59$ for $\Delta N + \text{C10c}$, and $0.81$ for 58-feature GBDT ($< 0.90$). Whether this represents a genuine scientific phenomenon or an experimental artefact depends on separating one real mathematical distinction from three plausible design artefacts:
  1. *Genuine mathematical distinction (bounded full-simplex metric vs. unbounded single-token log-loss):* $D_3$ accumulates the mean square root of the Jensen–Shannon divergence ($\sqrt{\text{JS}}$; Endres & Schindelin, 2003, `DOI:10.1109/TIT.2003.813506` [unverified]), a proper metric on the 64-token predictive simplex bounded within $[0, \sqrt{\ln 2} \approx 0.8326]$ per 8-step chunk. Conversely, `C10c` measures the absolute change in unbounded negative log-likelihood $|\Delta \ell_i| = |-\ln p(y_i^*|x_i)|$ on the single ground-truth target token $y_i^*$. During F6's $30\%$ label-noise distractor segments, conflicting gradients reduce overall logit confidence (lowering inverse temperature across all 64 tokens). As documented in **R4**, this logit dampening causes F6 to lose top-1 accuracy faster at short intervals ($0.29$ in $\Delta N \in [64, 255]$ vs. $0.11$ in F1) while rising more slowly on the unbounded cross-entropy loss scale at long intervals, churning probability mass across non-target tokens on the simplex.
  2. *Plausible Artefact A — 8-step chunk-accumulated path length ($D_3$) vs. two-point interval-endpoint difference (`C10c`):* Section 1 defines $D_3$ as accumulated over 8-step chunks ($\sum_k \text{mean}_{i\in R}\sqrt{\text{JS}(P_i(8k), P_i(8k+8))}$). If `C10c` in R3 is evaluated as net interval loss change $\text{mean}_{i\in R}|\ell_i(t_1) - \ell_i(t_0)|$, oscillatory distractor updates during $30\%$ label noise accumulate positively in 8-step chunked $D_3$ while cancelling out between endpoints in net `C10c`.
  3. *Plausible Artefact B — Bounded vs. unbounded codomain mismatch:* Comparing bounded $\sqrt{\text{JS}} \in [0, \sqrt{\ln 2}]$ against unbounded $|\Delta \ln p| \in [0, \infty)$ without including bounded target-probability change $\text{C10c}_{\text{prob}} = \text{mean}_{i\in R}|\Delta p(y_i^*|x_i)| \in [0, 1]$ and probe entropy change $|\Delta H(R)|$ conflates full-distribution tracking with simple probability saturation.
  4. *Plausible Artefact C — Single-seed leave-F6-out domain shift:* R3 uses only 1 seed per family, and F6 is the *only* family among F1–F6 with label noise or distractors. Training OLS on zero-noise families F1–F5 and testing on F6 yields *negative* held-out $R^2$ for $\Delta N + \text{C10c}$ on $D_1$ ($-0.40$), $D_{1\_\text{rel}}$ ($-0.26$), and $D_2$ ($-0.95$), proving that the linear slope relating `C10c` to change shifts under $30\%$ label noise.

* **Decisive, Preregisterable Test for $D_3$ vs. C10c in F6 (Q3):**
  * **Stage 0a Blind Identifiability Check (0 new runs, 0.1 CPU hours on existing `tech4` logs, strictly blind to $T_1$; Decision 2 = `yes`):** From the exact same 8-step reference forward passes on $R$, compute **8-step chunk-accumulated loss change** $\text{C10c}_{\text{chunk}} = \sum_k \text{mean}_{i\in R}|\ell_i(8k+8) - \ell_i(8k)|$, **8-step chunk-accumulated bounded target-probability change** $\text{C10c}_{\text{prob}} = \sum_k \text{mean}_{i\in R}|p_i(y_i^*; 8k+8) - p_i(y_i^*; 8k)|$, and **chunked probe entropy change** $\Delta H_{\text{chunk}}(R)$. If adding $\{\text{C10c}_{\text{chunk}}, \text{C10c}_{\text{prob}}, \Delta H_{\text{chunk}}(R)\}$ to $B_{\text{strong}}$ raises F6 reconstruction of $D_3$ to $R^2 \ge 0.90$, the R3 gap ($0.59 / 0.81$) was an aggregation or link-scale artefact and $D_3$ closes at Gate G2 at zero run cost.
  * **Stage A Multi-Seed Confirmatory Run (10 independent seeds $\times$ 4 families F1, F2, F3, F6 = 40 CPU runs; 3.25 laptop CPU hours at 12.3 runs/hour):**
    * *Fixed Pass/Fail Criterion:* Over $\Delta N \in [64, 1,280)$, $D_3$ passes if and only if (1) Gate G2 held-out $R^2 < 0.90$ for predicting the $D_3$ rate in F6 from $B_{\text{strong}}^+ = B_{\text{strong}} \cup \{\text{C10c}, \text{C10c}_{\text{chunk}}, \text{C10c}_{\text{prob}}, \Delta H_{\text{chunk}}(R)\}$ both leave-one-family-out and cross-seed *within* F6 (5-fold CV across the 10 F6 seeds), **and** (2) in Stage 0b (automatic after design lock), adding $\Delta D_3$ to $B_{\text{strong}}^+$ increases held-out within-run $R^2$ in F6 by mean **$\Delta R^2 \ge 0.02$** (with one-sided paired permutation $p < 0.01$ and positive $\Delta R^2$ in $\ge 8/10$ F6 seeds) on **both** pre-fixed cross-entropy loss forgetting ($T_1$) and functional accuracy drop ($T_{1,\text{acc}}$) on probe half A. Requiring both targets rules out a pure bounded-versus-unbounded metric-scale artefact.

##### 4. Fair Scope of Claims on FOREVER (`arXiv:2601.03938`), the Missing Interventional Scheduler Control, and Timing of the FOREVER Note (Section 6, Bullet 2 & Section 7, Decisions 5, 6, 8 — Addressing Q4)

* **What Can Be Claimed Fairly from R5–R9 About the FOREVER Clock (Facts):**
  1. *Falsification of the pre-fixed schedule-only prediction at the 8-step window level:* Because FOREVER (`github.com/WoodScene/FOREVER`, commit `bbeb549`) instantiates a fresh `transformers.Trainer` and fresh `AdamW` ($\lambda = 0$) at every calibration, current-task, and replay phase, resetting Adam's moments ($m_t, v_t$) causes early-phase steps to be $48\text{–}85\%$ larger per unit $\eta$ and late-phase steps $\sim 22\%$ smaller (R5). Consequently, $\sum\eta$ alone explains only $R^2 = 0.669$ (`lmtech1`), $0.58$ (`lmtech2`), $0.648$ (`bf16`), and $0.686$ ($\alpha = 32$) of the 8-step $\tau$ rate, failing the pre-fixed $R^2 \ge 0.80$ prediction across all configurations.
  2. *Near-complete reconstruction of $\tau$ by one AdamW telemetry scalar:* Despite moment resets, the single logged scalar $\sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$ reconstructs the 8-step rate of $\tau$ with held-out $R^2 = 0.941\text{–}0.958$ across all four configurations ($\sum\eta + \sum\eta\|\hat{m}\|$: $0.956\text{–}0.970$; GBDT on all telemetry: $0.976\text{–}0.988$).
  3. *Phase-level duration confounding and sparse Ebbinghaus triggers (R7):* Across phases, the high $R^2$ of $\tau$ from $\sum\eta$ ($0.975\text{–}0.991$) is a duration artefact matched by step counts $\Delta N$ alone ($0.975\text{–}0.987$); with duration removed (rate per phase), $\sum\eta$ explains only $0.09\text{–}0.18$. Moreover, out of FOREVER's 9 theoretical Ebbinghaus replay thresholds $d \in \{1, 2, 4, 7, 15, 30, 60, 90, 120\}$, only $d \in \{1, 2\}$ (in the 80 counted steps of `lmtech2`) and $d \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ (in the 160 counted steps of `lmtech1`) are ever reached, and a schedule-only clock triggers $d = 2$ at a median difference of $-1$ step and $d = 4$ about $9$ steps earlier (median, out of $\sim 85$).
  4. *Code-vs.-paper discrepancies, `fp16` `GradScaler` skips, and end-of-task replay interference (R8 & R9):* The repository uses LoRA $\alpha = 16$ and batch size $32$ (vs. $\alpha = 32$ and batch size $8$ in the paper), re-initializes an `fp16` `GradScaler` per phase causing $4.1\%\text{–}5.9\%$ skipped steps (`lmtech1`: $283/6,952 = 4.1\%$; $\alpha = 32$: $304/6,984 = 4.4\%$; reduced to $0\%$ under `bf16`, **Decision 6 = `bf16`**), and runs an end-of-task replay that drops current-task accuracy from $1.000$ ($\ge 0.998$ across all 6 runs in R9) immediately *before* replay to $0.85\text{–}0.94$ after replay in `lmtech1`/`lmtech2` (**Decision 5 = `before final replay`**, while reporting post-replay accuracy alongside it).

* **What Cannot Yet Be Claimed and the Decisive Interventional Experiment (Q4):**
  As Section 6 (Bullet 2) states, R5–R9 are passive observational reconstructions on trajectories already scheduled by $\tau$ on homogeneous synthetic facts. In closed-loop continual learning, shifting a replay trigger changes the buffer state, resets `AdamW` moments at a different step, and interacts with FOREVER's replay weighting. To make the FOREVER claim incontestable:
  * *Interventional Replay-Scheduler & SuperNI Control (4.5 laptop GPU hours):* On Qwen3-0.6B (`bf16`, $\alpha = 32$), actively drive FOREVER's replay scheduler (calibrated over the first 24 steps per task) using four competing clocks—(1) $\tau = \sum\|\Delta\theta_{\text{LoRA}}\|_2$, (2) the one-scalar telemetry clock $\sum\eta_t\|\hat{m}_t\|_2$, (3) the schedule-only clock $\sum\eta_t$, and (4) fixed step counts $\Delta N$—across 3 seeds on the 10-task `lmtech1` stream (12 runs = $3.0\text{ GPU hours}$), plus 3 seeds comparing $\tau$ vs. $\sum\eta_t\|\hat{m}_t\|_2$ on a 5-task heterogeneous subset of SuperNI (6 runs = $1.5\text{ GPU hours}$).
  * *Justification for Publishing a Short Note Now vs. Bundling (Q4 & Decision 8):* Because the LM-arm audit never touches or unblinds the synthetic-world forgetting target $T_1$, publishing a short standalone note on the FOREVER clock **now** (`Decision 8: now`, immediately incorporating the 0-hour offline LM `C10c` check and the 4.5-hour GPU interventional scheduler control) is fully justified and does not compromise the preregistration of the synthetic Stage A study.
